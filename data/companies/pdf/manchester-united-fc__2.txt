As the primary governing body of European football, UEFA continually evaluates the dynamics tn the football industry and
considers changes to the regulatory framework governing European football clubs. Clubs participating in EFA club
competitions are subyect to the UEFA Club Licensing and Financial Sustainability Regulations (“FSR”) Breaches tn the rules
may result in, among other things, fines, withholding of prize money, bans on registering new players for UEFA club
competitions and ultimately disqualification from UEFA club competitions Amongst other things, these rules are intended to
discourage clubs from continually opcrating at a loss and to ensure that clubs settle their football, staff and tax creditors on
time
Previously governed by Financial Fair Play (‘FFP ), clubs are now subject to the FSR, effective from | July 2022 The FSR
retains the "break-even" rule but increases the allowable loss limit to €60 million over three years (based on certam criteria
being achieved, including positive equity and/or owner contribution), or up to €90 million if certain good financial health
criteria are met
A key addition ts the squad cost rule, which limits spending on player and coach wages, transfers, and agent fees to 70% of
club revenues 1n a Calendar year
Revenue includes operating revenue and an average of the previous 36 months of player trading results This followed a
gtadual phase-in 90% in calendar year 2023, 80% in calendar year 2024, and 70% from calendar year 2025 onwards
The FSR has an additional positive net equity test as of the 31 December each year preceding each deadline For those clubs
with negative equity, an improvement of 10% must be shown year-on-year
Finally, the FSR includes increased overdue payables reporting under which clubs must have no overdue payables in respect of
other football clubs, social & tax authorities and employees Overdue payables reporting was previously required three umes a
year but can now be tested on up to four occasions
