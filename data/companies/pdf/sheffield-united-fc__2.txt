STATEMENT FROM THE CHIEF EXECUTIVE OFFICER
FOR THE YEAR ENDED 30 JUNE 2024

As we reflect on the 2023/24 season, Sheffield United has undoubtedly faced its share of challenges, but we also
have much to be proud of as a Club. Securing our return to the Premier League at the conclusion of last season
was a momentous achievement, and while we encountered hurdles throughout the season, we continue to view
this period as a critical step in our ongoing journey toward long-term success.
Our focus during the summer was to strengthen the squad, adding key players such as Vinicius Souza and
Gustavo Hamer, and our commitment to building a competitive team was clear. Although we faced difficulties in
terms of results, including key departures, our squad showed tremendous fight and resilience. The return of
Chris Wilder sparked a positive momentum that we will carry forward into the future. While relegation was
ultimately confirmed, our team’s commitment, determination, and growth throughout the season have laid a
strong foundation to secure a promotion to the top tier next season.
The Sheffield United Academy continues to be a key pillar of our long-term strategy. This season, we celebrated
the under 21's remarkable achievement in winning both the England Professional Development League; North
and National titles. We take immense pride in the development of our young talent, with players like William
Osula and Oliver Arblaster stepping up to the first team and making their mark. Our priority is to ensure our
academy remains a leader in developing top-tier talent while strategically managing our assets to maximise long-
term value. By making calculated decisions—whether through player development, transfers, or loans—we
position the club for sustained success, strengthening both our competitive edge and financial stability. This
balanced approach ensures we continue building a legacy of excellence on and off the field. Our investments in
facilities, combined with our commitment to achieving Category 1 Academy status, are vital steps in continuing to
develop world-class talent for the future.
As a Club, we remain highly focused on ensuring we are not only performing on the pitch but also achieving
long-term growth off it. We've seen strong performances across our revenue streams, from ticketing and
commercial partnerships to merchandising. Support continued to be strong throughout the season
demonstrating the continued loyalty of our fans with an average stadium attendance of 29,971. We have
expanded our brand presence globally through our commercial partnerships, including a landmark deal with CFI
in the MENA region. In merchandising, we are seeing impressive growth, particularly from international markets,
and we are investing heavily in improving our online platform to maximise our global reach.
At Sheffield United, we understand that modern, high-quality facilities are essential to supporting both player
development and fan experience. We have made significant investments this season, from upgrading training
facilities to refurbishing key areas of Bramall Lane. The purchase of additional land for fan development is an
important step in creating the best possible matchday experience. The ongoing refurbishment of our hotel and
plans for a state-of-the-art new training facility are part of our vision to create long-term, sustainable revenue
streams while also enhancing the overall experience for players and supporters alike.
While relegation to the Championship was a setback, we remain focused on the future. Our commitment to long-
term growth, through player development, facility improvements, and strategic partnerships, is stronger than
ever. We are optimistic about our future prospects, both on and off the pitch. We will continue to leverage our
Academy to develop world-class talent, enhance our commercial success through international partnerships, and
deliver a premium fan experience at every level. Sheffield United is on an exciting path, and | have no doubt that
the best is yet to come.

FOR THE YEAR ENDED 30 JUNE 2024
The directors are pleased to present the annual report for The Sheffield United Football Club Limited for the
"financial year ending 30 June 2024, which includes the financial statements and the Auditor's Report.

Principal activities ,
The principal activity of The Sheffield United Football Club Limited is the operation of a professional football club.
Business review
On-field performance
Men's First Team
Sheffield United experienced the excitement of securing promotion to the Premier League in the summer of
2023, but this achievement was swiftly met with the recognition of the significant challenges that lie ahead for
newly promoted teams competing in the top tier of English football.
During the summer transfer window, the Club strengthened its squad with seven permanent signings: Auston
Trusty from Arsenal, Gustavo Hamer from Coventry City, Vinicius Souza from Lommel, Tom Davies, Anis Ben
Slimane from Brondby, Bénie Traoré from BK Hacken, and Cameron Archer from Aston Villa. In addition, loan
signings included Yasser Larouci from ES Troyes AC, Luke Thomas from Leicester City, and James McAtee,
who returned to the Club on loan from Manchester City. Key departures included Iliman Ndiaye, who transferred
to Marseille, and Sander Berge, who joined Burnley, both transfers occurring due to the nearing expiry of their
contracts, and the players’ lack of desire to extend them further.
Despite strong performances in matches, including notable fixtures against Manchester City and Tottenham
Hotspur, the Club struggled to secure points, earning just one point from a home match against Everton before
securing the first win of the season against Wolverhampton Wanderers at Bramall Lane in November. A heavy
5-0 defeat away to Burnley in early December led to the departure of Manager Paul Heckingbottom and the
reappointment of Chris Wilder as the Club's Manager.
In the January transfer window, the Club made further efforts to reinforce the squad by securing the loan
signings of Ben Brereton Diaz from Villarreal and Mason Holgate from Everton, along with the permanent signing
of Ivo Grbic from Atletico Madrid. Although there was an improvement in performances during the second half of
the season, the Club remained in the relegation zone, and relegation to the EFL Championship was confirmed,
on the 27th April following a defeat to Newcastle.
While the first team faced significant challenges, the Academy experienced notable success. Several of the
Academy players made important developments, with William Osula, Rhys Norrington-Davies, Andre Brooks,
and Oliver Arblaster establishing themselves as first-team regulars, while Ryan Oné and Sydie Peck made their
senior debuts.
Player Development Pathway :
The Sheffield United Academy plays a pivotal role in the Club’s mission of "success with sustainability," aiming to
generate revenue through player sales or reduce costs by developing players capable of integrating into the first
team. The core objective of the Academy is to maximise player potential through a robust development program,
with a guiding philosophy to Out Run, Out Fight, Out Play the opposition.

Player Development Pathway (continued)
The Sheffield United Academy achieved significant success during the 2023/24 season, under the leadership of
Academy Manager Derek Geary and the Academy Management Team. The under 21’s team secured the
England Professional Development League; North title for the fourth consecutive year, also being crowned
National champions. The under 18's advanced to the 5th round of the FA Youth Cup, where they were narrowly
defeated by eventual finalists Leeds United, marking the second consecutive year the team was eliminated by a
finalist. Additionally, the under 21's reached the quarterfinals of the Premier League Cup, becoming the only
Category 2 team to progress to this stage.
The loan market has become an increasingly important tool for player development and commercial recovery.
Notable loan spells in the first half of the season included Oliver Arblaster at League One's Port Vale and Louie
Marsh at Doncaster, with Marsh's tenure cut short due to injury. Miguel Freckleton enjoyed a successful season
with Chesterfield, winning the National League title and contributing to their impressive FA Cup run, including a
standout performance against Championship side Watford.
A strong transfer, recruitment, and development strategy has been integral to the Academy's success. Key
signings during the summer transfer window included Ryan Oné from Hamilton and Jevan Beattie from
Motherwell. Several Academy graduates established themselves as regulars in the first team during the 2023/24

, season, including William Osula, Rhys Norrington-Davies, Andre Brooks, and Oliver Arblaster. In addition, Ryan
Oné and Sydie Peck made their senior debuts, with Jordan Amissah, Evan Easton, Dovydas Sasnauskas, Louie
Marsh, Sai Sachdev, Jili Buyabu, Owen Hampson, and Billy Blacker also featuring in matchday squads,
highlighting the importance of the Academy's development pathway to the first team’s success. Academy
graduate lliman Ndiaye was transferred to Marseille in the Summer 2023 window, providing a significant return
on investment for the Club.
Internationally, the Club saw several Academy players represent their respective countries: Oliver Arblaster in
the England under 20’s squad, Ryan Oné in the Scotland under 19’s squad, Levis Pitan in the Poland under 20’s
squad, and Dovydas Sasnauskas in the Lithuania under 19's squad. Sai Sachdev was also called up to the
England under 19’s squad.
Off the pitch, Sheffield United has made significant investments in its Academy facilities, reinforcing the Club’s
commitment to developing homegrown talent. At the end of the season, Michael Collins, under 21's Coach, was
promoted to the first-team coaching staff, and under 18’s Coach Matthew Thorpe moved on to a role with the
England national team. These changes led to the internal promotion of Gary McSheffrey to the under 21’s
position and Jim O’Brien to the under 18’s role, demonstrating the Club's commitment to continuous
improvement and talent development across its operations. ;
Despite relegation, the Club remains dedicated to supporting the Academy. The long-term goal is to achieve
Category 1 Academy status, and throughout the 2024/2025 season, the Academy will position itself to transition
smoothly to this next level while continuing to develop players capable of progressing to the first team or
generating revenue through player sales.
Off-field performance
Revenue Generation
Ticketing .
Ahead of securing a return to the Premier League, the Club undertook a benchmarking exercise on season
ticket pricing. It was determined that an increase was necessary for the forthcoming season to address rising
operational costs, particularly in relation to facility management. A £1 per match increase was implemented for
renewals, with 22,228 general admission supporters committing to a season-long membership, resulting in a
record level of season ticket income, supporting the Club’s ability to absorb rising costs such as utilities.

Ticketing (continued)
Matchday attendances remained strong, with an average of 29,971 spectators per match during the season
(2023: 28,763), ranking the Club 13th in the Premier League for match attendance. A record crowd of 31,543
was achieved in the season, marking the highest attendance at Bramall Lane since the 2019/20 season against
Brighton. Season ticket attendance increased by 3% year-on-year, and match-by-match attendance rose by
20%.
The Club remains dedicated to improving the fan experience, with a particular focus on enhancing access
control systems. In preparation for the 2024/25 season, work has been underway to implement near-field
communication (NFC) ticketing which will reduced costs, eliminate the need for physical tickets and season
cards, and ultimately provide an enhanced experience for fans. This initiative is expected to position Bramall
Lane as a premier event space, maximising its potential as a significant revenue stream.
Commercial
The Club’s commercial operation, encompassing sponsorship, advertising, partnerships, and hospitality, saw
both notable achievements and challenges during this period. Sheffield United made significant strides in
expanding commercial partnerships, enhancing matchday experiences, and broadening its global brand
presence. The sponsorship portfolio flourished, with the Club securing a seven-figure principal partnership with
CFI, further strengthening its brand in the MENA region, and a sleeve partnership with the UK-based technology
company G-Tech. Additionally, 75% of expired agreements were successfully renewed, particularly in sectors
such as technology, manufacturing, leisure, and retail. These partnerships have not only bolstered revenue
streams but have also enhanced the Club’s positioning as an innovative and attractive partner for the upcoming
season.
The competition for sponsorships in the Premier League proved to be challenging, particularly due to the Club's
stance on betting, gaming, and alcohol-related partnerships. Nonetheless, the Club's adaptability and
commitment to cultivating long-term relationships have kept it on track for sustained commercial growth. Looking
ahead, the Club remains confident in its strategy to strengthen Sheffield United’s commercial footprint both
domestically and internationally.
Merchandising
Sheffield United's official kit partnership with Errea continued into the 2023/24 season. During the year, replica
shirt sales increased by 6,000 units from the previous season, despite challenges in the market stemming from
the growing prevalence of counterfeit merchandise. To protect the Club's intellectual! property, the Club engaged
professionals who successfully removed 1,697 counterfeit listings. However, given the rise in counterfeit activity,
the Club may need to develop a more comprehensive strategy for enforcement and education going forward.
While merchandising revenues saw a slight decline following the closure of the Meadowhall store, a decision
made due to its cost inefficiency, performance indicators rémain strong. Conversion rates in the Bramall Lane
store improved compared to the previous year, and the average basket size remained constant. International
merchandising sales saw a 36% increase, with 32% of these sales originating from the USA, demonstrating the
potential for further growth in this market. This aligns with the Club's long-term goal to develop SUFC Direct as
the primary platform for merchandise sales, focusing on improving website usability and fulfilment processes.
We view this as the most significant opportunity for growth in this revenue stream.

Facilities and Property
Bramall Lane
The Club remains committed to enhancing the facilities at Bramall Lane to ensure the safety, comfort, and .
enjoyment of its loyal supporters. Significant investments were made during the 2023/24 season to refurbish all
Executive Boxes, the Boardroom, and the Directors’ Box. Additionally, at the close of the season, work
commenced on the refurbishment of the Platinum Suite, which will be rebranded for the 2024/25 season.
A long-term refurbishment plan for the general admission areas also began, with key infrastructure works taking
place, including upgrades to energy supply, fire protection systems, and kitchen refurbishments. These works
are expected to continue into the 2024/25 season. Furthermore, the Club purchased two pieces of land adjacent
to Bramall Lane, Cutlers Corner and Boundary Corner, in April 2024. The Club plans to develop the Boundary
Corner site into a dedicated FanZone before the start of the new season.
Shirecliffe
. Following the installation of a Desso pitch at the Shirecliffe site in 2023, ongoing work continued to improve the
quality of playing surfaces. The all-weather pitch was resurfaced, and new floodlights were installed. Additionally,
significant refurbishments took place in back-office and rehabilitation areas, including the installation of a
dedicated bath and ice room, as well as upgrades to gym and treatment rooms. Dining and nutrition areas were
also refurbished to support elite performance and player well-being.
The Club has expressed a desire to expand its Academy operations, as the current pitch space at Shirecliffe is a
limiting factor to the operation. While the Club continues to operate efficiently within the constraints of the site,
plans are underway to improve its functionality and capacity.
Dore Training Facility :
After three years of negotiations, the Club acquired the HSBC Sports and Social Club site in Dore, Sheffield, with
a deposit paid in April and the balance satisfied in August 2024. This acquisition offers the opportunity to develop
a state-of-the-art training facility that will meet the demands of a competitive Premier League team. Early-stage
planning is underway, and once operational, this facility will allow the Academy to take over the Shirecliffe site,
which will then be developed to meet the Category 1 Elite Player Performance Plan criteria, enabling the Club to
attract and retain top talent at the Academy level.
Business Centre
The Blades Business Hub, located at Bramall Lane, continues to grow and now houses over 20 small
businesses, including The Sheffield United Community Foundation Ltd, a related party to the group. The
Community Foundation delivers education and employability programmes in partnership with Barnsley College,
the University of South Wales, and DWP from its Youth Hub, within the Business Centre. The Hub's state-of-the-
art E-Sports facilities, including an Esports suite, F1 Simulator suite, podcast room, and student breakout room,
provides an unparalleled learning experience.
The Youth Hub, which helps over 200 young people a year into employment, is the only one of its kind located
7 within a professional football stadium in the UK and is the best performing Youth Hub in South Yorkshire for
2024.

Facilities and Property (continued)
Derek Dooley Community Sports Centre
The Club made the difficult decision to close the Derek Dooley Community Sports Centre in 2020 due to
changes in the gym market that rendered the operation unsustainable. However, over the 2023/24 season, the

. senior leadership team, in collaboration with consultants and planners, explored potential uses for the site.
These consultations have been positive, and the Club is hopeful to re-open the site in 2025, providing much-
needed sports facilities to the local community.
Engagement with Stakeholders
The values of integrity, hard work, ambition, and inclusivity are central to the culture at Sheffield United and play
a pivotal role in driving the Club’s long-term goal of achieving success and sustainability, both on and off the
pitch. The Board of Directors acknowledges the critical importance of engaging effectively with stakeholders to
meet these objectives. In line with their obligations under Section 172 of the Companies Act, which requires
directors to act fairly in the interests of all stakeholders and the wider community, the Club actively considers the
perspectives of stakeholders in both short-term and long-term planning. ;
To strengthen stakeholder engagement, the Club has undertaken a number of initiatives during the year. These
include the establishment of the Fan Advisory Board, the relaunch of the staff wellbeing committee, the
continuation of the equality, diversity, and inclusion group, and the expansion of employee support packages.
The creation of the Fan Advisory Board (FAB) has established a direct communication channel between the
fans, the Chief Executive Officer and the Senior Leadership Team. This initiative ensures that fans remain
integral to the Club's plans. Comprising eight representative supporters, the FAB meets regularly to offer advice
and provide feedback to the Chief Executive Officer, and the senior leadership team. The board's role is crucial
in shaping the fan experience while ensuring that the traditions and heritage of Sheffield United are preserved.
Sheffield United understands that its people are its greatest asset. The Club continuously reviews its people
policies to identify opportunities for improvement across all areas. It strives to create an environment where
every staff member feels valued and is recognised for their contributions. The Club believes strongly in staff
development and internal progression. Learning and development now play a key role in the annual appraisal
process, with numerous examples of the Club supporting professional growth. To assess employee satisfaction,
an anonymous employee engagement survey is conducted annually, which provides valuable insights that inform
planning for the year ahead.
The Club is also committed to fostering a safe and welcoming environment for all staff and visitors. A dedicated
team of professionals in areas such as Equality, Diversity and Inclusion (EDI), Safeguarding, and Health and
Safety, among others, works to ensure a positive experience for everyone involved with the Club. The
commitment to diversity and inclusion was recognised at the Football Supporters Association Awards, where
Sheffield United won the Fans for Diversity Award. Additionally, the Club earned the EFL Family Excellence Gold
Award, further highlighting its efforts in creating an inclusive environment.
The United Wellbeing initiative, led by the Club's Head of HR, promotes the emotional, social, psychological, and
physical health of all employees. The Wellbeing Committee, which includes representatives from across the
business, meets regularly to assess and improve the support provided to employees. This group is responsible
for developing a program of events that enhances the overall employee experience, both in their professional
and personal lives. Recent enhancements to employee benefits, such as the inclusion of life coverage for all
permanent staff members, further exemplify the Club's ongoing commitment to the well-being of its employees.
Additionally, we provide a range of initiatives to support our people, including enhanced healthcare services with
online GP access, mental health first aiders, and various well-being resources.

Future Developments
Sheffield United’s long-term goal is to establish itself as a sustainable, successful club, both on and off the pitch.
Achieving this will require a strategic business model that prioritises investment in the playing squad, the
development of elite academy players, global brand expansion, and the diversification of revenue streams.
Additionally, the Club will continue to identify and nurture talent across all areas of the business.
In recent years, the Club has made significant investments in the development of its stadium, transforming it into
a venue that is not only a suitable Premier League home ground but also a commercially attractive event space.
As part of its ongoing development, the Club will focus on enhancing Bramall Lane’s role as a key destination in
Sheffield. Over the course of the 2024/25 season, the Club plans to build on this progress by utilising the recently
acquired Boundary Corner site to create a dedicated FanZone area, further enhancing the matchday experience.
Additionally, the redevelopment of the hotel at Bramall Lane will be completed, making the stadium an even
more attractive venue for both football fans and other events. These developments will strengthen the Club’s
position to secure future concerts and competitive sports fixtures, adding valuable new revenue streams.
Beyond commercial planning, the Club recognises the need to continue expanding and improving its sporting
facilities. The much-anticipated acquisition of a new training site in Dore in the summer of 2024 marks the
beginning of an exciting new chapter for the Club. While significant work is required to make the site suitable for
hosting the First Team, this acquisition presents a unique opportunity. Over the next twelve months, the Club will
engage with planners and developers to design a state-of-the-art training facility that meets the standards
expected of an elite professional football team. Once completed, the First Team will relocate to Dore, which will
free up space at the current Shirecliffe site. This will allow the Club to expand its development programme,
aiming to secure a Category One Elite Player Performance Plan License and enhancing its player development
pathway.
These developments represent a clear, strategic vision for the Club's future, positioning Sheffield United for
continued success both on and off the pitch. :
At the start of the 2024/2025 season, the Club will begin its Championship campaign with a 2-point deduction,
following sanctions imposed by the EFL as a result of a disciplinary commission's decision on 28 March 2024.
Despite this challenge, the Club is confident that our first team recruitment and development strategy will result
in competitive performance in the 2024-25 season, and remains dedicated to a return to the Premier League.
Financial Performance Overview
The Club has reported a net profit of £2.4 million for the period, a significant recovery from a loss of £31.5 million
in 2023. This profit is stated after an impairment of tangible fixed assets of £10.7 million, following the revaluation
of property assets to reflect their revised market value and a provision of £14.8 million against intercompany
receivables due from the parent company.
Revenue :
The Club achieved revenue of £137.6 million, representing an increase of £73.7 million from £63.9 million in
2023. This growth was primarily driven by a substantial rise in broadcast revenues, which reached £114.3 million
(2023: £42.7 million) following promotion to the Premier League. Non-broadcast revenues also demonstrated
robust growth, increasing to £23.3 million (2023: £21.2 million), reflecting the ongoing expansion and
refurbishment of commercial inventory, alongside the Club’s commitment to enhancing Bramall Lane as a .
premier hospitality venue.
Despite a challenging commercial environment in the summer of 2023, where several clubs began the season
without shirt sponsors due to increased competition for marketing spend, the Club secured a successful principal
partnership for the 2023/24 season with Dubai-based financial services provider, CFI Financial Group. This

: partnership -provides a significant global marketing opportunity, leveraging the international platform afforded by
the Premier League.

Financial Performance Overview (continued)
Cost of Sales
Cost of sales increased to £77.5 million (2023: £57.8 million), primarily reflecting higher player wages and other
direct costs associated with the Club's promotion to the Premier League including increased investment in
technology to assist in player recruitment and performance management, in addition to general cost of living
increases such as increased utility costs and minimum wage increases.
Administrative Expenses
Administrative expenses (as split on the face of profit or loss) rose to £37.0 million (2023: £20.3 million), due toa
£2.4m increase in charitable donations made by the Premier League on the Clubs behalf, and the impairment of
certain intercompany loan balances of £14.8 million which have been provided for given the uncertainty of
recovery of a balance due from Blades Leisure Limited (see impairment of investments below).
Interest Expenses and Income
Interest payable amounted to £9.6 million (2023; £3.8 million), driven by the discounting of future cashflows

Impairment of Investments and Tangible Fixed Assets
Following a review of intercompany balances, a provision was made for the uncertain recovery of a balance due
from Blades Leisure Limited.
A revaluation of the Club’s property assets has been undertaken to ensure that they are stated at fair value.
Changes to the to the base rate since the prior valuation in 2021 have significantly impacted the discount rate
applied to the future cash flows from these assets, which is used to establish market value. This interest rate
change, alongside the general challenges faced by valuers in establishing market value through the comparable
sale of similar assets, in particular stadia, have significantly impacted the value calculation, resulting in an
impairment to the held asset value of £20.7 million. An impairment charge of £10.7 million and the elimination of
a revaluation reserve of £10.0 million have therefore been posted in the period. It should be noted however that
the cost of reinstatement of these assets to the Club, established at the same valuation date, is £196 million.

2.5 Turnover
Turnover comprises revenue generated by the Club's principal activities excluding transfer fees,
donations and value added tax. Revenue streams include the below:
Match day
Match day revenue consists of all income receivable in respect of match day fixtures held both at
home and away. it is inclusive of ticket revenues for home fixtures and a share of gate receipts from
cup fixtures held both at home and away from Bramall Lane. The share of gate receipts payable in
respect of cup fixtures is deducted from revenue.
Match day revenue which is received in advance of a period end but relating to a future year (mainly
in relation to seasonal facilities) is treated as deferred income. The deferred income is then released
to revenue as the matches are played.
Broadcasting
Broadcasting revenues represent income receivable from all broadcasting and publishing contracts
including the Premier League central deals, EFL distributions, broadcasting revenue from cup
fixtures, internet revenues and programme income. Premier League and EFL distributions comprise
of a fixed element, which is recognised equally over home fixtures played, along with additional
revenues that are variable based on final league position.
: Sponsorship & advertising .

Sponsorship and advertising revenues include revenue receivable from sponsors in the year,
including shirt sponsorship and other advertising at the ground. Such revenue is recognised on an
accruals basis across the years the sponsorship and advertising income relates to.

. Assets that are subject to depreciation or amortisation are assessed at each reporting date to

2.21 Pensions
Defined contribution pension plan :
The Club operates a defined contribution plan for its employees. A defined contribution plan is a ;
pension plan under which the Club pays fixed contributions into a separate entity. Once the
contributions have been paid the Club has no further payment obligations.
The contributions are recognised as an expense in the Statement of Comprehensive Income when
they fall due. Amounts not paid are shown in accruals as a liability in the Statement of Financial
Position. The assets of the plan are held separately from the Club in independently administered
funds.
Multi-employer pension plan
The Club is a member of a multi-employer plan. Where it is not possible for the Club to obtain
sufficient information to enable it to account for the plan as a defined benefit plan, it accounts for the
plan as a defined contribution plan.
Where the plan is in deficit and where the Club has agreed, with the plan, to participate in a deficit
funding arrangement the Club recognises a liability for this obligation. The amount recognised is the
net present value of the contributions payable under the agreement that relate to the deficit. This
amount is expensed in the Statement of Comprehensive Income. The unwinding of the discount is
recognised as a finance cost/(benefit).

2.24 Current and deferred taxation
Tax is recognised in the Statement of Comprehensive Income except that a charge attributable to an
item of income and expense recognised as other comprehensive income or to an item recognised
directly in equity is also recognised in other comprehensive income or directly in equity respectively.
The current income tax charge is calculated on the basis of tax rates and laws that have been
enacted or substantively enacted by the reporting date in the countries where the Club operates and
generates income.

Deferred tax is calculated at the tax rates that are expected to apply to the period when the asset is
tealised or the liability is settled based on tax rates that have been enacted or substantively enacted
by the reporting date.

Deferred tax liabilities are recognised in respect of all timing differences that exist at the reporting
date. Timing differences are differences between taxable profits and total comprehensive income that
arise from the inclusion of income and expenses in tax assessments in different periods from their
recognition in the financial statements. Deferred tax assets are recognised only to the extent that it is
probable that they will be recovered by the reversal of deferred tax liabilities or other future taxable
profits.

Current tax assets and current tax liabilities and deferred tax assets and deferred tax liabilities are
offset, if and only if, there is a legally enforceable right to set off the amounts and the entity intends
either to settle on the net basis or to realise the asset and settle the liability simultaneously.

26. Pension commitments

Sheffield United Football Club (‘the Club’) participates in the Football League Pension and Life Assurance
Scheme (‘the Scheme’). The Scheme is a funded multi-employer defined benefit scheme, with 92
participating employers, and where members may have periods of service attributable to several
participating employers. The Club is unable to identify its share of the assets and liabilities of the Scheme
and therefore accounts for its contributions as if they were paid to a defined contribution scheme.
The last actuarial valuation was carried out at 31 August 2023 where the total deficit on the on-going
valuation basis was £20.6 million. The key assumptions used to calculate the deficit at the 31 August 2020
actuarial valuation are:
Discount Rate: Gilt Yield Curve + 0.5% per annum
RPI inflation: Bank of England Inflation Curve
Pension Increases: Fixed 3.0% per annum for benefits accrued prior to 6 April 1997, and

modelled using the RPI inflation assumption with a cap of 5.0% per

annum and a floor of 3.0% per annum for benefits accrued after 6 April

1997. .
Mortality (pre-retirement): None
Mortality (post-retirement): SAPS (Series 3) with CMI_2022 projections, a long-term improvement

rate of 1.5% p.a. and an initial additional parameter of 0.5% p.a.
The accrual of benefits ceased within the Scheme on 31 August 1999, therefore there are no contributions
relating to current accrual. The Club pays monthly contributions based on a notional split of the total
expenses and deficit contributions of the Scheme.
The results of the 2023 valuation were rolled forward to 30 June 2024 on the same assumptions as
detailed above, and the Club’s notional share of the deficit was £509,576 (£484, 123 as at 30 June 2023).
As at 30 June 2024, the Club was paying total contributions of £10,984 per month (increasing by 5% p.a.
on 1 September each year) and based on the actuarial valuation assumptions detailed above will be
sufficient to pay off the deficit by 30 November 2028.
As at 30 June 2024, based on an appropriate discount rate of 4.96% per annum (6.39% per annum as at
30 June 2023), the present value of the Club’s outstanding contributions (i.e. their future liability) is
£592,444 (£497,167 as at 30 June 2023). This amounts to £137,296 (30 June 2023: £130,758) due within
one year and £455,148 (30 June 2023: £366,403) due after more than one year and is included within
other payables.
