Directors’ duties under section 172
The purpose of this statement is to outline how, during the year, the directors of the company had regard to the
matters set out in section 172(1) (a) to (f) of the Companies Act 2006 when performing their duties under section
172.
Under section 172(1) a director of a company must act in the way that he or she considers, in good faith, would be
most likely to promote the success of the company for the benefit of its members as a whole, and in doing so have
regard (amongst other matters) to:

+ the likely consequences of any decision in the long term;

« the interests of the company's employees;

+ the need to foster the company’s business relationships with suppliers, customers and others;

« the impact of the company’s operations on the community and the environment;

+ the desirability of the company maintaining a reputation for high standards of business conduct; and

« the need to act fairly towards all members of the company.
In order to fulfil their duties under section 172, the directors need to ensure that the company not only acts in
accordance with its legal duties but also has regard for, all its stakeholders when taking decisions. Understanding
the company’s stakeholders and how they and their interests will impact on the strategy and success of the
company over the long term is a key factor in the decisions that the Board make.
Fans
Every decision made at Chelsea FC is taken with the direct or indirect aim of promoting and improving on pitch
performance and, in turn, to provide enjoyment and entertainment to our fans. The Club's ownership group is firmly
committed to building on the Club’s remarkable history of success, and making fans proud.
The Club continues to offer a range of ways for supporters to find out about club activity, both domestic fanbase
and those living overseas. Fans are informed about Club activity through its website, official app, social media,
matchday programmes and more. Across all of these channels, we provide access to the players and coaches from
our men’s, women's and academy teams as well as training and match highlights, all within the safety requirements .
of the Government, the Premier League and The Footbali Association.

: The Club continues to develop Fan Engagement, with the key role of the Fan Advisory Board ('FAB') and the
growth of a dedicated Supporter Relations team of eight full-time staff working across a multitude of projects and
campaigns to enhance and develop fan sentiment and to ensure our supporters remain well-informed.

The FAB has played a pivotal role in club strategy that directly impacts supporters. This is shown through the
ongoing and regular consultation between senior club officials and the FAB on a breadth of areas including ticket
pricing as well as our transition to digital ticketing.
The Ciub continues to meet with a supporters on a consistent basis through the Fans Forum, Access Forum and

- Women’s Team forum. The Forums consist of a body of elected fans and senior members of the Club management
and meet at least three times per season to discuss Club matters with the aim of maintaining a positive and
constructive dialogue between the Club and fans, developing a better understanding of matchday and operational
issues that affect supporters and providing an opportunity for the Club to discuss new ideas.

Partners
The commercial partners of the Club are fundamental to our success. The Club works hard to build long lasting
relationships with like-minded brands that can help bring value to our club, our fans and our employees, moving

It is also vitally important to the Club that partners understand our commitment to innovation, success and

Employees
The Club are determined to establish and nurture a culture of transparency, accountability, inclusivity, diversity and
opportunity for all Chelsea employees. There is a clear intent is for the Club to be a beacon of hope, positivity and
good leadership for employees and the communities we all serve.
The Club will continue to build on the initial work they have already done to create a sustainable, positive culture
that will ensure Chelsea FC's future success. Through a comprehensive review of the structures, processes,
policies and support needed for its people across the organisation, taking steps to instil an environment consistent
with their values with a significant investment into the organisation and its employees.
The physical and mental health and wellbeing of all employees is paramount to our mission. We are creating a
workplace environment and corporate culture that empowers everyone in the Chelsea community to be safe,
included, and trusted as we work tighter to send and achieve the highest standards.
The club-wide employee survey and established feedback forums provide valuable insights that inform our people
priorities and wellbeing initiatives. The output from these surveys drives our action planning and helps build our
desired culture through targeted activities and support programs. The output of the first survey has informed people
priorities and initiatives and activities to support wellbeing and building the desired culture. Other forums to obtain
employee sentiment have been established.
The Club’s values are at the core of our people-related activities both on and off-the-pitch. Significant progress has
been made in relation to equality, diversity and inclusion, promoting fair and equitable policies and procedures
throughout its recruitment, retention, training, development, and recognition processes. The introduction of
employee resource groups connected to ED&I will only further strengthens our commitment in this area. Chelsea
FC's dedication to equal and fair opportunities is demonstrated through continued support of London and National
Living Wage, our Disability Confident accreditation, our early commitment to the FA Football Leadership Diversity
Code and continued work on the Premier League Equality Standard at Advanced Level.
The Club has also enhanced its maternity leave policy to better support employees during this important life stage,
reflecting its commitment to work-life balance and family wellbeing The Club has introduced quarterly performance
reviews to foster a continuous feedback culture, which informs the annual pay review cycle that focuses on
recognising performance and contribution.
Through these comprehensive efforts, Chelsea FC is building an inclusive, supportive workplace where every
employee can contribute to the organisation’s shared success.

Decisions are made in relation to the long-term strategy of the Club following thorough discussions between board

Regulatory bodies
The Company is regulated by The FA, the Premier League, UEFA and FIFA. As explained elsewhere in the
Strategic Report, the Company actively engages with these regulatory bodies to ensure compliance with all

Suppliers
The Club's suppliers are integral to the day-to-day operation of the business. Relationships are carefully managed
to ensure that the Club is always obtaining value for money and reflect the values of the club. The company seeks
to ensure that good relationships are maintained with suppliers through regular contact and the prompt payment of

Other stakeholders and the wider community
The Club is committed to ensuring that none of its activities have a detrimental impact on the wider community. The
Chelsea Foundation has a clear remit to focus on the communities close to Stamford Bridge, Kingsmeadow and
Chelsea Training Ground in Cobham. The Chelsea Foundation will continue to work closely with fan-led initiatives
and various projects designed to use the power of football and sport to motivate, educate and inspire.
With a hyper-local focus, The Chelsea Foundation works on a broad range of initiatives focusing on employment,
education, social deprivation, crime reduction, youth offending and much more.
The Club continually tracks and reviews to work towards further sustainable and environmentally friendly projects.
The intention is to continue to improve efficiency in the use of all resources and adopt an approach to implement
low and zero carbon technologies where appropriate.
The Club is committed to treating all relevant legat and environmental legislation and guidelines as a minimum
requirement and will seek to exceed them wherever possible. The Club is also fully ESOS (Energy Savings
Opportunities Scheme) compliant. The Club continues to assess potential climate risks. This is a developing
process which will be developed on in future reporting periods. At this time, the Club see the process of managing
these risks as having no material impact on the business. .
Decision making and section 172 of the Companies Act 2006
The Club’s primary strategy is to deliver on-field success, whilst taking into consideration all stakeholders.
During the past year the Club continued to support the needs of staff and the wider community.
The Chelsea Foundation continues to provide outstanding leadership, with its staff at the forefront of the delivery of
education programmes and established support sessions for people of all ages.
The Club continues to expand our anti-discrimination work with its No To Hate campaign. Using the club's global
reach, we provide guidance for fans on how to report racist abuse, both in the stadium and online. We work to
identify perpetrators of racist abuse and take the strongest possible action against them, working with police and
authorities to secure prosecutions. The club will continue to work with football authorities and campaigning
organisations to support best practice on tackling racism and promoting equality, diversity and inclusion.
The No To Hate campaign runs in tandem with the Club's Equality Action Plan, as well as our work as signatories to
the FA’s Football Leadership Diversity Code and our support for the Premier League’s No Room For Racism Action
Plan.
The Club also took action to manage costs throughout the period to protect the wider commercial interests of the
business.

Review of the business
Profit and Loss
The loss for the year before taxation was £256.7m compared to a loss of £64.7m for the prior year. Whilst overall
revenue increased by £27.5m year-on-year, cost of sales and administrative expenses rose by £129.0m. Profit on
disposal of player registrations decreased by £94.3m.
This year saw an overall increase in turnover from £415.0m to £442.5m, largely due to the Club's increase in
broadcasting revenue. Matchday revenue also increased, from £76.4m in the prior year to £87.2m and average
attendance of approximately 40,000 was maintained.
Broadcasting revenue increased by £40.1m to £203.2m compared to the prior year. The Club generated significant
revenue from their participation in both the UEFA Conference League and the FIFA Club World Cup, going on to
win both competitions. The Club also achieved a 4' place finish in the Premier League, resulting in a higher
domestic broadcasting share compared to last season.
Commercial revenue decreased from £175.5m in the prior year to £152.0m, largely due to reduced sponsorship
revenue.
Overall, operating expenses of £750.4m increased by £128.0m compared to the previous year. This was principally
because of increased amortisation and impairment on player registrations, increased cost of sales including player
wages, and costs related to regulatory matters, including amounts due of £26.5m under a settlement agreement
with UEFA which concluded during the year.
The Company made a net profit on player trading of £57.9m in the year (2024: £152.2m) principally due to the
sales of Conor Gallagher to Atletico de Madrid, Bashir Humphreys to Burnley, Kepa Arrizabalaga to Arsenal and
Angelo to Al-Nassr. In addition, the Company realised contingent fees in relation to a number of previous transfers.
Balance Sheet
intangible assets have increased to £1,043.9m from £1,030.7m. This movement is largely due to £305.5m of player
acquisitions including Liam Delap from Ipswich Town, Mike Penders from KRC Genk, Dario Essugo from Sporting
CP and Mamadou Sarr from Racing Club de Strasbourg Alsace. This was offset by total amortisation and
impairment losses of £224.3m and the disposal of player registrations with a combined net book value of £67.9m.
Tangible fixed assets are £126.7m at the year end, with additions of £2.4m offset by depreciation of £9.3m, and
disposals with net book value of £9.4m due to the sale of Kingsmeadow football stadium to fellow group
undertaking Chelsea Football Club Women Limited.
Net current liabilities of £2.3m have increased from £2.1m in the prior year, due to the increase of creditor amounts
falling due within one year, which includes accrued amounts owed to UEFA.
Creditors falling due after more than one year of £202.3m have decreased by £17.0m compared to the prior year.
This is primarily due to a decrease in the amounts owed in relation to player trading, offset by non-current amounts
owed to UEFA.

Principal risks and uncertainties
There are a number of potential risks and uncertainties which could have a material impact on the Clubs’s long-
term performance. These risks and uncertainties are monitored by the Board on a regular basis. The directors have :
specifically considered the additional risk to the Company of the persistent inflationary pressures, rising operating
costs, and currency volatility seen in the UK. Forecasted increases in operating costs because of these factors
have been included in the Club's budget to assess potential additional funding requirements as described further in
Note 1.3. The directors have begun monitoring the potential impact of climate change on the Club’s operations,
including regulatory compliance requirements, although this isn’t currently considered material.
income
The Club derives its income from football activities and related business activities of which there are three principal
sources: matchday revenue, broadcasting and commercial income.
Expenditure
: The Club's primary outgoings relate to matchday operations; the continued development of the football club's

brand; employee remuneration; support services to facilitate elite sporting performance; and the development of the
playing squad. The last of these is achieved through a combination of activity in the transfer market and investment
in the Club’s academy and youth programmes to nurture and develop young players for the future.
Regulatory environment
The Club is regulated by the rules of The FA, the Premier League, UEFA and FIFA. These regulations have a direct
impact on the football club as they cover areas such as the division of centrally negotiated broadcasting and media
agreements and the operation of the transfer market.
The Club monitors its compliance with all applicable regulations on a continuous basis, considering the impact of
potential changes and taking mitigating action where possible. The Club aims to balance success on the pitch
together with the financial imperatives of complying with UEFA and Premier League financial regulations.
Funding
Funding is provided by the intermediate parent company, Blueco 22 Limited, which is controlled by a consortium led
by Todd Boehly and Clearlake.
The Company reviews and updates its forecasts on a regular basis and keeps the parent company and ownership
aware of its financial commitments going forward. .
