GROUP STRATEGIC REPORT
FOR THE YEAR ENDED 30 JUNE 2025
Introduction
The principal activity of the Company continues to be the operation of a professional football club, together with
related activities.
The Directors present their strategic report for the year ended 30th June 2025.
Business review
The year under review saw the club achieving its primary objective of retaining EFL League One status, where
the on-field performance built on that of the previous season, whilst our strategic focus off-field continued to be
striving to be financially sustainable, with first class fan engagement, and the continuing development of future
talent through our academy.
However, a number of key issues towards the end of the accounting period, affected that position, and will
continue to do so in the year to come. The resignation of both Club Board Chair, and Chief Executive Officer in
close succession to each other, brought about a challenging end to the accounting period, which will more
adversely affect the 2025/26 accounting period.
This report provides a comprehensive overview of the club's financial performance, key achievements and
future strategies.
Profit Before Tax
Profit before tax reduced in 2025, although remained positive at £526,637. Administration expenses went up
with maintenance to the ground and infrastructure increasing on 2024 along with inflationary rises on the cost of
utilities and fuel experienced across the country.
Investment
The Club was able to purchase the freehold of the main building at St James Park, via a loan from the
Supporters’ Trust.
The on-going receipts for former players, assisted with the cashflow throughout the year, as did the sale of
. Millenic Alli to Luton in January 2025, and Jake Richards to the same club at the end of the season.
. Future Outlook

It would not be exaggerating to say that the club is facing a challenging time for its financial future. Many
mitigating actions have already been done to improve this situation, including a series of redundancies across
the breadth of the club, as well as reducing budgets in all areas, so as to get as close to sustainability as
possible. A big factor in that will be the retention of EFL League One status at the end of the 2025/26 season.
The club is however, committed to continue to be a sustainable football club, playing football at the highest
possible level. .
Summary
As Interim Chair of the Board, it is hard for me to write a summary of the 2024/25 season, as | was not on the
Board of Directors at the time. However, | can see that the work of everyone involved in the club, including
those Directors who were to step down later in 2025, ensured the best possible year and future of the club,
despite the ever increasing challenges that are brought by EFL League One.

Principal risks and uncertainties
The management of Exeter City AFC Ltd, and the nature of our ownership model in the ever increasing
competitive business in which we operate, continues to bring many risks — most notably that of being financially
sustainable, without a reliance on player sales. Detailed and accurate financial planning is required to ensure
that the club can survive, and, following the departure of the Chief Executive Officer at the end of this
accounting period, a detailed review of the club’s finances and cashflow, was undertaken, leading to the need
for an immediate reduction in costs and loans from the Supporters’ Trust.
Financial certainty remains our number one risk, and strenuous efforts are continually being made to ensure the
club can meet its many obligations going forward. This could lead to a reduction in all budgets across the
business, including the playing budget, which, in itself, brings about a risk of relegation from EFL League One,
and the consequent reduction in central funding this may bring. This is constantly on the Board's deliberations
to mitigate this situation as best as possible.
Allied to this, is the need to ensure that the Club employs staff who can help meet these expectations, and as
such a review of the staffing structure has been undertaken, which has, unfortunately led to a number of
, redundancies. It is now key that staff employed can meet the club’s aspirations of achieving financial stability
and sustainability.

Events since end of year
As mentioned previously, following the resignation of the Chief Executive Officer in June 2025, a thorough and
detailed review of the Club’s finances and cashflow was undertaken, which identified an exaggeration of the
club’s immediate financial position. This needed the immediate injection of cash from the Supporters’ Trust to
cover the club’s on-going commitments, as well as the voluntary reporting of VAT payments which had originally
been paid incorrectly. This has, and continues to place a detailed strain on the club as a whole, including a
wholescale change of Directors on the Board. However, the new Board continues to follow the previous
endeavours of striving for financial sustainability, and will continue to support all involved in the football club in
achieving this.

Engagement with employees
Exeter City FC use different methods to ensure employees are provided with information on matters of concern
and interest to them. We use these methods as a principal means of consultation ensuring employees are
involved in decision-making at a Management level. The football club use Workbuzz surveys to understand
employees’ views and holds meetings to keep employees informed on the performance of the Club.
Exeter City FC are registered as a ‘Real Living Wage employer’, having signed a charter to demonstrate
commitment to paying the Real Living Wage.
Exeter City FC is an equal opportunities employer and works in conjunction with the Equality Act 2010,
supporting disabled people including those with long health term health conditions, learning disabilities and so-
called ‘hidden’ disabilities such as dyslexia. If an applicant confirms that they have a disability, we make
teasonable adjustments to ensure that any selection process including interviews are fair and equitable.

Post balance sheet events
After the year end, the Club has invoiced £1,094,760 in connection with the sale of players. These are all
payable over a number of years.
Subsequent to the year end, the Board identified adjustments made within the accounting records that had the
effect of delaying VAT payments during the financial year. These matters were investigated and disclosed to
HMRC.
After the year end, a disciplinary hearing involving Joe Gorman concluded, and no appeal was lodged.
Changes to the club's finance team also occurred after the balance sheet date, with the departure of the
existing team and the appointment of an interim Financial Controller, Ray Storey.
Following the year end, the club received £200,000 of additional financial support from the Supporters’ Trust to
assist with cash flow difficulties.
On 12th November 2025, a fire broke out at St James Park and caused approximately £100k worth of damage.
As this event was deemed to be accidental, this was covered by insurance, and is non-adjusting.
In December 2025, the club received £985,000 from Close Brothers Leasing in connection with the factoring of
the future sell on clause relating to Jay Stansfield.

3.3. Going concern
Although the accounts show a profit of £348,214 due to transfer revenue, with the removal of this, it
shows a loss of £4,581,291, £281,388 of this loss relates to the women’s team, and £1,492 of this
loss relates to OTR (Exeter) Limited, with the remaining loss attributable to the men’s team. The
position of the company looks more favourable due to transfer revenue, but these are paid in
instalments across many years, meaning that this still creates an ongoing issue with cashflow.
The directors have considered the going concern basis of the Company taking into account the
recent events affecting the club’s finances. The directors have considered all aspects of the
Company's business when looking at the going concern basis.
Whilst the Company has posted a profit for the year ended 30th June 2025, it needed to seek a joan
of £400k from its majority shareholder Exeter City Supporters Trust to meet its obligations related to
the June 2025 payroll. A further loan was needed in November 2025 of £200k again to meet its
: payroll obligations.

In early December 2025 the Company arranged an advance of funds of £1m in relation to
instalments of player transfer fees that were not due to be received until July 2026, July 2027 and
July 2028.
Following a review of the Company's finances, several steps were taken to reduce costs including
employee redundancies. In addition, a full cashflow analysis was carried out to manage the income
and costs through to the end of the current season (2025-26). Further cashflows and budgets have
been prepared for the 2026-27 season running different scenarios, which sees the continued need
to reduce costs and increase income where possible in order for the Company to operate. These
budgets are in the first instance challenging and rely on the continued financial support of the
Supporters’ Trust, however, there are significant items of potential contingent income relating to past
players that would make this more manageable.
The directors acknowledge that due to the uncertainty of the future contingent income and the
teliance on the Supporters Trust, there exists a material uncertainty regarding going concern,
however, the preparation of the accounts on a going concern basis is still deemed appropriate.
