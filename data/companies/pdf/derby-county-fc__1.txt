Principal activities
The company's principal activities relate to the operation of a professional football club, competing in the English
Football League (EFL); the operation of a category one football academy; the hosting of conferencing and
events; an online club specific TV channel, ‘Rams TV'; and commercial activities relating to sponsorship and
advertising.
Business review
The company was incorporated on 23 June 2022 and is a wholly owned subsidiary of Clowes Developments
(UK) Limited, a company based in Ashbourne, Derbyshire, specialising in the acquisition of land, buildings and
property in order to achieve development sales. .
On 1 July 2022 the company purchased the trade and assets and certain liabilities as prescribed by EFL
regulations, of SEVCO 5112 Limited (in Administration), The Derby County Football Club Limited (in
Administration), Derby County FC Academy Limited (in Administration), Club DCFC Limited (in Administration)
and Stadia DCFC Limited (in Administration), collectively "the group (in Administration)".
The purchase was funded by Clowes Developments (UK) Limited who also acquired the club's stadium, Pride
Park Stadium, as a separate transaction through its acquisition of Gellaw 202 Limited. As a major investor and
property owner in the East Midlands, Clowes Developments (UK) Limited considered that the potential
liquidation of the local city football club was not in the best interests of the company or those of the city of Derby
itself. Clowes Developments (UK) Limited considered it had a corporate social responsibility to the local
economy of Derby and could not stand by and let the football club fail.
The key message from the opening statement issued on behalf of David Clowes when Clowes Developments
(UK) Limited purchased the club in 2022 was that around three key pillars: Stability, Integrity and Progress and
these remain at the forefront of the club's direction and strategy.
In May 2025 Clowes Developments (UK) Limited took the decision that Derby County Football Club and Pride
Park Stadium were to be unified once again within a new holding company. .
While the club and stadium have both been owned by Clowes Developments (UK) Limited, since the summer of
2022, they have been held in separate legal entities, the club within this company, (Derby County (The Rams)
Limited), and Pride Park Stadium in Gellaw Newco 202 Limited.
On 14 May 2025 the stadium company Gellaw Newco 202 Limited was re-named Derby County (The Rams)
Stadium Limited and sits alongside this company (Derby County (The Rams) Limited) within the group structure
and both companies are now 100% owned by a new holding company, Derby County (The Rams) Holdings
Limited.
Derby County (The Rams) Holdings Limited is 100% owned by Clowes Developments (UK) Limited (the ultimate
parent undertaking) and although there is no change of ownership, the move reflects a desire to allay concerns
from supporters regarding the separation of club and stadium (see note 24).
For the 2026 year end Derby County (The Rams) Holdings Limited will now be the reporting entity to the
Financial Reporting Unit of the English Football League.
David Clowes said at the time, “Having secured our goal of maintaining our Championship status after
promotion from League One, we felt it was the right time to make this change.”
In strict accordance with club regulations, the changes were discussed fully with the Supporters’ Board.

Financial review
The company generated revenue of £31.9m (2024: £19.4m),which was predominantly driven by increased
league and broadcasting income of £12m (2024: £2.9m). Ticketing, hospitality and sponsorship income
increased by 24% compared to prior year to £18.3m (2024: £14.8m). Ticketing revenue was significantly
bolstered by the club’s strong average home attendance of 29,018, which was in the top 4 of the EFL
Championship. A season high of 32,471 on the final day of the EFL Championship against Stoke City saw the
Rams secure Championship league status for the 25/26 campaign.
The company generated an operating loss of £11.1m (2024: £14.2m) which included profits on disposal of
player registrations and associated costs, primarily relating to compensation payments and contingent transfer
receipts of £10.3m (2024: £4.6m).
At the Balance Sheet date, the company had net liabilities of £44.9m (2024: £33.6m). The movement on net
liabilities is predominantly related to amounts owed to Clowes Developments (UK) Limited in relation to owner
funding injections for the day to day running of the club.
The loss for the year was £11.3m (2024: £14. 1m).
Football review
The 2024/25 season was one of growth and transition, with the appointment of former Derby County player
John Eustace as Head Coach inspiring a run of form that saw the club retain its EFL Championship status on
the final day of the campaign.
Eustace signed a three-and-a-half year contract in February 2025 to return to Pride Park Stadium and replace
Paul Warne, who departed with the thanks, gratitude and best wishes of everyone at the club.
The team were bottom of the table and seven points adrift of safety with 11 games to play, but six wins and
three draws from those games saw the club jump five places to finish 19th and secure another season in the
second tier.
A total of 18 new players were recruited permanently and on loan throughout the campaign, as the club
continued to reshape the Men's First Team squad following promotion back to the Championship in April 2024.
The club recruited senior internationals in the form of Sweden's Jacob Widell Zetterstraém and Norway's Sondre
Langas, while Derby County Academy graduate Eiran Cashin joined Premier League side Brighton and Hove
Albion for a significant undisclosed fee.
Ebou Adams’ form during his first full season at Pride Park was rewarded with the Jack Stamps Player of the
Season and Men’s Players’ Player of the Season awards.
Homegrown midfielder Liam Thompson was named as Sammy Crooks Young Player of the Season, while on-
loan striker Jerry Yates was the club's top goal scorer with 10 league goals.
Derby County Women marked their second season as a fully integrated part of the club, following on from the
successful 2023/24 campaign, when they won the FA Women’s National League Plate.
With their own base at the Moor Farm Training Centre and access to all facilities, the Women’s First Team
squad won six of their 22 games to finish ninth in the FA Women’s National League Northern Premier Division,
while the club continue to develop local footballers through the Female Talent Pathway.

Principal risks and uncertainties
Due to the principal activities of the company, the revenues of the business are susceptible to the on-pitch
performance of the football team. This is mitigated by maintaining top class training facilities, in order to attract
the best talent, having competitive wage budgets and operating a category one academy to ensure there is an
appropriate player pathway to the first team and to build value for the future.
The company's credit risk is relatively low and is primarily attributable to its trade debtors. Credit risk is
managed by running credit checks on new customers and by monitoring payments against contractual
agreements.
The company is financed by cash injections and the revenue that is raised through its business activities. The
objective is to ensure a mix of funding methods offering flexibility and cost effectiveness to match the needs of
the company; however the directors note that such dependency on parent company support is critical to
remaining a going concern.

Sustainability
Derby County Football Club together with its affiliated charity, Derby County Community Trust, play a vital role
within the local community. We recognise the impact that all aspects of the Club have on the environment.
Derby County is committed to protecting the environment from our work activities which is demonstrated by our
commitment to the implementation of actions aligned to the English Football League's Green Clubs Scheme.
Over the past year we have been awarded the English Football League Green Clubs Bronze Status. As a club
we undertook a number of key measures to show our progress such as; we made a club commitment to ESG
through a board approved statement which is shared publicly via our website, ensured compliance across the
business with all environmental legislation, Installed additional solar panels at Moor Farm as well as 2 EV
chargers for staff usage, re-commissioned the bio-mass boilers at both the stadium and training ground and
onboarded the new legislation relating to simpler recycling across both sites.
Over the coming year we have set clear performance indicators for ESG, complete a lifecycle analysis across
both our sites, look at the rewilding of our training ground, review our electricity, gas and water consumptions,
continue to review our waste management with clear focus on food waste, look at our suppliers and how we
procure across the business and develop new ways for staff and supporter engagement on ESG.

2.3. Turnover
Turnover represents income receivable, net of discounts and VAT, and excluding transfer fees

pattern of the lessee’s benefit from the use of the leased asset.

20. Pension commitments
The company participates in the Football League Pension and Life Assurance Scheme (the 'Scheme’).
The Scheme is a funded multi-employer defined benefit scheme, with 92 participating employers, and
where members may have periods of service attributable to several participating employers. The
company is unable to identify its share of the assets and liabilities of the Scheme and therefore accounts
for its contributions as if they were paid to a defined contribution scheme.
The last actuarial valuation was carried out at 31 August 2023 where the total deficit on the on-going
valuation basis was £20.6m.
The accrual of the benefits ceased within the Scheme on 31 August 1999, therefore there are no
contributions relating to the current accrual. The company pays monthly contributions based on a notional
split of the total expenses and deficit contributions of the Scheme. The liability outstanding as at 30 June
2025 was £294,318 (2024: £362,858) and contributions during the period amounted to £97,146 (2024:
£118,298). As the Scheme is closed to accrual, there are no additional costs associated with the accruing
of members’ future benefits. In the case of the football club being relegated from the football league, or
the company ceasing to trade, and being unable to settle its debt then the remaining clubs may, in
exceptional circumstances, have to share the deficit.
The company also operates a stakeholder defined contribution pensions scheme for qualifying
employees and directors. The assets of the scheme are administered by an independent pensions
provider.
Total pension payments recognised as an expense during the period amounted to £215,676 (2024:
£298,267). Pension contributions outstanding as at 30 June 2025 totalled £42,069 (2024: £60,909).
