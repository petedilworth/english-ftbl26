The directors present the strategic report for the year ended 30 June 2024.
Fair review of the business
The 2023/24 season was the club’s first season in the top tier since 1991/92 but despite some truly memorable
performances it ultimately ended in disappointment, as the club finished in 18th place in the Premier League and
was relegated back to the EFL Championship.
Having secured promotion via the play-off final in May 2023 the club immediately set about planning for life in the
Premier League, with improvements to Kenilworth Road stadium, including an entire new stand to accommodate
more fans and meet the broadcast and media requirements of the league. There were also upgrades to the
training facility at the Brache, new office space for back office staff and funds ringfenced for the club’s longer term
investment plans for an upgraded Academy and the new stadium at Power Court.
As well as infrastructure, one of the most important areas for investment was the playing squad, which started with
the acquisition of Mads Andersen from Barnsley prior to the 2022/23 year end. As the summer transfer window
progressed the club broke its transfer record on a number of occasions as it looked to improve the depth and
quality of the squad. Player acquisitions included Chiedozie Ogbene from Rotherham, Tahith Chong from
Birmingham, Marvelous Nakamba from Aston Villa, Ryan Giles from Wolverhampton Wanderers, Thomas Kaminski
from Blackburn Rovers, Jacob Brown from Stoke City and Teden Mengi from Manchester United.
In addition free agents Tim Krul, Andros Townsend and Ross Barkley were added to the ranks to bring some extra
Premier League experience, with the latter proving to be one of the bargains of the summer, proving he still had the
quality to play at the top level of the game and finishing the season as Luton's player of the season. The squad
was also strengthened by the !oan signings of Albert Sambi Lokonga from Arsenal, who would form an outstanding
midfield pairing with Ross Barkley, although his season was hampered by injury, as well as the pacey wing-back
Issa Kabore from Manchester City.
The team initially found it difficult to find their feet at the higher level with defeats away at Brighton and Chelsea
coming before a EFL Cup win at home to Gillingham. The club's first point of the season came in their fifth game as
they drew 1-1 against a Wolverhampton Wanderers team reduced to ten men, but this was immediately followed by
their first win, as they beat Everton 2-1 away from home at the end of September. Early November saw a
remarkable performance end in heartbreak as Liverpool equalised at the death to deny the home fans their first
home win of the season and they had to wait until the end of the month to finally see the team run out 2-1 winners
against Crystal Palace at Kenilworth Road. Early December witnessed an end to end encounter again end in last
minute dejection as Arsenal narrowly triumphed 4-3.
However, the truly seismic event of the season occurred on the pitch as Luton played Bournemouth at the Vitality
Stadium as club captain Tom Lockyer suffered a cardiac arrest. Thanks to the rapid actions of both clubs’ medical
staff, as well as emergency service workers, Tom's life was saved and tragedy was averted but, understandably, he
would not feature for the rest of the season. The incident seemed to galvanise the team as they immediately put
together back-to-back wins for the first time in the season, shortly followed, at the end of January, by their most
impressive result in the Premier League as they ran out 4-0 winners at home against Brighton.
January also afforded the opportunity to bolster the squad with the arrivals of Japanese international Daiki
Hashioka from Sint-Truiden and Tom Holmes and Taylan Harris both from Reading. In hindsight the club might
have reinforced more, as from the turn of the year onwards, injuries began to blight the season with a number of
key players succumbing to long term issues that would see them miss all or large parts of the remainder of the
season. Indeed the club recorded eleven missing players in a single game week, the joint-highest in the 2023/24
season, whilst eighteen of the twenty six man squad were ruled out at some point, with many of these injuries
concentrated in the second half of the season, severely hampering the team’s efforts to avoid the drop.
The season was also notable for the first points deductions by the Premier League for breaches of profitability and
sustainability rules which made for a hard fought relegation battle running to the penultimate game of the season.
The team took on West Ham United at the London Stadium on the 11th of May 2024 where a 3-1 defeat confirmed
the club's relegation back to the EFL Championship.

The team received many acknowledgments for the way they acquitted themselves throughout the season, having
been favourites to go straight back down at the outset. It was an impressive feat, with a comparatively limited
budget, to have fought to the second to last game of the season. But with relegation confirmed, the rebuilding job
began with several key players departing during the summer transfer window including the incomparable Ross
Barkley.
At the time of presenting these financial statements, the first team is occupying the lower half of the EFL
Championship tabie having had a difficult start to the 2024/25 season. The sizeable uplift in broadcast revenue as
a result of parachute payments has allowed the club to invest heavily in its long term strategic plan.
Financial Overview
Promotion to the Premier League resulted in significantly increased costs in 2022/23, as bonuses, contingent fees
and other related costs were incurred, but also led to a huge increase in turnover in 2023/24, as the club received
Premier League broadcasting distributions for the first time. Player wages also increased enormously from EFL
Championship levels, but gross profit still swung upwards by over £70m, illustrating the financial dominance of the
top tier of the English game.
Administrative expenses increased by approximately forty percent as the club grew to support the demands of the
Premier League and player trading activity was largely limited to acquisitions to improve the squad, which accounts
for the low level of related profit.
It is important to note, that due to on-going strategic investment and the way in which capital expenditure is
accounted for, the £48.9m operating profit is not excess cash, indeed the club’s bank balance is marginally lower at
the end of June 2024, than it was one year previously. All profits are reinvested to support the club’s long term
ambitions. For example the football club funded the Bobbers Stand redevelopment, spent over £25m on player
acquisitions, paid out promotion bonuses relating to 2022/23 season and made early repayment of the EFL COVID
loan. For transparency, it should be noted that no dividends have been paid to Directors from the club's profits.

The club invested a significant portion of the Premier League distributions in the playing squad as reflected in the
increased net book value of £22.3m. The new Bobbers Stand, as well as various other improvement works around
Kenilworth Road, raised the ground’s capacity and that, in conjunction with the return of top flight football resulted in
a large increase in average attendances. The wages to turnover ratio dropped significantly, as promotion bonuses
were included in the 2022/23 wage costs, whilst squad costs were controlled in 2023/24 to mitigate the risk of
relegation.
Principal risks and uncertainties
Having gained promotion to the Premier League at the end of the 2022/23 season, and with parachute payments
guaranteed for two years on relegation, the club has secured significantly increased revenues until at least
2025/26. However, this remains an uncertain time for the game with the Football Governance Bill aiming to
establish an independent regulator to ensure English football is financially sustainable. The redistribution of
broadcast revenue down the football pyramid remains an open question.
Financial risk management objectives and policies
League status risk
Due to the current distribution model of the football league, a principal risk for the football club is the performance of

. the first team in relation to league status. The group manages the impact of this through control of player wages
(relegation clauses) and situational planning dependent upon league status.
Credit risk
Financial instruments that potentially subject the club to credit risk consist primarily of cash equivalents and
accounts receivable. These instruments contain a risk of counterparties failing to discharge their obligations. The
club monitors credit risk and manages credit risk exposure by assessing the creditworthiness of counterparties.
Oue to the diversity of the debtor portfolio, the club does not assess this to pose a significant risk to the club at
present.
Liquidity risk
Our liquidity needs are affected by our performance on the pitch which impacts revenues and wage costs. Budgets
and cash flows are prepared annually and forecasts are updated on a monthly basis so funding requirements are
planned and approved well in advance. Despite relegation from the Premier League, the guaranteed parachute
payments for the following two seasons mean the Board assesses this risk as low.
Operational risk
The club operates a cashless stadium and has introduced access contro! systems for staff and fans. This has
greatly reduced operational risk associated with cash payments and unauthorised entry.
Future developments
The club’s long-term plan to consolidate towards the top of the football pyramid remains on course, particularly |
following promotion to the Premier League and the financial rewards that brought.
The club will continue to invest in a strong playing squad, as well as vital infrastructure, including the training
ground and new stadium at Power Court. The long held ambition of upgrading the Academy to EPPP2 (Elite Player
Performance Plan) will also be progressed in the coming year.

Employee Engagement
The group holds weekly management meetings, either in person or virtually, attended by departmental managers in
order to ensure that information can then be disseminated downwards to each department and employee.
The group actively encourages employees in any efforts to further their development via flexible working hours and
the funding of external training where applicable.

‘ In 2014 Luton Town Football Club became the first English professional football club to become a fully accredited
Living Wage employer, paying the ‘Real Living Wage’. The commitment to pay all employees a ‘Real Living Wage’
has been maintained throughout the period.

Administrative employees are provided the required office equipment in order to ensure that working from home is
a realistic and safe option.
The group offers a range of support to promote the health and mental wellbeing of staff. The Employee Assistance
Programme (EAP), which is confidential and managed independently by Health Assured, is available to all staff and
immediate family members. Annual health assessments are also undertaken for key workers and senior
executives.
The group continues to be an equal opportunities employer which is designed to ensure that all applicants and
employees receive the same treatment. Through the implementation of these principles, the group has a staff that
reflects the diversity of the town and wider region.
Upwards feedback from employees to managers is encouraged to drive improvement and employee engagement.
Employees are frequently involved in decision making through discussions with their managers.
Suppliers
The group continues to foster long term relationships by maintaining high standards of business conduct.
Department heads are responsible for ensuring that close communication is maintained with suppliers to allow
continued engagement and custom which is fair for both the group and the supplier. Local suppliers are utilised
where possible, in order to benefit the local economy.
Customers
Supporters of the club represent the most important customers of the group. Through the use of the club's website
and social media channels, the group is committed to communicating clearly and honestly with the fanbase. The
group also use these mediums to listen to feedback from supporters as well as distributing post-match
questionnaires to identify areas for improvement.
The group is actively engaged with the Luton Town Supports Trust (LTST), meeting with them on a regular basis.
Additionally, the Trust has the legal right to veto any future changes to Luton's identity, including its name,
nickname, colours, club crest and mascot. The CEO has also set up a direct messaging group chat with all LTST
committee members to increase transparency and immediacy of feedback on any issues.
Besides supporters, the group also has key customers such as sponsors, advertisers and tenants. Communication
with each customer is maintained by the relevant department head to ensure a positive customer relationship.

Community and Environment
Since acquiring ownership, the board has been committed to ensuring that the group has a positive impact upon
the local community and environment.
The group promotes the work of the Luton Town Community Trust, the official charity of Luton Town Football Club.
The Trust aims to help disadvantaged and vulnerable members of the local community.
The group has consistently made the conscious decision to avoid gambling sponsorship in the stadium, on the
website or on any aspect of the player kits. This is a matter of principle for the group and the board believe strongly
that gambling should not be promoted, particularly where children are exposed to such advertising.
Central to the ambitious development projects is a desire to bring economic growth and prosperity to the local
economy. Throughout the design and development stage of these key projects consultation has been held with the
local community to ensure that there has been a focus on delivering an end result which is mutually beneficial to
both the group and the inhabitants of Luton and Bedfordshire.
With regards to the long term sustainability of the environment, the board always consider the group's impact when
making key decisions. The primary waste removal supplier for the group sorts the waste to ensure that materials
are recycled and land-fill waste is minimised. The retail department use compostable mailing bags for posting
online orders and paper bags in store so that plastic waste can be reduced.
The stadium development project at Power Court has been designed with the environment in mind incorporating
cutting edge design which minimises impact upon the environment and promotes long term sustainability.
