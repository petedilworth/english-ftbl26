Strategic Report
for the year ended 30 June 2025
The Directors present their Strategic Report and Financial Statements for the year ended 30 June 2025.
The principal and continuing activity of the company is that of a football club, namely Birmingham City Football Club.
Financial and operating review
The loss for the year before tax of £34.6m represents a significant increase of approximately 115% as compared to the
loss of approximately £16.1m for the corresponding period jast year.
The 2024/2025 season saw the club play in the third tier of the English Football League (EFL). The squad enjoyed an
exceptional season, ultimately earning promotion back to the second tier of the EFL with a record breaking 111 points.
The year saw continued investment ‘into the playing squad and infrastructure at St Andrews @ Knighthead Park,
Knighthead Performance Centre and the Knighthead Elite Performance and Innovation Centre.
Despite relegation to the third tier of English football, the company has seen strong commercial growth, ultimately
increasing revenues year on year.
The company is firmly aligned with the Birmingham City Football Club Foundation (‘The Foundation’). This is the charitable
arm of the group and provides much needed support to the local community. The company held a hugely successful “Blue
Nose Day’ aiming to ensure 11,500 young people in care, received a Christmas gift from the Foundation.
The Birmingham City Football Club Academy continued to operate at Category 2 status under the Elite Player Performance
Plan at the start of the 2024/25 season. In May 2025, it was announced that the Birmingham City Football Club Academy
has been awarded Category 1 status after meeting strict criteria for staffing, facilities and infrastructure as audited by the
Professional Game Academy (PGA). Several academy trained players continued to participate in the first team squad
throughout the 2024/25 season.
The players, football management team, staff and directors would like to thank the fans for their loyal and continued
support. :

Principal risks and uncertainties
The directors of Birmingham City Football Club Limited have carried out a robust assessment of the principal risks facing
the company, including those that would threaten its business model, future performance, solvency, or liquidity.
In undertaking this review, the directors evaluated both internal and external risks, including financial, operational,
regulatory, and strategic exposures. Particular attention was given to the potential impact of the football industry's
regulatory framework, including the English Football League’s (EFL) Financial Fair Play regulations, as well as wider
macroeconomic conditions and market developments affecting football revenues, player trading, and operational costs.
The directors also considered the adequacy and effectiveness of the company's internal control and risk management
systems. This included reviewing the procedures for identifying, monitoring, and mitigating significant risks.
Funding and going concern
On 13 July 2023, the company’s ultimate parent undertaking, ZO Future Group (°ZO"), formerly known as Birmingham
Sports Holdings Limited (“BSHL”), announced that Shelby Companies Limited, owned by Knighthead Annuity & Life
Assurance Company and Knighthead Master Fund, L.P (Knighthead), had acquired 45.98% of the issued share capital of
the parent company Birmingham City Limited (“BCL”). Shelby Companies Limited acquired 24.34% of the shares in BCL
from ZO and 21.64% of the shares in BCL from Oriental Rainbow Investments Limited (ORIL).

Funding and going concern (continued) ,
On 7 November 2025, ZO Future Group Limited disposed of its 51.72% shareholding in Birmingham City Limited to Shelby
Companies Limited. Following the completion of this transaction, Shelby Companies Limited became the majority
shareholder of BCL. The directors have received written confirmation from Knighthead that continued financial support will
be provided for at least the next 12 months. At the balance sheet date funding of £188.3m has been provided by BCL to
the company, this amount is shown as due to the parent undertaking within one year.
In the prior year, the company entered into a facility agreement with funding available of up to £50m. The facility is available
for a period of 7 years from the date of the agreement and attracts a fixed interest rate of 11.90%. On 3 October 2024, a
Deed of Amendment was entered into between the BCL, BCFC and Shelby extending the loan facility to £100m. On 23
July 2025, a further Deed of Amendment was entered into between the BCL, BCFC and Shelby extending the loan facility
to £150m. The company does not have an overdraft facility and from 13 July 2024 onwards, the day to day funding
requirements were supported by Knighthead.
The directors have prepared detailed cash flow forecasts for the period to 31 December 2026 as part of the longer term
forecasts prepared for the company. The forecasts show that the company needs additional funding of approximately
£59m from Knighthead for the period from July 2025 to December 2026. The directors of the company and Knighthead
have indicated that they expect that the company will have sufficient working capital to be able to advance funds for the
company to meet its financial obligations as and when they fall due for the next twelve months and have therefore adopted
going concern as the basis of preparation with no material uncertainties noted.
Statement by the Directors on performance of their statutory duties in accordance with s172(1} Companies Act
2006
Section 172 of the Companies Act 2006 requires Directors to take into consideration the interests of stakeholders in their
decision making. This is achieved through regular contact (verbal and written) with the Senior Management Team and
external advisors, whose views are taken into consideration.
Long term decision making
The Directors, Senior Management and employees consider all potential impacts to the club and all relevant stakeholders
when making decisions believed to be in line with the long-term strategic plan of the group.
Any decision made is designed to lead to success on the pitch through promotion from the English Football League to the
Premier League. Given the short term nature of these objectives, the financial considerations are also heavily scrutinised
by the Senior Management to ensure the club remains financially stable.
Employees
The group places considerable value on the involvement of its employees and recognises the importance of good
communication within the workplace.
The quality and commitment of our people have played a major role in our business operations. This has been
demonstrated in many ways. They have shown flexibility in adapting to changing business environments and new ways of
working. Employees’ performance is aligned to the group's goals through an annual performance review process that is
carried out with all employees.
Over the 2024/25 season, the Club has undergone a significant period of growth and transformation, with the full-time
workforce increasing significantly. This expansion has included the creation of new roles, such as positions within Strategy
and Innovation, and the appointment of a dedicated Equality, Diversity and Inclusion Manager.
The Club also continues to publish its annual Gender Pay Gap Report, and in 2025 was successfully awarded Bronze
Level of the EFL Equality Code of Practice
Suppliers and supporters
The group is wholly invested in the local business community and intends to use local suppliers for procuring goods and
services where possible. The group has a good relationship with key suppliers which helps maintain the day to day
business activities.
The group recognises the supporters as the heartbeat of the Football Club. It is important to maintain an open dialogue
with supporters which has been achieved through; multiple “Open House” events as well as the re-introduction of official
supporter groups. This enables structured conversations with the senior leadership team at the Football Club.
Disabled Persons
Applications for employment by disabled persons are afways fully considered, bearing in mind the aptitudes of the applicant
concerned. In the event of members of staff becoming disabled, every effort is made to ensure that their employment within
the group continues and that the appropriate training is arranged. It is the policy of the group that the training, career
development and promotion of disabled persons should, as far as possible, be identical to that of other employees.

Financial instruments
The company's operations expose it to a variety of financial risks including the effects of changes in interest rates on debt,
credit risk and liquidity risk.
The company does not have material exposures in any of the areas identified above but can use derivative instruments to
manage these exposures.
The company's principal financial instruments comprise sterling cash and bank deposits and loans together with trade
receivables and trade payables that arise directly from its operations.
The main risks arising from the company's financial instruments can be analysed as follows:
Credit risk
The company's principal financial assets are bank balances, cash and trade receivables. The company's credit risk is
primarily attributable to its trade receivables. The amounts presented in the statement of financial position are net of
allowances for doubtful debts, estimated by the company's management based on prior experience and their assessment
of the current economic environment.
The company has no significant concentration of credit risk, with exposure spread over a large number of counterparties
and customers.
Liquidity risk .
The company's policy has been to ensure continuity of funding through operating within its banking facilities and operating
loan agreement with Knighthead.
Foreign currency risk
The company has some exposure in its trading operations to the risk of changes in foreign currency exchange rates relating
to future transfer instalments.

The club is committed to responsible energy management and will continue to practice and improve energy efficiency. The
club acknowledges climate change as a significant global threat and understands the role we have to play in reducing
green house gas emissions.
During the reporting period, the Club has undertaken a number of initiatives aimed at reducing its environmental impact
and supporting long-term carbon reduction objectives. A key achievement has been the introduction of a reusable cup
scheme. This initiative delivers a fully carbon-neutral solution, independently certified by Climate Partner. The system
operates using wash facilities powered entirely by renewable energy and designed for optimal water efficiency. All
polypropylene (PP) cups provided are certified as carbon-neutral products, enabling the Club to significantly reduce single-
use plastic waste and associated emissions.

Extent to which the audit was considered capable of detecting irregularities, including fraud
Irregularities, including fraud, are instances of non-compliance with laws and regulations. We design procedures in line
“with our responsibilities, outlined above and on the Financial Reporting Council's website, to detect material misstatements
in respect of irregularities, including fraud.
We obtain and update our understanding of the entity, its activities, its control environment, and likely future developments,
including in relation to the legal and regulatory framework applicable and how the entity is complying with that framework.
Based on this understanding, we identify and assess the risks of material misstatement of the financial statements, whether
due to fraud or error, design and perform audit procedures responsive to those risks, and obtain audit evidence that is
sufficient and appropriate to provide a basis for our opinion. This includes consideration of the risk of acts by the entity
that were contrary to applicable laws and regulations, including fraud.
In response to the risk of irregularities and non-compliance with laws and regulations, including fraud, we designed
procedures which included:
« Enquiry of management and those charged with governance around actual and potential litigation and claims as
well as actual, suspected and alleged fraud;
e Assessing the extent of compliance with the laws and regulations considered to have a direct material effect on
the financial statements or the operations of the company through enquiry and inspection;
« Reviewing financial statement disclosures and testing to supporting documentation to assess compliance with
applicable laws and regulations;
¢ Performing audit work over the risk of management bias and override of controls, including testing of journal
entries and other adjustments for appropriateness, evaluating the business rationale of significant transactions
outside the normal course of business and reviewing accounting estimates for indicators of potential bias.
Because of the inherent limitations of an audit, there is a risk that we will not detect all irregularities, including those leading

misstatement resulting from fraud is higher than for one resulting from error, as fraud may involve collusion, forgery,
