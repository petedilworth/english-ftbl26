First team
For the first time in seven years, Rovers encountered a mid-season managerial change, as John Eustace took over from
Jon Dah! Tomasson and successfully steered the club to Championship safety on a dramatic final day.
Rovers had talismanic top scorer Sammie Szmodics to thank, as the star striker ended the campaign as the
Championship’s leading marksman with 27 league goals, whilst also becoming the first Rovers’ player since Alan
Shearer to net over 30 goals in a single season. :
Having narrowly missed out on a play-off place on goal difference in the previous campaign, optimism was high heading
into Tomasson’s second season in charge at Ewood Park.
The squad was boosted with the early arrivals of free agents Arnor Sigurdsson, Sondre Tronstad and Niall Ennis, as well
as the loan signings of highly-rated young Premier League players Andy Moran and James Hill, from Brighton and
Bournemouth respectively.
Swedish goalkeeper Leo Wahlstedt and German striker Semir Telalovic were also signed on permanent deals, as Rovers
cast their transfer net into the European market-in search of value-for-money deals.
The departures of Ben Brereton Diaz and Bradley Dack at the end of the 2022-23 campaign left a guaranteed-goals void
in the squad, but one man more than stepped up to the mark.
Following an opening day triumph at home to West Bromwich Albion and progress through to the second round of the
Carabao Cup, Szmodics got his season up and running with a late brace in the 2-2 draw away to Rotherham United.
"He repeated the feat in the 2-1 home win over Middlesbrough, as Rovers celebrated the 5,000th league fixture in the
club’s history in style.
| Ryan Hedges netted Rovers’ Goal of the Season winner as we picked up our first away win of the season at Watford,
| before Tomasson’s team secured a record 8-0 away win at Harrogate Town, on a proud night for the Academy, with
i John Buckley (2), Jake Garrett, Zak Gilsenan and James Edmondson all on the scoresheet, whilst Patrick Gamble and
H Tom Atcheson also made their debuts.
Victory over Cardiff City in the third round of the Carabao Cup set up a mouth-watering tie against Chelsea at Stamford :
Bridge in November, whilst three straight league wins in October moved Rovers to within a point of the play-off places.
Three wins out of four in November ensured Rovers kept pace with the teams at the top, before a disastrous run of
results followed — just one win and eight defeats in the next 11 league games — culminated in Tomasson’s departure in
early February.
January had seen Szmoadics continue his fantastic form in front of goal, netting his first career hat-trick in the FA Cup
third round triumph over Cambridge United and taking his tally to 20 for the season with a brace against Wrexham in
the next round.
Rovers also recruited Connor O’Riordan from Crewe Alexandra, experienced duo Kyle McFadzean and John Fleck, as
well as the loan signings of Yasin Ayari from Brighton, Ben Chrisene fram Aston Villa and Billy Koumetio from Liverpool, -
whilst Adam Wharton joined Crystal Palace for a club record fee.
Eustace was unveiled on February 9th and Rovers celebrated the appointment with a 3-1 victory over Stoke City at
Ewood Park just 24 hours later. The former Birmingham boss immediately made Rovers harder to beat, picking up
valuable points away to Preston North End, Cardiff City and Middlesbrough, along with successive home draws against
Norwich City, Millwall and Plymouth Argyle.

Five clean sheets in the final nine fixtures underlined Rovers’ new resilience, with Szmodics scoring his 30th goal of the
campaign to seal a vital 1-0 victory away at Leeds United, before his final day double at newly-crowned champions ~ ,
Leicester City secured Rovers’ Championship status in front of a jubilant away end at the King Power Stadium.

Under-21s
Competing on five fronts for the first time in the team’s history, Rovers Under-21s enjoyed a strong second half to the
season to secure a place in the Premier League 2 play-offs.
Rovers ended the new-look league campaign in 15th position, which saw them paired with runners-up West Ham
United in the knockout stage, with the Hammers running out 4-1 winners at Rush Green.
Mike Sheron’s side juggled their PL2 commitments with fixtures in four different cup competitions throughout the
2023-24 season, competing in the Premier League Cup, Premier League International Cup, Lancashire Senior Cup and
the Central League Cup for the first time.
Zak Gilsenan enjoyed a purple patch in front of goal during the second half of the campaign, netting 11 goals in eight
Under-21s games, including braces against Sparta Prague, Sheffield United, Derby County and Aston Villa, as well as the
winner against former club Liverpool, prior to picking up an injury.
There were a number of memorabie moments throughout the season, including Harry Leonard’s hat-trick in a 7-1 win
against Preston North End, Igor Tyjon’s debut goal against PSV Eindhoven, aged just 15, Isaac Whitehall’s long-awaited
comeback after over two years out injured, a last-minute winner against Nice at Ewood Park and Leo Duru’s
development as a rampaging right-back, which saw him rewarded with a new long-term Rovers’ deal.
Academy
Rovers’ Under-28s lifted the Lancashire FA Youth Cup courtesy of a 3-0 victory over Bolton Wanderers in the final to :
! crown another successful season for the club’s Academy.
Further down the age levels, Rovers’ Under-16s reached the semi-finals of the Premier League Cup, whilst Rovers’
Under-13s reached the final of the National Cup North.
Academy starlet Rory Finneran wrote his name into the Rovers’ record books by officially becoming the youngest ever
player in the club’s history when the midfielder came on in the FA Cup third round victory over Cambridge United at
Ewood Park in January, aged just 15 years and 312 days. Finneran also captained Republic of Ireland U16s to glory in the
Victory Shield.
Earlier in the campaign, defender Tom Atcheson had become the fourth youngest player in the club’s history when he
made his debut in Rovers’ record 8-0 away win over Harrogate Town in the Carabao Cup, aged just 16 years and 343
days. Fellow scholar James Edmondson scored Rovers’ final goal in the game whilst also making his debut for the club.
Throughout the season, there were also first team debuts for Academy graduates Jake Batty, Patrick Gamble and Zak
Gilsenan, who scored in each of his first two appearances for the club in the Carabao Cup.
Goalkeeper Nick Michalski and striker igor Tyjon, who both featured in matchday squads for the first team in 2023-24
aged just 16, also received international recognition with England at youth level.
Women
Playing in the FA Women’s Championship for the fifth successive season, Rovers’ Women secured an impressive sixth- .
place finish under new head coach Simon Parker, breaking club records for the highest ever league finish, points won
and goals scored in the process.
An eight-game unbeaten run saw Rovers end the season in excellent form and with a tally of 36 points from their 22
league games, thanks to 11 wins and 25 goals scored.
Seven league matches were played at Ewood Park and an attendance record was also broken when 1,500 supporters
watched Rovers beat Southampton 2-1 in March, which coincided with Women’s Football Weekend.

EXECUTIVE REPORT

YEAR ENDED 30 JUNE 2024

On the pitch, the 2023-24 season saw another raft of changes within the football department and especially across the
Men’s senior team.

2023-24 was most definitely a season of two halves for the Men’s senior team, with a strong first half keeping us in and
around the play-off spots, only to see a dramatic fall in form from Christmas onwards that ultimately led to Head Coach
Jon Dahl Tomasson’s era coming to an end in early February 2024, having been in charge for 19 months.

Assistant Coach Remy Reijnierse and Performance Director Ben Rosen also left their respective roles within the senior
backroom coaching staff.

John Eustace came through a selection process for the new Head Coach, with Matt Gardiner and Keith Downing also
joining the club as Assistant Head Coaches.

Their remit was crystal clear and solely focused on maintaining Championship status come the end of the 2023- 24
season, especially with the future uplift in broadcasting fees for the 2024-25 season. They ultimately achieved this on
the final and very nerve-wracking last day of the season with a dramatic 2-0 away win at Leicester City thanks to both
goals coming from the prolific Sammie Szmodics.

With Championship status maintained there were further changes to the football staffing infrastructure with the
departures of Director of Football, Gregg Broughton, and Head of Recruitment, Sean Kimberley.

As stated above, the 2023-24 campaign ended with us narrowly avoiding relegation to the third tier of English football,
finishing in 19th place, just three points above Birmingham City on 53 points.

With 74 goals conceded, we were third worst in goals against, with only the bottom two clubs Huddersfield Town (79)

' and Rotherham United (89) conceding more. Whereas, in attack, we were 11th in the league, scoring a total of 60 goals,
with the exceptional Szmodics contributing 27 of them, which earned him both the Championship Golden Boot Award
plus a move over the summer to the Premier League with Ipswich Town.

In the domestic cup competitions we reached the last 16 of both the FA Cup and League Cup, going out 2-0 to Chelsea
at Stamford Bridge and, in the FA Cup, to Newcastle United in a memorable match at Ewood Park that ended in a 1-1
draw, only to see us narrowly lose 4-3 on penalties.

Overall, the first team squad had a very turbulent and inconsistent season, with the real standout performance being
that of Szmodics, with an overall goal tally of 33 keeping us both in the Championship and pushing us through to the
latter rounds of the cup competitions.

Throughout the 2023-24 season, the Under-21s remained under the experienced guidance of lead coach Mike Sheron,
assisted by Paul Butler, competing in the reconfigured Premier League 2 (PL2), plus four different cup competitions.
With the continued integration of the Under-21s with the senior first team squad, the acceleration of our developing
players saw them included in the first team planning and preparation for games. However, it was strongly felt that
there needed to be improvement in our loaning out of these young players to other clubs to give them the minutes
they required to get them ready for the challenges of playing in the relentless schedule of the Championship.

Whilst challenging for Mike and his coaching staff, this exposure of the loaning out for our emerging talent will without
doubt accelerate their progress by them playing up to a higher level than that of PL2.

2023-24 was another successful season for the club's Academy, with our Category One Academy programme
continuing to develop and produce talented young players aligned to the club’s philosophy. Player development was at
the forefront and is fundamental to the future growth of the club, so the focus was once again around creating the
opportunities and pathway to accelerate them into the first team as quickly as possible.

As at 30 June 2024, the company had net current liabilities of £142,522,425 and reported an operating loss, before
changes in intangible fixed assets, of £17,193,910 for the year. In common with many footbail clubs, the company may
continue to make operating losses and incur net cash outflows depending on a number of variables, including the
success of the team in league and cup competitions and the level of transfer activity. :
The company is funded through a bank overdraft facility and shareholder loans, and in view of the current financial
position, the company remains reliant on its ability to maintain existing and obtain additional funding as necessary.
In managing the finances of the company, the directors remain mindful of the need to ensure the company will comply
with the Championship Profitability and Sustainability rules.
As part of the directors’ assessment of going concern they have prepared detailed cash flow forecasts for the period to
the end of June 2027. These forecasts indicate that the company will require significant funding in addition to the
current facilities available to the company.
The amount of additional funding required wil! be dependent on the net proceeds of any player trading, on field
performance, and availability of bank facilities. In view of this the directors have received confirmation from the
ultimate parent company, Venkateshwara Hatcheries Private Limited (“VHPL”), that it has sufficient funds and is willing
to provide such additional financing as may be required to fund the company, to the extent necessary for the company
to continue to trade and to pay its liabilities as and when they become due, for the 12 manths following approval of
these accounts and thereafter for the foreseeable future even in the event of the bank facility not being renewed.
Further, the ultimate parent company, Venkateshwara Hatcheries Private Limited, has confirmed that it will not recall

: the amount outstanding of £133,972,098, included in creditors due in less than one year, within twelve months from
the date of approval of these financial statements.
The directors would like to bring to the attention of readers of these financial statements, of an ongoing legal matter
involving the ultimate parent company, Venkateshwara Hatcheries Private Limited and the Directorate of Enforcement
(“ED”) in India, whereby the ED have queried the application of funds by one of its subsidiaries, based in the UK, of

. funds remitted to them by VHPL. It is important to note that this does not include remittances made to The Blackburn
Rovers Football and Athletic Limited (“BRFC”).
The directors are pleased to confirm that the ED issued a No Objection Certificate, dated 12 March 2024, which
confirmed that VHPL has been granted permission to make a remittance of £15 million to its wholly owned subsidiary,
namely Venkys London Limited, the immediate parent of BRFC. This permission is subject to certain conditions which
have been met already or that will be met prior and subsequent to the remittance being made.
On receipt of the remittance by Venkys London Limited, that company will be able to remit those funds onwards to
BRFC without restriction or conditions imposed.
Based on the forecasts prepared, and including the above-mentioned available remittance, the directors are satisfied
that the company has sufficient available funds to enable it to meet its liabilities as they fall due for the foreseeable
future.
The directors have maintained liquidity through a combination of strong cashflow management and utilisation of player
sale proceeds. Furthermore, BRFC has remained compliant with all relevant statutory and football authority regulations

Principal risks and uncertainties
The board constantly assesses the threats to the business, by close monitoring of the football sector at its level, and via
communication with the company’s owners.

competitions it participates in. Business risks identified include the possibility of the football clubs relegation from the
league in which it competes. This would result in reduced income arising from uncertainty as to the progression of the
Company in the various competitions it participates in. During the year under review, the company has traded without
restriction under Championship Profit and Sustainability rules.

regulations of these bodies could have an impact on the company as they cover areas such as; competition format,

with all relevant rules and regulations, and monitors the impact of any potential changes.

smallest identifiable group of assets that generates income that is largely independent of the income streams
from other assets or groups of assets.

. value in use, the expected future cash flows are discounted to their present value using a pre-tax discount rate that
reflects current market assessments of the rate of return expected on an equally risky investment. For an asset that
does not generate largely independent income streams, the recoverable amount is determined for the income-
generating unit to which the asset belongs.

Financial assets that are measured at cost and amortised cost are assessed at the end of each reporting period for
objective evidence of impairment. If objective evidence of impairment is found, an impairment loss is recognised
in the profit and loss account.
For financial assets measured at cost less impairment, the impairment loss is measured as the difference between
an asset's carrying amount and best estimate, which is an approximation of the amount that the company would
receive for the asset if it were to be sold at the balance sheet date.
Leases
Assets acquired under finance leases are capitalised and the outstanding future lease obligations are shown in
creditors. Operating lease rentals are charged to the profit and loss account on a straight line basis over the
period of the lease.
Grants
Grants are accounted for under the accruals model as permitted by FRS 102.
Revenue grants are credited to profit and loss account in the same period in which the revenue expenditure to
which they relate is charged. Capital based grants are credited to revenue over the life of the asset to which they |
relate. The deferred element of grants is included as deferred income.

\ Freehold buildings 50 years
| Fixtures and fittings 10 years
1 Taxation
The charge for taxation is based on the loss for the year and takes into account taxation deferred because of
timing differences between the treatment of certain items for taxation and accounting purposes.
Deferred tax is recognised, without discounting, in respect of all timing differences between the treatment of
certain items for taxation and accounting purposes which have arisen but not reversed by the balance sheet date,
except as otherwise required by FRS 102.
A deferred tax asset is recognised only if it can be regarded as more likely than not that there will be suitable
taxable profits from which the future reversal of the underlying timing differences can be deducted.
Pensions
The company is making contributions in respect of its share of the deficit of the defined benefit section of the
Football League Pension and Life Assurance Scheme (the “Scheme”).

‘ Under the provisions of FRS 102 the Scheme is treated as a defined benefit multi-employer scheme. The financial
statements provide for the company's estimated share of the Football League Limited Pension and Life Assurance
deficit. The value provided is based on the current actuarial valuation dated 31 August 2023. Contributions are
made as advised by the scheme actuary.
