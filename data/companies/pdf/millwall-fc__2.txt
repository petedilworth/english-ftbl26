Strategic Report
Principal activities
The Company is the holding company of a group engaged primarily in the operation of a professional football club (Millwall or the Club), through its
subsidiary, The Millwall Football and Athletic Company (1985) Ltd (together the Group).
Business review
The campaign under review, 2023/24, was the club's seventh consecutive season in the Sky Bet Championship, which is the second tier of English football.
A turbulent season for The Lions ended on a high, with five straight League wins to finish | 3th in the table on 59 points, averaging 1.28 points per game.
This was nine points lower than the previous campaign tally and five League positions lower - the club's lowest finish since the 2018/19 season.
After nearly four years in charge, manager Gary Rowett departed the club in mid-October by mutual consent. Assistant manager, Adam Barrett, took
temporary charge of senior team affairs and was assisted by first team coach, Paul Robinson. Joe Edwards was installed as the new Head Coach on the 6th
November 2023 and in his first game, led the club to its highest away win in the Championship since 2001, a 4-0 win at Sheffield Wednesday. Just four wins
from 19 however forced the club to make a change, with Neil Harris returning as head coach, winning eight of the final 13 games to steer the club away
from relegation dangers.
Across the campaign a total of 27 players were used. This total was joint second lowest across the League, and highlights the club's tight controls around
budget and squad size in comparison to some of its competitors. The average age of the players used throughout the campaign was 26 years old.
There were a number of academy highlights to look back on fondly from the 2023/24 season. Having made his senior debut the previous year, academy
graduate Romain Esse featured 25 times in the League and was involved in the First Team squad 47 times across all competitions. Millwall's U21s retained
their Professional Development League title, the UI8s meanwhile, were crowed Professional Development League Cup champions and reached the semi-
finals of the FA Youth Cup.
The club is required to comply with the EFL’s Profit and Sustainability Rules which, in overview, permit clubs to incur losses up to £39m over a three-year
period before any restrictions on spending are applied, providing that losses in excess of £5m per year are funded through equity. In the season under
review, the club were once again well within these financial limits and the Board remains totally committed to complete compliance with the rules to ensure
the sustainability and health of the club for the benefit of all stakeholders.
Competitive imbalances within the Championship continue to grow each season as the financial strength of the Premier League, the division above, increases
year on year. Clubs relegated from the Premier League into the Championship arrive with parachute payments, which serve to distort on-pitch competition,
player salaries, the recruitment market, and more.
Discussions around an independent regulator continues and the club welcomes the reintroduction of The Football Governance Bill, initially introduced in
March 2024 but failed to pass through parliament before the general election was called in May. The club remains supportive both of calls for an Independent
Regulator of the sport and a redistribution of income from the Premier League to the EFL, providing it comes with enhanced regulation to help improve the
sustainability of clubs, .
A review of the Group's financing, property development and regeneration activities is provided later in this report.
John Berylson
In July 2023, John Berylson, the club Chairman tragically passed away following a car accident. John’s sudden and tragic passing unquestionably impacted all
those who were fortunate to have known him. Since first becoming involved in the club in 2006, he has presided over some of the greatest moments in
Millwall’s history, and his influence in providing the platform for those was immeasurable.
With John’s tragic passing, his son James Berylson was appointed Chairman of the club. James has been able to continue with his late father’s vision, and
ensured a continuation of the close relationship between the club and the Berylson family. ~

Strategic Report

Commercial Endeavours
The club would like to thank all its various sponsors and partners, at all levels, for their invaluable support throughout 2023/24.
A growth of overall commercial and matchday revenues, not just through sponsorships, but also through retail, hospitality, conferencing and events, and
ticketing, was of core importance to the club's overarching strategic direction.
The season launched with Fanatics as its new multichannel retail partner, providing a wider range of merchandise and products for supporters worldwide
to enjoy moving forward. By early August, the club had launched its three kits for the upcoming campaign alongside its new technical partner, Errea. Losses
of revenue receipts were incurred as a result of Huski, the club’s front-of-shirt sponsor, going into administration which resulted in the termination of the
partnership in May 2024.
The season saw the club record its highest average attendance in 70 years, averaging 16,544, The jump in numbers is aided by record breaking season ticket
sales ahead of the 2023/24 season — almost 9,000 - as well as an increase in fans attending on a weekly basis as opposed to picking and choosing fixtures.
Millwall’s international audience continues to grow healthily, too. A staggering 11,000 unique overseas supporters visited SE16 for at least one match last
season — a 63% increase on 2022/23's total. The club proudly welcomed 1,764 fans from abroad for Millwall's home fixcure win against Norwich City on
29th December 2023; a record by some margin for a single game at The Den.
Supporters flocked from all around the globe to get a flavour of Millwall Football Club, with fans travelling from across 70 different nationalities and territories,
including some more remote places such as the islands of Aruba and Réunion. German Lions made up almost |5% of our international audience, with |,351
supporters visiting last season. Over |,000 Americans came to cheer on the team, closely followed by Zian Flemming’s countrymen from the Netherlands.
Italy and Sweden made up the rest of the club's top five overseas supporting fanbases — as well as all Nordic countries; Denmark, Finland, Norway and
Sweden taking up a large portion of the overseas following.
Records were also set domestically. Just over 7,000 UK-based supporters visited The Den for the first time in 2023/24.
Giving the club's location close to the heart of the City of London, che competitive landscape around conferencing and events continues to be a challenging
one. While business continues to improve, there remains significant potential and room for growth in this area for the club.
Finance
The Company continues to be funded by its principal shareholder, Chestnut Hill Ventures LLC (“CHV”) and by its Directors. The existing facility is £20m
(CHV Facility) with £10m outstanding at the year end. Additional funding requirements during the year have been met by CHV and certain Directors
subscribing for new ordinary shares of £10,000 each at par. During the year 1,938 ordinary shares were issued for cash raising £19.38m of funds for the
Company.
The CHV Facility and the £525,000 2013 Unsecured Loan Notes (Loan Notes), held by Directors and their associates, were repayable on | July 2024. CHV
and the Loan Notes holders had agreed that no interest would accrue in respect of the CHV Facility and Loan Notes until 30 June 2024. By an agreement
dated 30 June 2024, CHV extended the suspension of interest until | July 2025 (when interest will begin to accrue again unless CHV agrees otherwise). The
other Loan Notes holders have agreed that no interest will accrue on the Loan Notes until the date on which the Company and CHV agree that interest
will begin to accrue again in respect of the CHV Facility. The repayment date of the CHV Facility and other Loan Notes was also extended to | July 2026.
The Directors are of the view that, together with the further arrangements referred to in note | to the financial statements, ongoing equity investment and
extended loan facilities from CHV and the Directors, provides the Company with sufficient access to working capital to fund the operations of the Company
over the next few years.
Regeneration
As reported in the previous year, the club's improved relations with Lewisham Council and Renewal, the property developer for the adjacent site, continued
during the year with further progressive talks held with the Mayor, Damien Egan and his replacement Brenda Dacres as well as with senior planning officers
from Lewisham.
In May 2024 the club and Lewisham Council agreed the key terms of a 999-year lease for the land surrounding The Den, which will give the club development
rights as part of plans designed to benefit the club, its supporters, the Council itself as well as the local community generally.
At the time of writing, the full lease agreement is being finalised and is expected to be signed in early 2025. The club expresses its gratitude to the Council
and its senior officers for their approach to these discussions.
Training Ground
As reported in the previous year, in October 2022, Sevenoaks Council's planning committee formally approved the club's plans for a new training ground
ona 100-acre site in West Kingsdown, Kent. This new location is designed to bring together the First Team and Academy, and their respective support
and administration staff, rather than have them operate on separate sites as it currently the case. This brings about varying logistical challenges which a
new location would serve to reduce.
During 2023/24 the club has continued to look at cost analysis and tendering processes as part of the project development costs. With the significant
increases in cost of building materials and labour, this has impacted on initial project plans and has impacted on the short-term completion goals initially
set. Progress is continuing on the planning, however at present no lead contractor has been appointed.
To give the club certainty over its training facilities, discussions have commenced regarding the leasehold site currently occupied as the First Team training
ground, and the club are currently exploring the opportunity to purchase the freehold to this site, which will allow for further short-term development
and infrastructure improvements, as well as consideration around a longer term London base as part of its academy footprint.

Community Trust
Millwall Community Trust (MCT) continues to work proudly across Lewisham and Southwark, which they have done since 1985, to support the local
community. The 2023/24 season saw the Millwall Community Trust provide 8,000 free meals whilst also providing the same number of free places on its
holiday camps, whilst working with over 12,000 people in the local community
The club’s commitment to a thriving and ultimately successful Community Trust is reflected by having two of its Directors and two members of senior
management on the Board of Trustees. The strength and depth of the relationship is also demonstrated by financial support and by making club facilities,
resources, and assets available for their benefit, including the arranging of first team player visits to different programmes across the season.
Millwall's Military Veteran Football Programme in partnership with Military Veteran Football Club was set up in April 2024 and has grown from strength to
strength, After completing Tommy's D-Day Trek for Royal British Legion Industries (RBLI) - raising over £4,000 the programme then competed at this
year's Royal British Legion EFL Cup, beating Fleetwood Town Military Veterans in the final 4-0. Millwall Romans and Pride - Millwall’s LGBT sides -
continues to grow as well, with over 60 team members across the two sides, with four league titles to their name.
Club Restructure
In May 2024 following an internal review by the board, a number of changes were announced both to the structure of the club but also to some of the key
personne! with Alex Aldridge, Billy Taylor and Steve Kavanagh leaving the club. In line with modern football practices the key operations have been split
between the football operations and non-football. This restructure is linked to a clear vision around the long-term strategy, with a focus on development
of footballing talent and a self-sustaining reinvestment model, as well as business growth, diversification and improved efficiency.
The club thanks Steve, Billy and Alex for all their hard work and commitment to the club over their tenure and wishes them well for the future.
Matija Sarkic
On the [5th June 2024, the club was left devastated by the sudden passing of its goalkeeper, Matija Sarkic, at the age of 26 years old.
Matija signed for the club in August 2023, making his debut in The Lions’ League opener away at Middlesbrough in a | -O win. He would go on to make 33
appearances in total in the 2023/24 campaign, and proudly represented his national Montenegro on an international stage.
The Montenegrin’s crucial save at St Mary's against Southampton in Neil Harris’ first game back in charge of the club helped the club to a vital three points,
whilst Sarkic's penalty save at Swansea City on the final day of the season will live long in the memory.
The goalkeeper's fast fixture was for Montenegro on 5th June against Belgium in an international friendly.
Always a happy character, Matija’s smile, and personality will be sorely missed by all at Millwall Football Club.
Principal risks and uncertainties
In common with many football clubs outside the FA Premier League the main business risk is the maintenance of a positive cash flow, bearing in mind the
uncertainty of turnover and the high cost of maintaining a playing squad on which the success of the Group's business is largely dependent. In order to
achieve a positive cash flow there is the constant requirement to raise new finance and refinance existing facilities which, in turn, requires the continuing
support of existing providers of chose facilities.
A significant amount of the Club's revenue derives from ticket sales. Income generated from gate receipts is highly dependent on the level of attendance at
matches. The COVID-19 pandemic has highlighted the reliance of all football clubs on matchday revenue, and also cost control measures can and have been
implemented, these cannot mitigate the lost income seen at present.
Some income streams of the Club (such as television rights and related income) are dependent on third party contracts and arrangements to which the _ _
Club is not.a party and over which the Club can exercise no or little influence. - 7
As part of its normal activities, the Club deals in the trading of player registrations and there is always a risk of significant and lasting injuries to players that
may impair player values. Players aged 24 years or older are free to move between clubs once their contract has come to an end and Directors monitor
expiry dates carefully with a view to renewing contracts or realising value.
Relevant business risks are discussed during Board meetings so that, where a materia! exposure is identified, mitigating action can be taken.
Details of the Group's exposure to financial risks and the management procedures in place to mitigate these are set out in note 16 to these financial
statements.

Going concern
in assessing the appropriateness of the going concern assumption, the Directors have produced detailed cash flow forecasts until 30 June 2026.
The Director's have continued to undertake a range of measures to ensure a prudent running of the organisation. There is a £20m facility in place from
CHV that was drawn to £10m as at the year end, which does not fall due for repayment until | July 2026. The Directors believe this, in conjunction with
the ongoing support of the Company's principal shareholder, provides sufficient working capital for the current needs of the Group and Company for the
foreseeable future. .
However, forecasts prepared for and reviewed by the board demonstrate that in most reasonable scenarios additional shareholder funding will be required
which is not yet guaranteed. As such, the directors have identified a material uncertainty that may cast significant doubt over the Company's and the Group's
ability to continue as a going concern for the foreseeable future.
As reported last year, the UK Government has been progressing with the Football Governance Bill, which is currently going through the parliamentary
review process. This regulation includes the establishment of an independent regulator for football as well as looking at the engagement with supporter
groups through the establishment of a shadow board and a review of the uneven distribution system through the English football pyramid. As a Club the
Directors support the recommendations made in the Government review and welcome the progress that has been made to date on the Football Governance
Bill, and are actively taking steps to adopt some of the measures recommended in advance of any structural change within the football environment.

Based on our risk assessment, we considered the areas most susceptible to fraud to be management override of controls, improper revenue recognition
and management bias in accounting estimates.
Our procedures in respect of the above included:
* Testing a sample of journal entries throughout the year, which met a defined risk criteria, by agreeing to supporting documentation;

+ Selecting a sample of commercial income balance and agreeing to supporting contracts; and
+ Comparing the list of commercial sponsors on the club website to the commercial income received in the year to confirm completeness.
We also communicated relevant identified laws and regulations and potential fraud risks to all engagement team members who were all deemed to have

Our audit procedures were designed to respond to risks of material misstatement in the financial statements, recognising that the risk of not detecting a
material misstatement due to fraud is higher than the risk of not detecting one resulting from error, as fraud may involve deliberate concealment by, for
example, forgery, misrepresentations or through collusion. There are inherent limitations in the audit procedures performed and the further removed non-
compliance with laws and regulations is from the events and transactions reflected in the financial statements, the less likely we are to become aware of it.
