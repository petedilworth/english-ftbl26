From a sporting perspective, the 2023/24 season was successful with the first team bouncing back from its a,
. relegation to the EFL Championship in the 2022/23 season with a 4th place finish in the EFL Championship, gaining
: : promotion to the Premier League via the play-offs and a win against Leeds United, following amemorable home

In the Cup competitions, the first team was knocked out of the EFL Cup by Gillingham in the first round (2022/23: :

_ Russell Martin was hired as the first team manager ahead of the 2023/24 EFL Championship season and

At the time of writing the first team’s 2024/25 Premier League performances have seen the Club in the bottom three
positions of the league and battling to avoid relegation. The Club has taken the difficult decision to part ways with
"Russell Martin due to the Club’s league position and is actively recruiting his replacement. .

League 2 Division, whilst holding a strong position in the Premier League International Cup (2nd place) ard the
Premier League Cup (1st place). . : ,

The first team finished in 20" place in the Premier League in the 2022/23 season, which led to the relegation to the
_ EFL Championship for the 2023/24 season. Consequently, as a result of that relegation, the Club’s turnover
decreased by £61.5m (42.3%) to £84.0m inthe year.:
_ This decrease in turnover is largely explained by the reduction in broadcasting revenue, which is significantly lower
in the EFL Championship than the Premier League. The broadcasting revenue recognised in the year is primarily
made up of parachute payments received from the Premier League, which enables a staggered approach for newly-
: relegated clubs to mitigate the impact. of the significant discrepancy between the Premier League and EFL :
broadcasting revenue. Consequently, broadcasting revenue decreased from £108.0m to £55.9m, a decrease of
48.2%, , _
. Match day revenue decreased by 15.8% from £19.1m to £16.1m, primarily due to less successful first team cup
. runs during the 2023/24 season: A positive season ticket campaign, which included an average reduction of prices .
by 10%, contributed to strong sales of general admission season tickets, whilst match day tickets also sold very
- well, contributing to multiple sold-out fixtures during the season in spite of relegation. When combined with a greater
. number of home league matches being played in the Championship, and the home playoff semi-final, the Club was
able to partially offset the reduction in revenue generated from cup matches, whilst ensuring that the EFL
, Championship ticket pricing was affordable for our fans. oo : oO
: - Commercial revenue fell by £5.5m from £15.4m to £9.9m (a 36.1% reduction), due to a drop in the Club's ,
partnerships revenue, most notably the amount.received from the Club’s main sponsor, which was offset by the ,
Club's decision to in-house retail merchandise sales and non-match day conference and events. Despite our drive
; ; to increase commercial revenues within the Group, it‘is difficult to offset the substantial decrease in broadcasting
. revenue between the Premier League and the EFL Championship. : , :
. : Other income decreased marginally from £3.0m to £2.2m which largely relates to grants received to support our
: EPPP Category 1 status academy and license and merit payments for our girls and women’s football programme.
: In 2023/24, the Club generated a profit before tax, primarily due to successful player trading, which generated total
profit on the disposals of players of £123.0m (2022/23: £7.4m). Profit was generated on the sales of Diallo, Olaigbe, :
Salisu, Livramento, Lavia, Ward-Prowse, Tella, Djenepo, Doyle, Finnigan and Perraud, together with contingent
transfer receipts in relation to previously transferred players crystallising during the season. This compares .
- favourably to the previous financial year, when profit on the disposal of players was generated from the sales of ~
-Romeu, Valery, Jay-Morgan, Nlundulu and Orsic, together with contingent transfer receipts similarly crystallised. .
, The amortisation of player registrations of £51.1m has decreased marginally in comparison to last year’s charge of
. £52.0m as a result of player sales during the 2023/24 season.
, The Club's cash balance at the Statement of Financial Position date was £13.1m (2023: £13.2m) and amounts
owed to group undertakings decreased from £154.8m to £122.5m. No amounts were paid to the Company’s
directors or ownership by way of dividends. . , ae .

- expectation that the Club will continue in operational existence for the foreseeable future. The directors

. current liabilities of £151,814,000 and net liabilities of £68,373,000. -

- detailed cash flow forecasts which extend to no less than June 2026. The Club's forecasts take into
‘ : consideration various scenarios, including the risk of the Club being relegated from the Premier League.
: _In the event of relegation, the Club, as with such clubs; will incur a significant reduction in turnover as
, _ Premier League broadcasting revenue is replaced with parachute payments. The Club will be required
to take steps to reduce costs and borrowings to a level more sustainable for a Championship club. In
. this scenario, the Club would expect a significant reduction in wages and salaries, which will be largely
. . achieved by contractual means existing in player and employee contracts and player sales.
, ~The Club has forecast a net inflow of cash from player trading, as is common for many clubs relegated
from the Premier League and was the case when the Club was relegated at the end of the 2022/23
season. In such a case, to support the Club’s obligations, the directors will consider and utilise financing
, - options available, including but not limited to, player receivable financing. :

- satisfied-that sufficient funds can be generated or otherwise obtained by the Club, if this was absolutely
: necessary. ; a _

which has helped to reduce the forecasted cashflow deficit. Some of the cash flows from these sales,

oo. transactions. : ;
