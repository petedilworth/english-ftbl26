Chairman's statement
For the Year Ended 31 May 2025
On behalf of myself, my business partners at Trivela Group, and the rest of the Board of Directors, | am pleased to
present the Annual Report and Accounts for the year ended 31 May 2025.
The headline result is a loss of £1.968 million although this again includes a significant amount of depreciation, primarily
related to the acquisition of the Pallet-Track Bescot Stadium, and non-cash impacting interest accruals on monies
injected by Trivela Group UK LLC. As such, a more appropriate measure of performance would be our operating
result/EBITDA of (£878,000) which was a marginal improvement on the previous year.
As | articulated last year, with Trivela Group UK owning the majority of the equity of the Football Club, the use of debt
injection to make these investments (as opposed to equity) is merely a function of simplicity and practicality.
Throughout the year we continued our strategy of investing in the Football Department, in facilities, in supporter
experience and in personnel, whilst also navigating the challenging external economic conditions faced by ail businesses
operating in the UK. All of this also set against the backdrop of significant cost inflation in English football.
Moving forward, one of our key objectives is to run the Club in a sustainable and responsible way and | am very
confident that our strategic investments have grown our capacity to generate additional revenue and deliver cost
savings in the short, medium and long term.
The 2024-25 season saw the Men’s First Team finish 4th in League Two, narrowly missing out on promotion after defeat
in the Play Off Final at Wembley Stadium. The team achieved a good run to the EFL Cup 3rd Round, beating higher
division opposition in rounds 1 & 2, before lasing out on penalties to Premier League Leicester City in the 3rd round at a
raucous Bescot Stadium. There were also credible performances in both the FA Cup and EFL Trophy. Attendances at
the Bescot Stadium were outstanding, with a home league average of 6,317 across the season. By our reckoning, this
was the highest figure in over 20 years - with an average of 5,667 Walsall fans at those fixtures, beating our targeted
growth for the year which was set at 5,500 from the outset. The Women’s First Team finished 2nd in the league, and
had strong cup runs in atl competitions, reaching the final of the League Cup but narrowly missing out on lifting the
trophy. This showed a marked improvement on prior years’ performance. We continue to develop our support of
women's & girls’ football, integrating this within the Club, and believe that we will see the benefits grow over the coming
years. Our Academy had a positive year of growth and development once again on and off the pitch. A number of
Academy player sales occurring during the 2024-25 season, enabling us to reinvest in the provision. Academy scholar
Jenson Kilroy signed his first professional contract at the end of the season, and we are confident that we will see the
rewards of the efforts of all within the Academy over the years to come in terms of players coming through the system.
Off the pitch, we saw a strong year of growth in a number of departments across the Club, including retail, ticketing,
conference & events and the Locker. Season Ticket sales grew year on year, as did the number of supporters buying
Club Memberships. Matchday ticket income, and food & beverage sales, were excellent - due in part to the highest
average home attendances in two decades. The Locker developed into a key part of the matchday experience for our
supporters, in turn providing valuable income to the Club. Kit sales were at the highest level in memory, thanks to the
excellent design. Investment in our marketing & media department proved fruitful, with innovative content developed
and delivered to bring our supporters closer to the Club.
As seen across the football industry, and wider business climate in the UK generally, cost controls proved an ongoing
challenge, with inflation leading to significantly higher cost of sales over the year. This was experienced in all areas of
the Club and business, including but not limited to player wages, staff wages, food and beverage purchases, utility/rates
bills as well as general day to day running expenses.

2. Accounting policies
2.1 Basis of preparation of financial statements
The financial statements have been prepared under the historical cost convention unless otherwise
specified within these accounting policies and in accordance with Financial Reporting Standard 102, the
Financial Reporting Standard applicable in the UK and the Republic of Ireland and the Companies Act 2006.
The preparation of financial statements in compliance with FRS 102 requires the use of certain critical
accounting estimates. It also requires management to exercise judgement in applying the company’s
accounting policies (see note 3).
The following principal accounting policies have been applied:
2.2 Going concern
The company is currently reporting net current liabilities of £2187m (2024 - £1.814m). The directors have
prepared cash flow forecasts which show that the club is able to meet its debts as they fall due in the
foreseeable future. The company has also obtained support from their ultimate parent company
confirming their intention to support the company for a period of 12 months from the date of these
accounts, On the basis of this support and the cash flow forecasts the financial statements have been
prepared on the going concern basis.
2.3. Revenue
Turnover represents all income arising from the activities of the company excluding Value Added Tax.
Included within turnover are player transfer fees, Football League receipts, net gate receipts, .
merchandising/retail receipts, sponsorship and advertising receipts, conferencing and events receipts and
other income.
Football League income is recognised in full in the season to which it relates.
Season tickets and seasonal hospitality income is recognised over the season to which they relate and
released over the home matches played. ,
Sponsorship and advertising income is recognised over the duration of the contract. Dependent on the
terms of the contract this can be spread on a match to match or straight line basis.
Net gate receipts, including match day hospitality receipts, are recognised on the relevant match date.
Conference and event income is recognised on the date of the event.
Retail income is recognised at the point of sale.

2.5 Tangible fixed assets

the company. The carrying amount of the replaced part is derecognised. Repairs and maintenance are

assets that have been previously impaired are reviewed at each balance sheet date to assess whether
