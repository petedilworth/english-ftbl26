The principal activity of the company is that of a football club (Southampton Football Club Limited (the “Club”))
whose men’s team participates in the English Football League Championship ("EFL Championship’).
Football Overview
From a sporting perspective, the 2024/25 season was a disappointing one for the first team, which culminated in
relegation from the Premier League to the EFL Championship, with the team finishing a disappointing 20" place in
the Premier League (2023/24: 4" place finish in EFL Championship and promotion to the Premier League via play-
Offs).
In the Cup competitions, the first team was knocked out of the EFL Cup by Liverpool in the quarter-finals (2023/24:
first round). The Club reached the fourth round of the FA Cup (2023/24: fifth round) before losing to Burnley.
In addition, during the year, the Club parted ways with two first team managers and associated support staff
including Russell Martin in December 2024 and his replacement, Ivan Juric, in April 2025 with Simon Rusk taking
over as interim first team manager for the remainder of the season.
Prior to the commencement of the 2025/26 season, Will Still was appointed as the Club’s first team manager and
at the time of writing, due to the difficult start to the season which has seen the Club placed 17" in the league, the
Club parted ways with Will Stili and some of his support staff. Tonda Eckert, the U21 team’s head coach, is currently
the interim first team manager. The Club also reached the third round of the EFL Cup before being knocked out by
Liverpool for a second successive season.
The U21 team finished in a strong 7' position in the U21 Premier League 2 in the 2024/25 season, eventually
qualifying for, but unfortunately losing, the playoff final match against Manchester City to miss out on winning the
league (2023/24: 17" place). At the time of writing, the U21 team has made a good start to the 2025/26 Premier
League 2 season and is currently 5" out of 29 teams. The U21 team played their first fixture in the Premier League
International Cup against the U21 Real Madrid team which drew in an impressive crowd of almost 11,000 to St
Mary's. In 2023/24, the U21 team finished 7" place in their group in the Premier League International Cup. The
team’s start in the group stages of the English National League Cup has been challenging (2023/24: finished 7® in
their group).
The U18 team season finished a very reputable 2" in the U18 Premier League South in 2024/25 season (2023/24:
10), At the time of writing, the U18 team has enjoyed a good start to the 2025/26 U18 Premier League season
and is currently 3° in the league. They also performed well in the FA Youth Cup during the season teaching the
quarter finals, before being knocked out in the quarter finals by Watford (2023/24: 3" round).

Financial Overview (continued)
The first team was promoted to the Premier League at the end of the 2023/24 season off the back of a 4th place
finish and play-off final win against Leeds United. Disappointingly, the 2024/25 season saw the first team finish in
20th place in the Premier League, which led to relegation back to the EFL Championship for the 2025/26 season.
The results of the financial year ended 30 June 2025 represent the season in which the Club participated in the
Premier League and the comparative figures are those of the 2023/24 season in the EFL Championship.
Consequently, as a result of promotion, the Club’s turnover increased by £73.5m from £84.0m to £157.5m in the
year.
This increase in turnover compared to the 2023/24 season is largely explained by broadcasting revenue, which is
significantly higher in the Premier League than the EFL Championship. Consequently, broadcasting revenue
increased from £55.9m to £114.7m, an increase of 105.2%. When compared to the 2022/23 Premier League
season, broadcasting revenue increased by 6.2% from £108.0m.
Match day revenue increased by 22.4% from £16.1m in the 2023/24 season to £19.7m in 2024/25. Compared to
when the Club was competing in the Premier League during the 2022/23 season, match day revenue has increased

: by 3.1% from £19.1m. 2024/25saw an incredibly successful sold-out season ticket campaign and an increase in
revenue across both general admission and hospitality season tickets and match day tickets.
Commercial revenue increased by 110.1% from £9.9m in 2023/24 to £20.8m in 2024/25, with the most significant
increase attributable to commercial partnerships, primarily being the amount received from the Club’s main
sponsor, In comparison, commercial revenue for the 2022/23 season in the Premier League was £15.4m,
highlighting a significant increase of 35.1%. The Club is committed to driving commercial revenues in order to
mitigate the significant discrepancy between the Premier League and EFL Championship broadcasting revenue.
Further information on ongoing commercial revenue projects can be found in the “Infrastructure and People” section
of this report.
Other revenue increased marginally from £2.2m to £2.4m. Other revenue largely relates to grants received to
support our Category 1 status academy.
Operating loss before player trading has significantly improved by 73.6% from an operating loss of £38.1m in
2023/24 to £10.0m in 2024/25. Further compared to the operating loss before player trading in the 2022/23 Premier
League season, this still represents a 59.2% improvement. There are several factors which have contributed to this
improvement, primarily being tight cost management — in particular regarding wages — and the investment into our
commercial activities.
In 2024/25, the Club has generated a loss before tax of £45.3m with a total loss on player trading of £23.8m
contributing to over 52.5% of the total comprehensive loss before tax. The loss on player trading is driven by the
player amortisation charge for the year exceeding the profits generated from the sale of players such as Alcaraz,
Mara, Lyanco, Caleta-Car. As a result of promotion to the Premier League at the start of the 2024/25 season,
significant investment was made into the playing squad with the purchases of Harwood-Bellis, Ramsdale, Downes,
Archer, Fernandes, Brereton-Diaz, Sugawara, Kayi Sanda, Wood, Edwards and Juan.
When comparing to the 2023/24 season, when the Club had just been relegated, the focus of player trading was
more on profit-generating sales in order to counteract the significant drop in broadcasting revenue from the Premier
League to the EFL Championship. This resulted in a comparable total profit on player trading of £74.8m driven by
the sales of Diallo Salisu, Livramento, Lavia, Ward-Prowse, Tella, Djenepo and Perraud amongst others, together
with contingent transfer receipts in relation to previously transferred players crystallising during the season.
The Club's cash balance at the Statement of Financial Position date was £9.3m (2024: £13.1m). No amounts were
paid to the Club's directors or ownership by way of dividends. .

Infrastructure and People
The Club continued its programme of capital investment at St Mary’s Stadium during the year, with several
infrastructure projects reaching completion. These included the installation of perimeter fencing and one of the
largest safe standing areas in the UK and upgrades to the press deck, each of which had been initiated in the
previous financial year.
Additional developments included the conversion of the former ticket office into The Dell, a pub named in tribute to
the Club’s former home ground, and the construction of the Northam Fan Zone. This new space was designed to
enhance the matchday experience and provide a flexible venue for community and private events.
The Club also introduced the Knightwood Lounge, a pitch-facing hospitality suite inspired by the local New Forest
area, offering a more contemporary environment for matchday guests and expanding the stadium’s non-matchday
venue hire capabilities.
At year-end, capital expenditure continued with the redevelopment of the South Stand into a mixed-use
entertainment venue. Having just opened in October 2025, LEVEL1 provides a range of leisure activities and
contribute to the Club’s aim of positioning St Mary’s as a year-round destination for fans and the wider community.
These capital investments reflect the Club's commitment to diversifying revenue streams, reducing reliance on
broadcasting income, and strengthening the Club’s role as a community-focused venue.
The average number of employees during the year was 347 (2024: 342) and the Club ended the season with 353
FTE employees including our players and scholars; the increase to support the wider commercial activities at the
stadium.
There were several organisational changes in the 2024/25 season, most notably being the resignation of a board
director and the appointment of the group technical director and the managerial changes in respect of the first team.

Infrastructure and People (continued)
The Club continues to provide active promotion to Saints Foundation, the Club’s affiliated charity, and its needs-
ied work in the community. During this financial year the Club has again provided financial donations to the charity,
and in-kind support for the charity through the access to office space, utilities, and staff expertise. Additionally,
Saints Foundation has continued to benefit from access to rooms and facilities at St Mary’s Stadium which enable
the charity to run activity for the community at an inspirational venue.
A real strength this year has been the support of Club staff for Saints Foundation’s activities, including staff raising
funds through challenge events and providing engaging opportunities for Saints Foundation’s beneficiaries through
unique experiences at St Mary’s Stadium. This contributed to the charity’s record year in the delivery of its strategy
to support those people most impacted by inequality in Southampton. Through its 15 projects, 6,511 participants
were engaged, with a record number of 5.545 achieving a positive outcome, such as increasing school attendance,
improved health behaviour changes, reducing offending behaviours, or finding employment or training. The
combination of these factors helped the charity to increase its social value in 2024/25, with over £10m of social
value delivered for the benefit of the Southampton Community.
Fan engagement statement
At Southampton Football Club, our fans are at the heart of everything we do. We are committed to building a strong,
inclusive, and lasting connection with our supporters, ensuring that every voice matters so that every supporter
feels valued and able to be consulted in relation to certain key decisions made by the Club. We have a very
dedicated and loyal fan base, who follow us passionately at home and away. We want to recognise the invaluable
contribution and help protect the Club that they care so deeply about through our commitment to a clear supporter
engagement strategy.
Through open communication, regular supporter forums, surveys, and dedicated fan liaison channels, including
our own Supporter Relations team, we actively listen to feedback and work together to enhance the matchday
experience while deepening the connection between the Club and the communities around us. In partnership with
the Saints Foundation, the Club further strengthens this bond through a wide range of diverse programmes and
initiatives.
In order to be able to reinvest into football, we need to grow revenue and profits across our commercial areas. To
achieve this, we continue to invest in and around the stadium with the introduction of new experiences and facilities
such as LEVEL1 and The Dell. These aim to further enhancing the experience that our fans have on matchdays,
and other visitors can experience on non-matchdays. In addition to this work the Club is proud to be part of the
engagement with the City Council’s masterplan for the longer-term redevelopment of Southampton. We see these
kinds of initiatives as being a vitally important part of making the Club and Saints Foundation fully sustainable within
the city for the long term.
We actively promote accessibility, family-friendly initiatives, and opportunities for fans of all ages and backgrounds
to engage with the Club both at St Mary’s Stadium and beyond. By supporting local communities and fan groups
and investing in grassroots football, and fostering pride in the Saints’ identity, we aim to create a culture of belonging
where every supporter feels part of the journey. This is evident in our work with Rainbow Saints, and Southampton
Pride, Southampton Disabled Supporters Association and our Fan Advisory Board.
The Saints Fan Advisory Board meets with the CEO and other board members every quarter, while the Chair also
holds monthly meetings with a board representative to ensure the voice of our fans is consistently heard and
represented. The Club is committed to ensuring that the Fan Advisory Board will be consulted on any decision-
making that relates to protected, legacy assets such as our stadium, our Club colours and our presence in the city
of Southampton. To enable a broad cross-section of our supporters to be directly represented, we have
implemented a policy of rotation for the appointments to the Fan Advisory Board and the first set of rotations will
occur in the 2025/26 season. Recruitment to the Fan Advisory Board will be completed by the Club and the Fan
Advisory Board to ensure a transparent, fair and ongoing equal representation of the different interests that exist
across our fanbase.

Principal risks & uncertainties

Team performance risk

As is common with many professional football clubs, a principal risk is the performance of the men’s first team and
the league in which it operates. The Club manages the impact of this through close control and detailed forecasting
of its direct costs and cash outgoings, relative to forecast revenue and cash incomings.

Liquidity risk .
The Club reported a net cash position of £9.3m at the Statement of Financial Position date (2024: £13.1m).

Credit risk

This relates primarily to trade debtors from commercial activities and player trading and the Club monitors this risk
closely with the aim of minimising it at all times.

Political risk

The main political risks that are monitored by the Club are around the impact on the price of goods, services and
utilities, in part due to the Russia/Ukraine conflict, and the consequently high levels of inflation within the UK, which
it manages through close cost contro! and a detailed tendering and procurement process. The Russia/Ukraine
conflict also impacts the Club with regards to potential restrictions on opportunities in the transfer market, which is
monitored closely.

Furthermore, the movement of the interest rates within the UK over the course of the year is another risk closely
monitored, which the Club manages through detailed forecasting of the net and gross debt requirements, close
monitoring of working capital requirements within the Club and maximising its utilisation of interest-bearing
accounts.

The directors have concluded, after reviewing the work performed and detailed above, that they can adopt the
going concern basis in preparing these financial statements.

2.3 Going concern
These financial statements are prepared on a going concern basis. The directors have a reasonable
expectation that the Club will continue in operational existence for the foreseeable future. The directors
are aware of certain uncertainties which could cause doubt on the Club’s ability to continue as a going
concern if they were to arise and remain unremedied.
For the year ended 30 June 2025, the company made a loss after tax of £44,793,000 and has net
current liabilities of £206,752,000 and net liabilities of £113,166,000.
In assessing the appropriateness of the going concern assumption, the directors have produced
detailed cash flow forecasts which extend to no less than November 2026. The Club’s forecasts take
into consideration various scenarios, including the risk of the Club staying in the EFL Championship for
: another season.
In the event that the Club is not promoted to the Premier League, the Club will incur a further reduction
in turnover as the amount of parachute payments received will reduce in accordance with Premier
League regulations for season 2026/27. The Club will be required to take steps to reduce costs and
borrowings to a more sustainable level. In this scenario, the Club would expect a reduction in wages
and salaries, which will be largely achieved by contractual means existing in player and employee
contracts and player sales or loans.
The Club has forecast a net inflow of cash from player trading over the period of assessment. In such
a case, to support the Club's obligations, the directors will consider and utilise financing options
available, including but not limited to, player receivable financing.
In the event that the Club's financial performance varies or is less than that modelled, the directors are
satisfied that sufficient funds can be generated or otherwise obtained by the Club, if this was absolutely
necessary.
During previous transfer windows, the Club have realised significant value from the sale of players,
which has helped in the past to reduce the forecasted cashflow deficit and is a lever the Club can pull
if necessary to remedy any cash flow shortfalls. Some of the cash flows from these sales, purchases
and loan deals are immediate and some are deferred, as is customary in football player transactions.
The current football transfer market remains strong and the Club believes that future potential player
trading consistent with forecasts is reasonably achievable. The underlying models contain key
judgments about future player trading, which are subject to a significant degree of uncertainty, along
with other variables.
The Club benefits from the support of the majority shareholder of its parent undertaking and the directors
have received a letter of support from Mr Solak expressing his intention to continue to provide such
support. Management forecasts do not indicate the need for future funding from Mr Solak during the
going concern review period.
In light of this financial support, and the directors own considerations with regard to the availability of
. such support, the directors have not identified a material uncertainty that may cast significant doubt
over the Club’s ability to continue as a going concern for the foreseeable future.
Under all of these reasonable foreseeable scenarios, based on the cashflow forecast, the expectations
of player trading and the availability of funding, as required, the directors are satisfied that the Club will
have sufficient resources available to be able to meet its liabilities as they fall due and have concluded
that it is appropriate for the financial statements to be prepared on a going concern basis.

2.4 Turnover
Turnover represents the total amount receivable from the principal activities of the Company, excluding
transfer fees receivable, and is stated net of value added tax. The fixed element of broadcasting

2.5 Intangible assets
Player registrations
The element of each player's transfer fee which relates to his registration is capitalised as an intangible
asset and amortised over the period of his contract including any agreed extensions, subject to any
provision for impairment. Where there are deferred payment terms and no interest is charged, the future
cash flows are discounted to their present value at the date of the transition using the prevailing market
rate for such instruments. Contingent fees payable, which are dependent upon factors such as the
number of first team appearances and international debuts being made, are capitalised in the year when
it is considered probable that the conditions of the transfer agreement will be satisfied.
The Company does not consider it to be possible to determine value in use of an individual player in
isolation as that player cannot generate cash flows on his own. As such, the Company considers the
smallest cash-generating unit to contain all of the first team players, the stadium and the training
facilities.
However, management may consider that an individual player is highly unlikely to play for the first team
again, either due to serious injury or other circumstances outside the Club's control, and therefore will
not contribute to the future cash flows earned by the cash generating unit. In these instances, the
Company compares the carrying value of the asset to its recoverable amount. To the extent that the
carrying value exceeds the recoverable amount, the asset is impaired and the impairment loss is
recognised in the Statement of Comprehensive Income.
Video and audiovisual material
In the research phase of such an internal project it is not possible to demonstrate that the project will

economic benefits and that its cost can be reliably measured. The capitalised video and audiovisual

24. Pensions .
The Club participates in the Football League Pension and Life Assurance Scheme (‘the Scheme’). The
Scheme is a funded multi-employer defined benefit scheme, with 92 participating employers, and where
members may have periods of service attributable to several participating employers. The Club is unable
to identify its share of the assets and liabilities of the Scheme and therefore accounts for its contributions
as if they were paid to a defined contribution scheme.
The last actuarial valuation was carried out at 31 August 2023 where the total deficit on the on-going
valuation basis was £20.6 million. The accrual of benefits ceased within the Scheme on 31 August 1999,
therefore there are no contributions relating to current accrual. The Club pays monthly contributions based
on a notional split of the total expenses and deficit contributions of the Scheme. The results of the 2023
valuation were rolled forward to 30 June 2025 on the same assumptions as detailed above, and the Club’s
notional share of the deficit was £159,000 (£270,000 as at 30 June 2024). As at 30 June 2025, the Club
was paying total contributions of £12,000 per month (increasing by 5% p.a. on 1 September each year)
and based on the actuarial valuation assumptions detailed above will be sufficient to pay off the deficit by
31 October 2026.

30 June 2024), the present value of the Club’s outstanding contributions (i.e. their future liability) is
