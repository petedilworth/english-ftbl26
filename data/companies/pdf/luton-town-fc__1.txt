The directors present the strategic report for the year ended 30 June 2025.

Fair review of the business

The 2024/25 season marked Luton Town's return to the EFL Championship following relegation from the Premier
League. Despite high hopes for an immediate bounce back, the campaign ended in disappointment as the club
finished 22nd and was relegated to League One.

: Despite planning for the possibility, relegation proved an unsettling experience for the squad and wider club. There
was significant transfer activity during the summer of 2024, with several key players from the 2023/24 season
departing for significant fees including Ross Barkley, Ryan Giles and Chiedozie Ogbene. In their place came
experienced Championship performers Shandon Baptiste, Liam Walsh and Mark McGuinness, as well as the
promising young talents of Reuell Walters fram Arsenal and Lamine Fanne from AIK Stockholm. The window also
saw the arrival of midfielder Tom KrauR from Mainz.

The new season began with a number of key first team players out injured and an inexperienced defence lining up
against Burnley at home, a match that ended in a 4-1 defeat. It wasn’t until the fifth game of the campaign that the
team recorded their first win, away at Millwall. Results were frustratingly inconsistent throughout the first half of the
season, with the positivity of the 3-0 win in the local derby against Watford in October, quickly cancelled out by
comprehensive defeats to Middlesbrough, Leeds United and Norwich City in November.
Four defeats in a row around the Christmas period led to the club taking the difficult decision to part company with
Rob Edwards and his management team in early January and was also the catalyst for a busy January of player
trading. Joe Taylor departed for Huddersfield and Tom Kraus loan move was brought to an early conclusion, but
the squad was bolstered with the arrivals of Christ Makosso, Isaiah Jones, Thelo Aasgaard, Millenic Alli and Lasse
Nordas. .
Matt Bloomfield was appointed as the new First Team Manager, but with the squad lacking confidence, it took until
March for the team to record its first win under the new regime, away at Portsmouth.
That first win galvanised the squad and they sparked back into life, picking up 21 points from a possible 33 in the 11
“games leading up to the final day. In most season's that kind of form would have been good enough to secure
safety, but with the teams around us also enjoying an upturn in results, the table at the end of April saw the team
just 1 point clear of the relegation zone, with an inferior goal difference and needing a win at West Bromwich Albion
to ensure survival in the Championship.
As it transpired, our opponents on the final day played with the freedom of a team secure in mid-table and no
pressure to get a result and built an unassailable 5-1 lead by the 61st minute. Goals from Jordan Clark and Millenic
Alli restored some respectability to the scoreline but proved too little too late and relegation was confirmed at the
final whistle.
At the time of presenting these financial statements, the first team is just outside the play-off places in League One
with Jack Wilshere and Chris Powell recently installed as manager and assistant after another inconsistent start to
the season. With a huge amount of player turnover in the summer transfer window, following back-to-back
telegations, it is not surprising that it has taken time for the squad to find its feet. But recent results have suggested
that confidence is once again running through the squad and hopes for a positive campaign remain high.

. Financial Overview :
Relegation from the Premier League resulted in significantly reduced turnover in 2024/25 principally from broadcast
revenue. Despite a strong showing in the Premier League the club had planned for this risk, with relegation
clauses included in all player contracts helping to mitigate the reduction in income.
Financially the key difference from 2022/23, when the club had last competed in the second tier, was that Premier
. League parachute payments had been secured for the 2024/25 and 2025/26 seasons. These are distributed to
relegated clubs to allow a phased transition to an EFL Championship cost base. Given the club's long-term strategy
and continued commitment to operating a sustainable business model, a significant proportion of this revenue was
earmarked for capital investment activity.
Whilst the club’s support functions grew following promotion to the Premier League, the scale of the overall
operation remained comparable with other Championship clubs of a similar size, so fundamental re-organisation
was not required following relegation. Administration expenses, therefore, remained consistent with the prior year.
The club recorded a gross profit of £14.4m which was fully offset by administration costs, but the summer transfer
activity resulted in an operating profit of £17.1m. As in the prior year, all profits were reinvested in player
acquisitions or development activity and associated capital allowances were group relieved to fully offset any tax
liability.

Despite the club's revenue approximately halving, the club's wages to turnover ratio only increased by 16%, due to
squad restructuring and relegation clauses being triggered.

The net book value of the squad increased from the prior year, with the major summer departures relating to
players acquired for minimal acquisition costs and the consequent player trading income being used to fund new
acquisitions.

The club remains in good financial health with strong liquidity and net assets in excess of £45m at year end.
Movements in cash and key working capital are primarily driven by the timing of player trading transactions, with
trading subsequent to the year end further strengthening the club's asset position.

Principal risks and uncertainties

The Independent Football Regulator (IFR) has now been established to work with clubs from the Premier League to
the National League, to ensure the solidity and sustainability of the football pyramid. Consultation has begun on
exactly how this will be achieved, but the values of this club and its Board are shared by the leadership of the new
body, so the expectation is that the regulatory impact should be limited.

The IFR does, however, have backstop powers to ensure fairer distribution of broadcast income between the
leagues. With the club’s parachute payments finishing at the end 2025/26 season, the outcome of these
discussions is pivotal and the club will be fully engaged in the consultation process.

Financial risk management objectives and policies

League status risk

Due to the current distribution model of the football league, a principal risk for the football club is the performance of
the first team in relation to league status. The group manages the impact of this through control of player wages
(relegation clauses) and situational planning dependent upon league status. Given the club is competing in League
One in 2025/26, the downside tisk is significantly reduced in comparison to recent seasons.

Credit risk

Financial instruments that potentially subject the club to credit risk consist primarily of cash equivalents and
accounts receivable. These instruments contain a risk of counterparties failing to discharge their obligations. The
club monitors credit risk and manages credit risk exposure by assessing the creditworthiness of counterparties.
Due to the diversity of the debtor portfolio, the club does not assess this to pose a significant risk to the club at
present. Recovery of player trading receivables is supported by regulatory sanctions placed upon any defaulting
club.

Liquidity risk

Our liquidity needs are affected by our performance on the pitch which impacts revenues and wage costs. Budgets
and cash flows are prepared annually and forecasts are updated on a monthly basis so funding requirements are
planned and approved well in advance. Despite consecutive relegations the guaranteed parachute payments mean
the club's liquidity position is strong.

Operational risk .

The club operates a cashless stadium and has introduced access control systems for staff and fans. This has
greatly reduced operational risk associated with cash payments and unauthorised entry. .
Other key operational risks pertain to stadium safety, force majeure and system failures. Annual inspections, formal
certification, disaster recovery plans and insurance policies are all established to mitigate risks. .
Future developments
The club’s long-term strategy remains to build a football club that can stand on its own two feet by operating a
sustainable business model, whilst remaining competitive at the highest levels of the game. This requires the
construction of Power Court stadium with a combination of both debt and equity funding. This project will be
undertaken by another group undertaking, with a significant proportion of the parachute payments earmarked for
this purpose to support the group undertaking.

Employee Engagement

The group holds weekly management meetings, either in person or virtually, attended by departmental managers in
order to ensure that information can then be disseminated downwards to each department and employee.

The group actively encourages employees in any efforts to further their development via flexible working hours and
the funding of external training where applicable.

In 2014 Luton Town Football Club became the first English professional football club to become a fully accredited
Living Wage employer, paying the ‘Real Living Wage’. The commitment to pay all employees a ‘Real Living Wage’
has been maintained throughout the period. .

Administrative employees are provided the required office equipment in order to ensure that working from home is
a realistic and safe option.

The group offers a range of support to promote the health and mental wellbeing of staff. The Employee Assistance
Programme (EAP), which is confidential and managed independently by Health Assured, is available to all staff and
immediate family members. Annual health assessments are also undertaken for key workers and senior
executives.

" The group continues to be an equal opportunities employer which is designed to ensure that all applicants and
employees receive the same treatment. Through the implementation of these principles, the group has a staff that
reflects the diversity of the town and wider region.

Upwards feedback from employees to managers is encouraged to drive improvement and employee engagement.
Employees are frequently involved in decision making through discussions with their managers.
Suppliers
The group continues to foster long term relationships by maintaining high standards of business conduct.
Department heads are responsible for ensuring that close communication is maintained with suppliers to allow
continued engagement and custom which is fair for both the group and the supplier. Local suppliers are utilised
where possible, in order to benefit the local economy.
Customers
Supporters of the club represent the most important customers of the group. Through the use of the club's website
and social media channels, the group is committed to communicating clearly and honestly with the fanbase, The
group also use these mediums to listen to feedback from supporters as well as distributing post-match
questionnaires to identify areas for improvement. :
The group is actively engaged with the Luton Town Supports Trust (LTST), meeting with them on a regular basis.
Additionally, the Trust has the legal right to veto any future changes to Luton's identity, including its name, .
nickname, colours, club crest and mascot. The CEO has also set up a direct messaging group chat with all LTST
committee members to increase transparency and immediacy of feedback on any issues.
Besides supporters, the group also has key customers such as sponsors, advertisers and tenants. Communication
with each customer is maintained by the relevant department head to ensure a positive customer relationship.

Community and Environment

Since acquiring ownership, the board has been committed to ensuring that the group has a positive impact upon
the local community and environment. : :
The group promotes the work of the Luton Town Community Trust, the official charity of Luton Town Football Club.
The Trust aims to help disadvantaged and vulnerable members of the local community.

The group has consistently made the conscious decision to avoid gambling sponsorship in the stadium, on the
website or on any aspect of the player kits. This is a matter of principle for the group and the board believe strongly
that gambling should not be promoted, particularly where children are exposed to such advertising.

Central to the ambitious development projects is a desire to bring economic growth and prosperity to the local
economy. Throughout the design and development stage of these key projects consultation has been held with the
local community to ensure that there has been a focus on delivering an end result which is mutually beneficial to
both the group and the inhabitants of Luton and Bedfordshire. :

With regards to the long term sustainability of the environment, the board always consider the group's impact when
making key decisions. The primary waste removal supplier for the group sorts the waste to ensure that materials
are recycled and land-fill waste is minimised. The retail department use compostable mailing bags for posting
online orders and paper bags in store so that plastic waste can be reduced.

The stadium development project at Power Court has been designed with the environment in mind incorporating
cutting edge design which minimises impact upon the environment and promotes long term sustainability.

In April 2025, Reflo was introduced as the new official teamwear partner. Every piece of kit is crafted from recycled
materials, and the innovative Reloop technology also makes it recyclable.

Key Board Decisions

During the period the board made a number of key decisions which were considered to be in the interests of the
group and other key stakeholders:

* The board continued their investment into the Power Court development project. It is felt that the
construction of a new stadium and surrounding development is key to the long term success of the group
as well as the prosperity of the focal area.

* The board have also invested in the Academy as it continues to pursue EPPP 2 category status and plans
to significantly improve the facilities over the next ear. As well as the potential benefit to the club of
developing young talent for the senior squad, this also provides aspiration and encouragement to young .
people in the local area.

* The board took the decision to acquire the historic Mill Yard site in the heart of Luton's cultural scene. This
has secured the future of the town's much loved ‘The Bear Club’ in its current location and format which
was considered very important in the wider context of the board's commitment to the regeneration of
Luton's town centre.
