Introduction
The directors present their Strategic Report for Charlton Athletic Football Company Limited ("the Club" or
“the Company") for the year ended 30 June 2025.
Principal activity ;
The principal activity of the Company continues to be the operation of a professional football club,
currently competing in the Sky Bet Championship of the EFL (English Football League), together with
associated activities.
Review of Business
Introduction
The 2024/25 season was transformational for Charlton Athletic, culminating in a dramatic and successful
return to the Championship after a five-year absence. The aim throughout the season was to secure
promotion, and this was achieved through the play-offs, with a memorable victory over Leyton Orient in the
final at Wembley Stadium.
This achievement was built on the superb efforts of everyone at the Club, with the extraordinary support of
our fans and partners. The backing of the Valley faithful was outstanding throughout the campaign, with
attendances increasing markedly from March onwards and several home fixtures attracting crowds in
excess of 20,000. Over 55,000 tickets were sold in three hours for the Wembley final, with further demand
requested (but unable to be fulfilled) from the EFL.
The on-pitch success was complemented by significant progress across the Club's commercial, operational
and infrastructural activities. Following promotion, the Club moved decisively to invest in facilities and
strengthen the playing squad, positioning Charlton Athletic as a sustainable, competitive force in the
Championship. These initiatives collectively demonstrate the Club's renewed ambition and long-term
commitment to establishing itself at a higher level of English football.
On-pitch performance
League Campaign
The 2024/25 season proved to:be one of sustained competitiveness and resulting triumph. Finishing in 4th .
position with 85 points, the Club secured its place in the League One play-offs. Captain Greg Docherty
lifting the play-off trophy at Wembley represented the pinnacle of the season, finally removing the
uncertainty around our league status and delivering Championship football for our supporters.

Cup Competitions
Cup competitions provided valuable competitive matches, though the Club exited all three competitions in
the early rounds. We reached the third round of the FA Cup before a defeat away at Preston North End
ended our involvement. In the EFL Cup, Birmingham City defeated us at The Valley in the first round, while
Leyton Orient progressed at our expense in the second round of the EFL Trophy. While these results were
disappointing, the Cup fixtures provided opportunities for squad rotation and development of younger
players, which proved valuable across the season.
Financial Performance
The Club is committed to transparency in our financial activities, and we aim to set out clearly our financial
position below
Revenue Performance
Total turnover for the financial year was £11,167k, representing an increase of 26.7% on the prior year
(£8,812k). This growth was driven by several factors:
Matchday Attendance: Average attendances at The Valley increased to 15,255 (2023/24 - 13,481),
representing a rise of 13.2%. Particularly encouragingly, attendances grew steadily from March onwards as
the play-off push intensified, with four matches attracting crowds exceeding 20,000 fans (2023/24 - zero
matches). The highest attendance of the season was 25,722 for the home play-off fixture against Wycombe
Wanderers. Season ticket sales remained level with the 23/24 season at approximately 8,000.
Commercial Revenue: Retail operations were brought under the direct control of the company during the
year, with stock and cost of sales now included in the financial statements. Retail operations performed
well, supported by the refurbished club shop and in-house merchandising operation.
The Fan Zone was expanded in March, strengthening F&B income and providing additional space for fans to
congregate as attendances began to rise. RSK extended their sponsorship contract, and the club signed a
new kit supply deal with Reebok to commence from the start of the 25/26 season.
Broadcasting and Central Distributions: Sky Bet League One status and associated EFL distributions,
along with any specific broadcasting revenue, rose in line with the EFL's commercial deals. We also
generated a small additional amount through the play-off campaign. ,
Player Trading: Net transfer expenditure of £nil was incurred in the year. The Club generated profitable
sales of Alfie May despite him entering the final year of his contract and the sale of Rarmani Edmonds-
Green, which included a bonus for the buying club reaching the play-offs. This demonstrated our evolving
capability in player trading and recruitment, delivering a promotion-winning squad with nil net transfer
expenditure.

Cost Base
Total operating costs increased to £28.3m (2024: £23.9m), reflecting the higher investment in playing
talent necessary to achieve promotion and the increased operational activity associated with improving
attendances and commercial activity. We also were adversely impacted by the increase in National
Insurance Contributions during the financial year. .
The wages-to-revenue ratio was 141% (2024: 138%), demonstrating planned investment in squad. Total
employee costs were £15.7m (2024: £12.1m). These figures include bonuses earned by players and staff
: related to promotion to the Championship.

Strategic investment in facilities and infrastructure, combined with player acquisition and development
costs, contributed to the Club's loss for the financial year of £15.4m (2024: loss £13.9m). This loss reflects
the deliberate investment strategy pursued by our shareholders to position the Club for sustained success
in the Championship.
Financial Position
The Club's financial position remains robust, with total assets of £12.0m at the year-end (2024: £7.3m) anda
cash balance of £0.46m (2024: £0.03m). . :
Importantly, the Club remains debt-free in terms of external borrowing, with our balance sheet primarily
reflecting shareholder investment rather than bank debt. This provides significant financial flexibility as we
embark on our Championship campaign.
Our financial stability was maintained throughout the year despite operational challenges, including the
cyber-attack in August 2024 that temporarily disrupted systems. The Club's response to this incident,
including investment in enhanced IT security, has strengthened our operational resilience.

Capital Investment

A key strategic priority following promotion was investment in our infrastructure to ensure The Valley and

our training facilities meet Championship standards and support our longer-term ambitions.

Total capital expenditure of approximately £3 million was committed during and immediately following the

season, comprising:

Stadium Infrastructure:

e Pitch Installation: A new hybrid playing surface was installed in summer 2024, providing a high-quality
playing surface suitable for Championship football and capable of withstanding the increased demands
of a longer season.

e LED Floodlighting: Modern LED floodlights were installed, improving match-day experience, reducing
energy consumption, and meeting broadcast standards for Championship fixtures.

e Office and Retail Refurbishment: The club offices and shop underwent comprehensive refurbishment,
creating a more professional environment for staff and an enhanced retail experience for supporters.

e FanZone Expansion: The popular FanZone area was expanded, providing additional pre-match and
post-match entertainment facilities and improving the overall match-day experience.

« Stadium Maintenance: Essential maintenance works were completed across The Valley to ensure
compliance with safety standards and maintain the quality of facilities for supporters.

These infrastructure investments demonstrate the shareholders’ commitment to providing elite facilities

and reflect our determination to establish Charlton Athletic as a sustainable Championship club. The timing

of these projects, executed in the close season following promotion, ensured minimal disruption while
maximising readiness for the step up in competition.

Future Outlook

Looking ahead to the 2025/26 season, our focus is firmly on establishing ourselves in the Championship

and continuing the progress demonstrated throughout the 2024/25 campaign.

On-pitch Objectives: The immediate priority is consolidation in the Championship, demonstrating that we

can compete effectively at this level. Our recruitment strategy will continue to identify players who

combine quality with value, building on the successful signings of the previous season. Squad depth will be
enhanced to cope with the demands of Championship football, while maintaining the core group that
secured promotion.

Infrastructure and Facilities: Building on the capital investment committed in 2024/25, we will continue to

enhance our training facilities at Sparrows Lane and explore further improvements to The Valley. These

investments are essential to support player development, attract quality players, and provide an excellent
match-day experience for supporters.

Summary
The Board is immensely proud of the progress made during the 2024/25 season. Achieving promotion was
the primary objective, and to do so via the play-offs, culminating in victory at Wembley, made the success
all the more memorable.
This achievement was built on solid foundations: strategic recruitment, infrastructural investment, growing
attendances, improved commercial performance, and the unwavering support of our fans. The Club entered
the Championship better positioned than at any point in recent years, with modern facilities, a competitive
squad, financial stability, and a clear strategic direction.
We are under no illusions about the challenges ahead. The Championship is one of the most competitive
leagues in world football, and establishing ourselves at this level wilt require continued investment, hard
work and focus. However, with the backing of our shareholders, the quality of our staff at both The Valley
and Sparrows Lane, and the passion of our supporters, we are confident in our ability to thrive at this level.
The Board would like to extend its gratitude to our players, management team, staff, and wider Charlton
Athletic community for their contributions during this pivotal season. We look forward to building on this
success in the seasons ahead. We would also like to thank Charlie Methven, who left us as Chief Executive
in early March 2025. Charlie played a pivotal role in putting the right people in place on both the football
and business side of our club, which contributed to improved performances on the pitch and meaningful
increases in our revenues.

Principal Risks and Uncertainties
The principal risk to the Company is the team's performance, which directly affects core revenue streams
such as matchday income, commercial sponsorship, and broadcasting rights. Following promotion to the
Championship, this risk profile has evolved, with increased competitive pressure but also greater revenue
opportunities.
To mitigate this risk, we have continued to diversify our revenue base, reducing dependency on matchday
performance. A key focus has been developing off-pitch income sources through the development of
commercial partnerships and optimisation of our facilities at The Valley. The subsequent enhancement of
Sparrows Lane training ground and ongoing improvements at the stadium are part of this strategy, creating
assets that provide financial stability even during periods of on-pitch volatility.
Investment in data-driven recruitment strategies that align with our playing philosophy and squad-building
model has been prioritised. This approach, which focuses on identifying value in the transfer market while
developing talent through our academy, allows us to maintain a competitive playing squad without
overextending financially. _
Our long-term capital investments, such as the development of enhanced training facilities at Sparrows
Lane and infrastructure improvements at The Valley, serve to future-proof the Club's operations. These
investments support player development, help attract quality recruits, and generate additional revenue
streams through facility usage and commercial activities.
In the broader economic context, inflationary pressures on wages and operational costs remain a concern,
particularly in the Championship where financial competition is intense. We manage these risks through
careful financial planning, maintaining close control over operating expenses, securing long-term contracts
with key suppliers, and actively pursuing new revenue streams from non-football events and commercial
partnerships. Our strategy of securing off-pitch income ensures ongoing financial stability and provides
resources for continued investment in our playing squad.
Financial risk management
The Company closely monitors financial risks to minimise potential impacts on the business:
Trade Debtors: Trade debtor risk is actively managed, with amounts due from other clubs covered by EFL
regulations that help reduce risk. Credit policies are strictly enforced, and a risk register is regularly
reviewed by the Board. Appropriate mitigations and actions are implemented to address identified risks
and uncertainties.

Inflationary Pressures: In the current economic climate, inflationary pressures on wages and operational

ensure the interests of all stakeholders are aligned.

2.4 Turnover
Turnover represents income receivable from football and related commercial activities
excluding transfer fees receivable and value added tax. Broadcast and matchday income is
recognised when related matches are played. Advance season tickets sales are included within
deferred income and released to turnover in the relevant season.

2.5 Cost of sales
Cost of sales comprises costs directly attributable to the generation of merchandise revenue
and is recognised in the same accounting period as the related income. Cost of sales includes
the purchase cost of merchandise sold together with directly attributable customisation and
branding costs and inbound freight, customs duties and handling costs. Cost of sales also
includes any write-down of merchandise inventories to net realisable value where the carrying
value exceeds the amount expected to be recovered from sale.
Merchandise inventories are stated at the lower of cost and net realisable value.
All other operating costs of the Club are recognised as administrative expenses and are not
included within cost of sales.

2.8 Pensions
Defined contribution pension plan
The Company operates a defined contribution plan for its employees. A defined contribution
plan is a pension plan under which the Company pays fixed contributions into a separate entity.
Once the contributions have been paid the Company has no further payment obligations.
The contributions are recognised as an expense in profit or loss when they fall due. Amounts not
Paid are shown in accruals as a liability in the Statement of Financial Position. The assets of the
plan are held separately from the Company in independently administered funds.
Defined benefit scheme
The club is a member of the Football League Pension and Life Assurance Scheme (‘the
Scheme’). The Scheme is a multi-employer defined benefit pension scheme, where it is unable
to identify its share of the underlying assets and liabilities on a consistent and reasonable basis.
Therefore, as required by FRS 102, the club accounts for the Scheme as if it were a defined
contribution scheme. As a result, the amounts charged to Administrative expenses represent the
contributions payable to the Scheme. Where material, contributions payable under an
agreement with the Scheme to fund past deficits are recognised as a liability in the club’s
financial statements.

Grants of a revenue nature are recognised in profit or loss in the same period as the related

24. Pension commitment

The club participates in the Football League Pension and Life Assurance Scheme (‘the Scheme’).
The Scheme is a funded multi-employer defined benefit scheme, with 92 participating employers,
and where members may have periods of service attributable to several participating employers.
The club is unable to identify its share of assets and liabilities of the Scheme and therefore accounts
for its contributions as if they were paid to a defined contribution scheme.
The accrual of benefits ceased within the Scheme on 31 August 1999, therefore there are no
contributions relating to current accruals. The club pays monthly contributions based on a notional
split of the total expenses and deficit contributions of the Scheme. At 30 June 2025 the club's share
of the deficit was £40k (2024 - £76k).
The club currently pays total contributions of £36k (2024 - £34k), per annum which increases at 5.0%
per annum (first increase at 1 September 2016) and based on the actuarial valuation assumptions
used in the Scheme, will be sufficient to pay off the deficit by 1 July 2026.

28. Cyber - attack and control issues
On 6 August 2024 the club were subject to a ransomware cyber-attack to its legacy accounting
system which had been in use until 31 October 2023. As a result of the attack - for which the financial
impact was covered under the club's insurance policy - significant financial data on the system was
wiped and unavailable. While the direct financial impact was mitigated, the attack also disrupted
other areas of activity including the financial records of the business.
Subsequently, the Board took steps to increase resource available to post and reconcile
transactions. However, this action has yet to prove successful and support for balances, including
those posted in the year ended 30 June 2025, is not of a standard that would ordinarily be expected.
This has again led to difficulty in providing a full audit trail to facilitate our external audit resulting in
the qualified audit report included on pages 14 to 19 of this annual report.
