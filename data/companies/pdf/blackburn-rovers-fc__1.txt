EXECUTIVE REPORT
YEAR ENDED 30 JUNE 2025 .
Blackburn Rovers narrowly missed out on a Championship play-off place for the second time In three seasons, following
a dramatic final day at Bramall Lane.
Just one more goal against Sheffield United in the final fixture of the 2024-25 campaign would have secured sixth spot.
Instead, a 1-1 draw with the Blades meant the 3,000 travelling Rovers fans left South Yorkshire proud of their team’s
efforts, both on the day and across the season, yet still dreaming of what might have been.
{t was a remarkable turnaround from the previous campaign, when Rovers had only avoided relegation on the final day
at Leicester City.
Supported by newly appointed Head of Football Operations Rudy Gestede and Head of Technica! Development Adam
Qwen, Head Coach John Eustace set about strengthening his squad over the summer, determined ta shape a side
‘ capable of challenging at the opposite end of the table.
Senegalese striker Makhtar Gueye and japanese forward Yuki Ghashi were first to arrive on permanent deals for
undisclosed fees from RWD Molenbeek and Sanfreece Hiroshima respectively.
They were followed by Danny Batth, Andi Weimann and Lewis Baker, who added valuable Championship experience to
the group, whilst promising young Premier League players Owen Beck and Amario Cozier-Ouberry arrived on season-
long loan deals from Liverpool and Brighton.
Rovers completed two more deals on deadline day, bringing in Hungarian goalkeeper Balazs Toth from Fehervar and
proven promotion winner Tadd Cantwell from Rangers.
By this point, Sammie Szmodics, who netted 33 goats in the previous campaign and who had already scored in Rovers’
opening two games of the 2024-25 season, had completed a big money move to Premier League newcomers Ipswich
Town. Leo Wahlstedt, Semir Telalovic and Sam Gallagher had also departed the club.
Eustace’s side enjoyed an impressive start to the season, turning Ewood Park into a fortress, with Rovers winning their
first five home league fixtures for the first time in 30 years.
Six straight Championship victories, including five successive clean sheets, throughout November and Oecember
propelied Rovers up to fifth in the table, prior to a disappointing run of results over the festive period which saw Rovers
win just one of their next nine league games.
January saw another busy transfer window, with Dion Sanderson, Adam Forshaw, Augustus Karrgbo, Yuri Ribeiro,
Emmanuel Dennis and Cauley Woodrow all arriving to bolster Rovers’ play-off push.
However, for the second successive season, a mid-season managerial change disrupted momentum, as head coach John
Eustace departed for Derby County, with David Lowe winning two of his three games in caretaker charge.
in late February, former West Brom, Watford and Besiktas boss Valerien Ismael arrived through the Ewood doors. After a
challenging start, the Frenchman steered the side to four successive victories over Luton Town, Millwall, Sunderland and
Watford, bringing the club to within touching distance of the play-offs.
Victory at Sheffield United on the final day would have sealed sixth spot and Rovers looked on course when Ohashi
netted his 10th goal of the campaign early in the second half. But Anel Anmedhodzic’s equaliser ended those hopes ~
denying Rovers a potential return to the Premier League, 30 years on from the club’s historic title triumph.
Rovers Under-21s competed in six different competitions for the first time in the club’s history — featuring in Premier
League 2, the Premier League Cup, the Premier League International Cup, the newly-formed National League Cup, the
Central League Cup and the Lancashire FA Senior Cup. ‘
In the league, Rovers finished 21st out of 26 teams, whilst in the Premier League Cup, Mike Sheron's side advanced to
the knockout stage, before falling to a heavy defeat away to Chariton Athletic.

Basis of preparation of the financial statements .
The financial statements have been prepared under the historical cost convention, as modified by the revaluation
of freehold property and In accordance with Financial Reporting Standard 102, the Financial Reporting Standard
applicable in the United Kingdom and the Republic of Ireland and the Companies Act 2006.
} The financial statements are prepared in sterling which is the functional currency of the company. Monetary
amounts in these financial statements are rounded to the nearest £.
The preparation of financial statements in compliance with FRS 102 requires the use of certain critical accounting
estimates. It also requires management to exercise judgment in applying the company's accounting policies (see
note 3).
The financial statements contain information about The Blackburn Ravers Football and Athletic Limited as an
individual company and do not contain consolidated financial information as the parent company of a group. The
company has taken advantage of the exemption available to it under s400 of the Companies Act 2005 from the
requirement to prepare consolidated financial statements as it and its subsidiary undertaking are included within

The principal accounting policies applied by the company are described below.
Going concern
As at 30 June 2025, the company had net current liabilities of £153,623,451 and reported an operating loss,
before changes in intangible fixed assets, of £18,988,509 for the year. In common with many football clubs, the
company may continue to make operating losses and incur net cash outflows depending on a number of variables,
including the success of the team in league and cup competitions and the level of transfer activity.
The company is funded through a bank overdraft facility and a shareholder joan, and in view of the current
: financial position, the company remains reliant on its ability to maintain existing and obtain additional funding as
necessary.
In managing the finances of the company, the directors remain mindful of the need to ensure the company will
comply with the Championship Profitability and Sustainability rules.
As part of the directors' assessment of going concern they have prepared detailed cash flow forecasts for the
period to the end of June 2028. These forecasts indicate that the company will require significant funding in
addition to the current facilities available to the company.
The amount of additional funding required will be dependent on the net proceeds of any player trading, on field
performance, and availability of bank facilities. In view of this the directors have received confirmation fram the
ultimate parent company, Venkateshwara Hatcheries Private Limited (“VHPL”}, that it has sufficient funds and is
willing to provide such additional financing as may be required to fund the company, to the extent necessary for
the company to continue to trade and to pay its liabilities as and when they become due, for the 12 months
, following approval of these accounts and thereafter for the foreseeable future even in the event of the bank
facility not being renewed.

Impairment
The carrying amounts of the company’s assets are reviewed for impairment when events or changes In
circumstances indicate that the carrying amount of the asset may not be recoverable. If any such indication exists,
the asset’s recoverable amount is estimated.
An impairment loss is recognised whenever the carrying amount of an asset or its income-generating unit exceeds
its recoverable amount. Impairment losses are recognised in the profit and loss account.
impairment losses recognised in respect of income-generating units are allocated to the carrying amount of the
intangible fixed assets in the unit on a pro rata or more appropriate basis. An income generating unit is the
smallest identifiable group of assets that generates income that is largely independent of the income streams
from other assets or groups of assets.
The recaverable amount of fixed assets is the greater of their net realisable value and value in use. In assessing
value in use, the expected future cash flows are discounted to their present value using a pre-tax discount rate that
teflects current market assessments of the rate of return expected on an equally risky investment. For an asset that
does not generate largely independent income streams, the recoverable amount is determined for the income-
generating unit to which the asset belongs.
