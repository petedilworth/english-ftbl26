STRATEGIC REPORT
FOR THE YEAR ENDED 30 JUNE 2024
The directors present the strategic report for the year ended 30 June 2024.
Fair review of the business
The focus for the 2023/24 season was to consolidate our position in EFL League One and lay the groundwork for future
success, both on and off the pitch. The team achieved a stable mid-table finish, demonstrating consistent performances
throughout the season.
Our passionate supporters once again provided invaluable backing, with strong attendance figures at both home and away
fixtures. This unwavering support has been a driving force behind the club’s progress.
The strategic objectives set for the season were as follows:
1. Infrastructure Developments
The club remains committed to improving its facilities and infrastructure to meet the demands of modern football. During the
season, initial steps were taken toward developing both the stadium and training ground. While these projects are at an early
stage, they represent a key part of the club's long-term vision to enhance the experience for players, staff, and supporters.
2. Sustainability
Financial sustainability continues to be at the forefront of the club's strategy. A competitive playing budget was maintained, .
supported by a focused approach to transfers and player recruitment. The club also explored additional revenue streams to
support its growth, with plans to create sustainable financial models that will benefit Bristol Rovers in the years to come.
3. Youth Development
Our commitment to youth development has remained a core pillar of our philosophy. This season, we again exceeded league
averages for the inclusion of academy-developed players in both starting lineups and matchday squads. Plans to elevate the
academy from Category Three to Category Two are progressing steadily, with the club investing in the resources needed to
achieve this upgrade.
4. Fan Engagement
The 2023/24 season saw a continued increase in fan engagement, driven by initiatives that strengthened the connection
between the club and its supporters. Season ticket sales and matchday attendance figures both saw year-on-year growth,
highlighting the loyalty and passion of the fanbase. We are committed to building on this momentum by ensuring fans remain
at the heart of everything we do.
5. Community Impact
Our partnership with the Community Trust continued to thrive during the season, delivering impactful programs that
benefited the local area. These efforts received recognition once again, with the club celebrating another successful year of
community outreach and engagement.
6, Continuous Improvement
The Executive Committee continued to focus on operational excellence, driving improvements across all areas of the club.
From enhanced recruitment processes to stronger commercial performance, the club is committed to fostering a culture of
innovation and progress.

Looking Ahead
As we look to the 2024/25 season, our ambition remains to strengthen our position in League One while building a
foundation for future success. Although infrastructure developments are in the early stages, they are central to our long-term
vision for the club. We will also continue to focus on youth development, financial sustainability, and engaging with our
supporters and community.
On behalf of the board, we extend our sincere thanks to all our fans, staff, and partners for their continued dedication and
support. Together, we are building a bright future for Bristol Rovers.

Basis for opinion

Auditor's responsibilities for the audit of the financial statements
Our objectives are to obtain reasonable assurance about whether the financial statements as a whole are free from material
misstatement, whether due to fraud or error, and to issue an auditor's report that includes our opinion. Reasonable assurance ~
is a high level of assurance but is not a guarantee that an audit conducted in accordance with ISAs (UK) will always detect a
material misstatement when it exists. Misstatements can arise from fraud or error and are considered material if, individually
or in the aggregate, they could reasonably be expected to influence the economic decisions of users taken on the basis of
these financial statements.
The extent to which our procedures are capable of detecting irregularities, including fraud, is detailed below.
We gained an understanding of the legal and regulatory framework applicable to the company and the industry in which it
operates, and considered the risk of acts by the company that were contrary to applicable laws and regulations, including
fraud. We designed audit procedures to respond to the risk, recognising that the risk of not detecting a material misstatement
due to fraud is higher than the risk of nat detecting one resulting from error, as fraud may involve deliberate concealment by,
for example, forgery or intentional misrepresentations, or through collusion.
We focussed on laws and regulations which could give rise to a material misstatement in the financial statements, including,
but not limited to, the Companies Act 2006 and UK tax legislation. Our tests included agreeing the financial statement
disclosures to underlying supporting documentation and enquiries with management. There are inherent limitations in the
audit procedures described above and, the further removed non-compliance with laws and regulations is from the events and
transactions reflected in the financial statements, the less likely we would become aware of it. We did not identify any key
audit matters relating to irregularities, including fraud. As in all our audits, we also addressed the risk of management override
of internal controls, including testing journals and evaluating whether there was evidence of bias by the directors that
represented a risk of material misstatement due to fraud.
