## Origins

Fulham was founded in 1879 as Fulham St Andrew's Church Sunday School FC,
making it London's oldest professional football club. The club has played
at Craven Cottage, on the bank of the Thames in Fulham, since 1896, and
takes its nickname, "the Cottagers," directly from the ground's name.

The club's affluent riverside setting has also given it an unusual
reputation among English football's largely working-class fan culture:
photographs of Fulham supporters' elaborate away-day food and drink —
cheese, champagne, on one trip to Sheffield United even caviar — circulate
widely and draw regular ribbing from rival fans. In May 2022, celebrating
promotion back to the Premier League, the club's own shop sold a £20
cheeseboard engraved with the crest and the Championship trophy as part of
its promotion merchandise range — read by fans and press alike as the club
knowingly playing up to the stereotype rather than fighting it.

## Trajectory

Fulham drifted through the lower divisions for most of the twentieth
century before Mohamed Al-Fayed's 1997 takeover funded one of English
football's fastest rises: champions of the Third Division in 1996-97
under Kevin Keegan, champions of the Second Division in 1998-99, and
champions of the First Division in 2000-01, reaching the Premier League
for the first time in 2001-02 — three divisional titles in five seasons,
all bankrolled personally by Al-Fayed. The club stayed in the top flight
for thirteen seasons before its first relegation, in 2014.

A different pattern followed. Promoted back to the Premier League via the
2017-18 Championship play-offs, Fulham spent over £100m in the following
summer transfer window — the first promoted club to do so — and were
relegated with 26 points, the joint-worst Premier League points total in
over a decade at that point. The club has yo-yoed at the top of the
Championship and the bottom of the Premier League since: promoted again in
2020, relegated in 2021, and promoted a third time in 2022, since when it
has held its Premier League place.

## Ownership & Finance

Mohamed Al-Fayed bought Fulham for £6.25m in 1997 and funded the club's
rise and subsequent losses personally for sixteen years. That ownership
produced one significant legal defeat: manager Jean Tigana, dismissed for
alleged gross misconduct on the final day of his contract in 2003, sued
for wrongful dismissal and won, with the High Court ruling in 2004 that
the club had failed to substantiate its case and ordering it to pay
Tigana more than £2m in lost share options and other sums. Al-Fayed sold
the club to American businessman Shahid Khan in July 2013, writing off
the club's substantial loans to him beforehand so that Khan took over a
debt-free club; the fee itself was not disclosed, estimated at
£150-200m.

## Infrastructure & Environment

Craven Cottage has been Fulham's only home since 1896; its Grade II
listed pavilion, the "Cottage" the ground is named after, dates to 1905.
A long-planned redevelopment of the Riverside Stand — delayed by the
COVID-19 pandemic and the 2023 insolvency of its main contractor — was
completed in 2025, adding a "Sky Deck" with a rooftop swimming pool, a
boutique hotel and event space, and raising the ground's capacity to
around 28,500.
