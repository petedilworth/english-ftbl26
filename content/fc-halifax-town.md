## Origins

There have been two clubs. **Halifax Town AFC was formed on 24 May 1911** at
the Saddle Hotel — the company that ran it was incorporated seven weeks later —
and was a founder member of the Third Division North in 1921. **FC Halifax Town
was formed in 2008**, by people from the old club's board, after the old company
was liquidated. They are not the same legal person, and no honest account of
this club can start in 1911.

Halifax, like Huddersfield, was a rugby town first. The rugby clubs long
predate the football one, and the football club arrived in 1911 with no
permanent ground and did not get one for a decade. What it eventually got, it
never owned.

## Trajectory

The old club left the Football League twice and came back once.

**1992/93**: bottom of the fourth tier, relegated out of the League after
seventy-two years. The documented material is what happened next rather than
why it happened — income collapsed in the Conference, supporters stayed away,
and the club nearly went under in 1994/95. **1997/98**: Conference champions by
nine points, and straight back up.

**2001/02**: relegated again, and this time the causes are on the record and
they are financial. The chairman who took over after a boardroom vote of no
confidence put the club up for sale in February 2002, saying the **wage bill had
doubled to £1.2m** under the previous regime and that, combined with the cost of
a **new stand left unfinished when the money ran out**, the club was
"financially crippled". In April 2002, days after relegation, it went into
administration owing about **£900,000, of which £220,000 was to the Inland
Revenue**. It was rescued by ten local businessmen putting in £10,000 each,
alongside the supporters' trust.

Hold that stand in mind. It comes back.

Then **2008**, and the mechanism matters more than the story. Halifax Town went
into administration on **14 March 2008** and were **docked ten points** by the
Conference. This site's table shows what that did and did not do: they finished
**twentieth on 42 points, one point clear of Altrincham** — they survived on the
pitch. The debt was **£814,000 to HMRC** against total liabilities over £2m,
and the administrator could not agree a **CVA**. Conference rules did not permit
a club to start the following season without one. The club was removed from the
competition in **May 2008**, an appeal failed, and a winding-up order followed.
The company was not formally dissolved until **16 February 2019** — it outlived
the club it had run by more than a decade.

So the sequence is worth stating plainly, because it is the thing most often
got wrong. **Entering administration cost Halifax ten points. Failing to leave
it killed them.** Those are two different sanctions with two different
triggers, and only the first is a football punishment at all. A knock-on: 
**Altrincham, who finished twenty-first, were reprieved** by the place Halifax's
disappearance left vacant — their third consecutive reprieve.

The new club was placed by the FA in the **Northern Premier League Division One
North for 2008/09** — the eighth tier of English football, three levels below
the Conference place the old club had held — after appeals to be admitted higher
failed. Nothing was "docked": the old club's league membership lapsed with the
old club, and a new club applied to enter the pyramid. That is milder than it
sounds by comparison: **Chester's phoenix was placed at the same level in 2010,
and Darlington's four divisions down in 2012** on the FA's recommendation.

The climb back took five years: champions of Division One North in 2009/10 with
**100 points and 108 goals**, champions of the Northern Premier the season
after, and up through the Conference North play-offs in 2013. Those five years
are the six seasons this site's data cannot see — the gap in the record from
2009 to 2013, plus 2016/17. **In 2015/16 the club won the FA Trophy and was
relegated in the same season**, and came back at the first attempt. It has been
in the fifth tier ever since.

The new club has never had a point deducted. Its only sanction on the record is
a **transfer embargo in August 2026 for non-compliance with league financial
regulations** — one of six clubs named at the same time, which the club said
concerned the submission of budget information rather than its finances.

## Ownership & Finance

**David Bosomworth** has been a director of the new company since it was
incorporated in 2008 and its chairman and principal shareholder for most of the
time since; he was one of a small group who had been funding the old club for
more than a year before it folded. The stated model is explicit and unusually
public: one of the **lowest budgets in the division**, a gap between income and
expenditure that is not narrowing, and a refusal to repeat 2008.

The structural point the club keeps making is worth recording because it applies
to every club on this site's fifth tier: **the National League imposes League
Two travel and fixture costs on far smaller central distributions.** A club that
has been relegated out of the Football League keeps the cost base and loses the
income; a club that has never been in it, like this one, never had the income at
all.

There is a real gap in what can be said here. **This site's finance table holds
no rows for FC Halifax Town**, and no turnover, wage bill or loss figure could
be sourced for the current company. The supporters' trust that helped rescue
Halifax Town AFC in 2002 is documented; **no shareholding, percentage or board
seat for a trust in the current club is**, and this page does not assert one.

## Infrastructure & Environment

The Shay was a rubbish tip.

The site held a Georgian mansion, designed by John Carr and built around 1770,
which was demolished in **1903**; the ground takes its name from the house. The
corporation drove a road through, built an embankment out of **waste from the
Halifax gas works**, and used the land as a **tip** and then as a **Territorial
Army practice ground** during the First World War. On **4 August 1920 the council
leased the Shay estate to the football club** for seven years, and the first
football was played there on 3 September 1921.

**The club has never owned the freehold.** In **1987 the council bought the lease
back**, and from that point Halifax have been straightforward council tenants.
The rent — disclosed through a Freedom of Information request — is **£56,732.40
a year**, the same as the rugby league club pays.

The unfinished stand of 2002 was finished with public money. Work on the East
Stand began in the summer of 2000 and stopped when the club ran out of cash;
**Calderdale Council paid £4.5m to complete it**, taking the ground towards a
planned 11,541 and producing a stand of about 4,000 with hospitality behind it.
So the ground that bankrupted the club in 2002 was rebuilt by the landlord, and
the club went on paying rent to play in it.

Since 2024 the council has been trying to get rid of it. Selling The Shay would
save it around **£161,000 a year from 2025/26**. In **March 2025 the cabinet
resolved to dispose of the stadium to the owner of Huddersfield Giants for a
nominal sum**, in exchange for upgrades to Super League standard, continued
tenancy for both clubs and a covenant preserving sports use. **On 1 June 2026 he
withdrew** — after the council changed hands at the May elections and the
decision was put under review, with a threatened legal challenge and sustained
opposition from a section of supporters. He had already lent both tenants,
interest-free, their share of an £800,000 new pitch. Rival proposals have come
from a rugby-led consortium, from Halifax FC Women and from community groups.
The council is, in its own words, exploring options.

And the co-tenant has now done what the football club did. **Halifax Panthers
were wound up by the High Court in February 2026 on an HMRC petition**, lost
their governing-body membership and their place in the competition, and were
replaced by a new company with new directors — the same shape of event, in the
same building, eighteen years later.

Which is the sentence this page ends on. The Shay is a municipal tip that became
a municipal stadium, leased to a football club since 1920, bought back by the
council in 1987, rebuilt with council money, offered away for nothing in 2025
and still council property. Both of its tenants have been liquidated. **The
ground has outlived the clubs.**

One note on the data. This site keeps all of it — Halifax Town AFC's fifty
seasons and FC Halifax Town's thirteen — under a single identifier, which is
this database's normal convention for a phoenix club and the reason the 2008
deduction appears against the current club's name. The two are legally
distinct. Where the table says Halifax "stayed up" in 2008, that is a statement
about where they finished, not about what happened to them.
