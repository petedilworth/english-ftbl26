## Origins

Today's Barnet FC traces a genuinely tangled lineage rather than one clean
founding. Woodville FC (1882) became New Barnet FC (1885), then Barnet FC
(1888) — the club that gives today's side its popular founding date —
before folding in 1902. Local rivals Avenue FC took over the Barnet name
and ground, then merged in 1912 with Barnet Alston, a works team playing
in black and amber, reverting to the name Barnet FC in 1919. The nickname
"the Bees" is widely attributed to that amber-and-black kit together with
beehives that reportedly stood near the club's Underhill ground in the
early twentieth century, though no single documented source confirms the
story outright.

## Trajectory

Barnet's off-pitch history has turned less on points deductions or formal
insolvency — none are confirmed — than on ground standards and geography.
A severe financial crisis in 1992–93 under chairman Stan Flashman left
wages unpaid, brought a £50,000 Football League fine, and forced a
tribunal to void player contracts after a near-expulsion vote; accounts
differ on whether the club formally entered administration or was rescued
just short of it. A longer-running problem was Underhill's pitch, whose
pronounced slope fell increasingly short of Football League ground-grading
standards as they tightened, contributing to the club's eventual move.
That move, to a new stadium built in the neighbouring borough of Harrow in
2013, came with its own regulatory catch: the site's lease initially
barred Football League fixtures outright, a restriction lifted only by a
2015 variation timed just ahead of the club's promotion that year.

## Ownership & Finance

Businessman Tony Kleanthous has owned Barnet since 1994, funding and
building the club's current stadium before buying its freehold from the
London Borough of Harrow in 2018. His daughter, Natasha Kleanthous, was
appointed chief executive of the "Bees Football Group" in 2024, though
ownership itself remains with her father. The cost of the move away from
Barnet has been a recurring theme: local reporting has put the value of
lost borough revenue and support at around £1 million a year, and
Kleanthous has pledged to personally fund a proposed new stadium back in
Barnet to end what he has publicly called a "10-year exile."

## Infrastructure & Environment

Underhill Stadium was Barnet's home for 106 years, from 1907 to 2013,
before the club left for a new, purpose-built stadium some six miles away
in Edgware — a move the club's own chairman has since described as an
exile from the borough that gives it its name. The move followed a
breakdown in lease talks with Barnet council and the ground-grading
shortfall described above; Underhill was later demolished and replaced by
a school. The new stadium's pitch is natural grass, not artificial —
despite its site hosting separate 3G training pitches used for hire and
academy football, which has led to some confusion with genuine
3G-related grading disputes at other clubs. A proposed return to a new
stadium near the old Underhill site has faced local planning setbacks
since being announced in 2024.
