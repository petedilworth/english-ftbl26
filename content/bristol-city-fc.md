## Origins

Bristol City F.C. traces its roots to Bristol South End, a club formed on
12 April 1894 following a split within an earlier Bedminster club and the
disbanding of Bristol South AFC; the new club played at St John's Lane,
Bedminster. In 1897, on turning professional and joining the Southern
League, it was renamed Bristol City. The club is nicknamed "the Robins," a
name that displaced earlier nicknames — "the Garibaldians," for the red
shirts resembling Garibaldi's followers, then "Bristol Babes" from 1926 —
and became established by the 1940s, popularly associated with the 1920s
song "When the Red, Red Robin (Comes Bob, Bob, Bobbin' Along)."

## Trajectory

The defining off-pitch crisis in Bristol City's history came in early 1982,
when the club — in the Second Division relegation zone — carried debts of
around £850,000 and was said to be losing roughly £4,000 a week, putting it
hours from ceasing to trade. On the morning of 3 February 1982, with the
club due to stop trading at midday, eight first-team players — since known
as the "Ashton Gate Eight" — voluntarily tore up contracts worth a combined
£290,000, cutting the club's liabilities to roughly £100,000, in exchange
for a settlement of around £10,000 each plus proceeds from a benefit match.
The old company was wound up and the club continued play under a newly
formed company, Bristol City FC (1982) Ltd, with a new board; sporting
continuity was unbroken throughout, distinguishing this from a full
phoenix-club refoundation. The team went on to suffer relegation in three
consecutive seasons around this period, ending up in the Fourth Division.

## Ownership & Finance

After the 1982 restructuring, Bristol City passed through a period of more
conventional local-business ownership before Steve Lansdown — a
Bristol-born entrepreneur and co-founder of the wealth-management firm
Hargreaves Lansdown — became involved with the club from 1996, going on to
become chairman in 2002. He stepped back from the chairman title in 2021,
handing it to his son Jon Lansdown, while remaining the club's ultimate
owner. Lansdown's investment vehicle, Bristol Sport, also funds Bristol
Rugby, Bristol City W.F.C., Bristol Flyers basketball and a motorsport
operation — a multi-sport benefactor group rather than a football
multi-club-ownership network.

## Infrastructure & Environment

Ashton Gate has been Bristol City's home for over a century. A major
redevelopment between 2014 and 2016 rebuilt three of the four stands and
added the three-tiered Lansdown Stand, raising overall capacity to
approximately 27,000.
