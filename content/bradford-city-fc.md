## Origins

Bradford City did not emerge from a football-specific founding but from a
code conversion: Manningham Football Club, an established Bradford rugby
league side founded in 1880, voted at its 1903 annual meeting to abandon
rugby league and adopt association football, prompted in part by a civic
push, led by local journalist James Whyte, to secure senior football for a
city then without a Football League club. The new club took over
Manningham's Valley Parade ground and inherited its claret-and-amber
colours. The "Bantams" nickname followed a few years later, around 1908,
drawn from the resemblance of the claret-and-amber kit to bantam plumage,
and was made the club's official identity with a bantam crest in 1981.

## Trajectory

On 11 May 1985, a fire broke out in Valley Parade's wooden main stand
during a match against Lincoln City, killing 56 people. The Popplewell
Inquiry, convened under the Safety of Sports Grounds Act 1975, found the
fire most likely started from a discarded cigarette or match igniting
rubbish that had accumulated beneath the stand's timber structure over
years — a finding about the stand's construction and maintenance rather
than any dispute over cause — and fed directly into the era of
stadium-safety reform in English football. Roughly fifteen years later,
financial overreach caused a separate crisis: after Premier League survival
in 1999-2000, chairman Geoffrey Richmond sanctioned a heavy burst of
transfer and wage spending on established top-flight players that he
himself later described as "six weeks of madness." Relegation in 2001, the
collapse of the ITV Digital broadcasting deal, and a failed player sale
left the club around £36m in debt, leading to administration in May 2002
and a second administration in 2004.

## Ownership & Finance

Through the 2002 and 2004 crises, ownership passed from Geoffrey Richmond
to Gordon Gibb and then, after the second administration, to Julian Rhodes,
who took the club out of administration in December 2004 with support from
a supporters' fundraising campaign. Ownership remained UK-based until 2016,
when German investors Stefan Rupp and Edin Rahic completed a takeover
reported at around £5.5m. Rahic departed in December 2018, leaving Rupp as
sole owner from 2019 via his holding company ER Sports Group GmbH.

## Infrastructure & Environment

Valley Parade dates to 1886, originally built for Manningham FC's rugby
league side and inherited by the football club at its 1903 founding.
Following the 1985 fire, the club played home matches away from Valley
Parade during rebuilding — initially at Leeds United's and Huddersfield
Town's grounds, then based at Odsal, home of the Bradford rugby league club
— before a rebuilt, safety-compliant Valley Parade reopened in December
1986.
