## Origins

The original Bradford (Park Avenue) A.F.C. was voted out of the Football
League in 1970 and spent its final four seasons in the Northern Premier
League, selling its Park Avenue ground in 1973 and ground-sharing with
Bradford City amid mounting debts. On 3 May 1974, shareholders voted to
liquidate the company, with reported debts of £57,652, ending 67 years of
senior football under that name. Supporters moved almost immediately to
keep the identity alive, registering the club name and restarting as an
amateur Sunday League side in the old club's colours; that continuation was
relaunched as a semi-professional club later in the 1980s, from which point
it began climbing the non-league pyramid as the club known today.

## Infrastructure & Environment

A brief return to what remained of the old Park Avenue site was cut short
when an indoor cricket centre was built on part of the pitch, forcing a
nomadic spell — including a ground-share with Batley Rugby League Club at
Mount Pleasant — before Horsfall Stadium, which had opened in 1931 as an
athletics venue for Airedale Harriers, was upgraded for football and became
the club's settled home in 1996.
