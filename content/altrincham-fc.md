## Origins

Altrincham's roots trace to around 1891, when a Sunday school connection in
Broadheath produced the Rigby Memorial Club, which soon merged with another
local side, Grapplers, to form Broadheath F.C. — a founder member of the
Manchester League in 1893. The club moved to Pollitt's Field in 1903 and
took the name Altrincham Football Club.

## Trajectory

The club's defining off-pitch story is its exclusion from the Football
League despite dominating non-league football at its peak: as founder
members of the Alliance Premier League (now the National League),
Altrincham won the title in both 1979–80 and 1980–81, but under the
re-election system then in force, promotion depended on a vote of existing
Football League chairmen rather than being automatic. In 1980 Altrincham
lost that vote by a single point, 25 to Rochdale's 26 — reportedly because
two chairmen who had pledged support failed to cast their votes after a
mix-up — and lost again the following year as reigning champions. Episodes
like these are widely credited with the pressure that led the League to
abolish re-election in favour of automatic promotion from 1986–87, closing
off the exact obstacle that had twice denied Altrincham. The club also came
close to financial collapse around 2001 under roughly £750,000 of debt,
stabilised by a new board without entering formal administration.

## Ownership & Finance

Altrincham is currently led by Mark Luby, who became the club's largest
shareholder after joining an investor group in a 2025 funding round and was
appointed executive chairman that July, following a succession of chairmen
stretching back to Geoff Goodwin — credited with steering the club through
its 2001 financial difficulties — and Grahame Rowley. A supporters' trust
has existed since the early 2000s as a fundraising and fan-voice body,
though ownership has remained with individual benefactors rather than
passing to the trust.

## Infrastructure & Environment

The club moved to Moss Lane in 1910 after its previous ground, Pollitt's
Field, suffered chronic drainage problems and the local council sought to
redevelop the site. Moss Lane — commercially known as the J. Davidson
Stadium since a 2013 sponsorship deal — is leased from Trafford Council
rather than owned by the club, and the pitch has been well regarded enough
that Manchester United's own groundstaff have praised it after a pre-season
friendly there.
