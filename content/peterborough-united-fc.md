## Origins

Peterborough United was formed on 17 May 1934 at a public meeting in the
city's Angel Hotel, filling the void left when Peterborough & Fletton
United — itself a 1923 amalgamation built on Fletton United, founded 1901
— went into liquidation some nineteen months earlier. The new club took
over the London Road ground lease from the city council. Its nickname,
"the Posh," predates it by over a decade and outlived the club that first
carried it: it's traditionally attributed to Fletton United's manager,
Pat Tirrell, who is said to have remarked in 1921 that he was looking for
"posh players to form a posh team" — posh then simply meaning good. The
story has been repeated almost everywhere since, including by the club
itself, but a contemporary source for the exact words has never turned up,
and Peterborough United's own history page hedges it as "almost
certainly" true rather than settled fact.

## Trajectory

The club's clearest recent regulatory episode came in 2023, when the EFL
fined it £50,000 and suspended a three-point deduction until the end of
that year for allowing David Paton to act as chief executive without the
league's authorisation while he was subject to a disqualifying condition —
a governance breach, not a financial one. Money troubles have surfaced at
a few earlier points without tipping into confirmed formal insolvency: a
fan-funded "Posh Lifeline" campaign kept the club going through a 1982
crisis, and in 1996 a change of ownership headed off an owner's stated
intention to put the club into administration.

## Ownership & Finance

Darragh MacAnthony has controlled Peterborough United since taking it over
from Barry Fry for a nominal £1 in September 2006. In 2018 he sold half
the club to Canadian investors Jason Neale and Stewart Thompson; after
saying in 2022 he intended to sell up entirely, he instead bought back the
larger share of their stake in December 2023 and holds a majority again.
A separate financial strain surfaced the same year at one remove from the
football club itself — see Infrastructure & Environment.

## Infrastructure & Environment

Football has been played on the London Road site since at least 1891, and
it has been Peterborough United's only ground since 1934. The council
owned and leased it to the club for most of that time, until London Road
Peterborough Properties Ltd — a separate company co-owned by the club's
principal shareholders — bought the stadium and surrounding land from the
council in March 2021. That ownership vehicle, not the football club,
was placed into receivership in March 2023 over a £6.6m debt dispute with
a lender, and the dispute was settled by the end of that year without
affecting the club's own tenancy or playing status.
