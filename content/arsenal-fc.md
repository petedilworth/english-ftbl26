## Origins

Arsenal began in 1886 as Dial Square, formed by workers at the Royal
Arsenal armaments factory in Woolwich; the club was renamed Royal Arsenal
within weeks and became Woolwich Arsenal on turning professional in 1891.
By 1913 the club moved some twelve miles north to Highbury, Islington — not
a footballing decision but a financial one: Woolwich's poor transport links
and industrial, low-attendance catchment left the club unable to draw the
crowds a professional side needed, and chairman Henry Norris forced the
relocation to keep it solvent. The club's "Gunners" nickname and cannon
emblem both trace to that armaments-factory origin, though it was likely a
name first applied by rival towns' journalists and opposing fans rather
than one the club coined for itself.

## Trajectory

The club's defining off-pitch episode came in 1919, when the Football
League expanded the First Division from 20 to 22 clubs after the war and
had to fill the new places. Arsenal had finished only fifth in the Second
Division in the last full pre-war season, with no sporting claim to
promotion; Tottenham Hotspur, who had finished bottom of the First
Division, had the stronger case by precedent. At a League vote in March
1919, Arsenal were elected 18 votes to 8. Popular legend attributes the
result to Henry Norris buying support for the vote, but no contemporary
evidence of that has surfaced; what is documented is that Arsenal lobbied
hard through the trade press and were credited by other clubs with having
established League football in London. Whatever decided it, a vote rather
than the league table put Arsenal in the top flight, and the club has never
left it since — and the episode is the root of the Arsenal–Tottenham
rivalry.

A different kind of reputation was earned rather than voted on. Under
manager George Graham, Arsenal won the 1990-91 league title losing only
once all season and conceding just 18 goals in 38 matches, a defensive
record opposition supporters mocked with the chant "boring, boring
Arsenal." Arsenal's own fans reclaimed the jibe almost immediately,
singing it back with pride alongside "one-nil to the Arsenal" as Graham's
teams ground out result after result; both chants have resurfaced
ironically among Arsenal supporters in later, far more attacking eras of
the club.

## Ownership & Finance

Arsenal was boardroom- and family-controlled for most of the twentieth
century before floating shares in 1989. Kroenke Sports & Entertainment
first bought into the club in 2007 and built a majority stake by 2011,
triggering a mandatory takeover offer; a rival stake of around 30% held by
Alisher Usmanov blocked full consolidation for years until he sold to
Kroenke in 2018, after which the remaining minority shareholders were
compulsorily bought out and Arsenal went fully private under Kroenke's
ownership. The takeover-by-accumulation and Kroenke's reputation as a
largely hands-off owner have drawn sustained organised opposition from the
Arsenal Supporters' Trust, culminating in a large "Kroenke Out" protest
outside the stadium in April 2021 after the club's brief, aborted
involvement in the breakaway European Super League.

## Infrastructure & Environment

The move from Highbury was financed by roughly £260 million borrowed from a
bank consortium, with total project costs eventually put nearer £390–500
million; a 2004 naming-rights deal with Emirates, together with the sale of
the Highbury site for development, was central to repaying it. The new
ground opened as Emirates Stadium in 2006. Highbury itself was redeveloped
into flats by 2010, its listed Art Deco stands preserved as facades and its
pitch turned into a communal garden. The club had been displaced once
before: bombing destroyed Highbury's North Bank in 1941, and Arsenal played
home games at bitter rivals Tottenham's White Hart Lane from 1940 until
Highbury reopened in 1946.
