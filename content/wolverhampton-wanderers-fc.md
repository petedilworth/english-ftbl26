## Origins

Wolverhampton Wanderers began as St Luke's F.C., formed in 1877 by pupils
of St Luke's Church school in Blakenhall after headmaster Harry Barcroft
presented the boys with a football; John Baynton and John Brodie organised
the side. In 1879 the club merged with a local cricket and football club,
The Wanderers, adopting the combined name Wolverhampton Wanderers.

## Trajectory

Relegated from the Premier League in 2011-12 under chairman Steve Morgan,
Wolves went straight down again the following season, from the
Championship to League One — a rare two-divisions-in-two-seasons fall.
The recovery was just as fast: Kenny Jackett's side won League One in
2013-14 with a club-record 103 points, and after several settled seasons
in the Championship, Nuno Espírito Santo's side won the title in 2017-18
to return the club to the Premier League, where it has remained since.

## Ownership & Finance

Chinese conglomerate Fosun International bought Wolverhampton Wanderers
in July 2016, working closely from the outset with the Portuguese agent
Jorge Mendes — whose agency, Gestifute, Fosun also holds a stake in — a
relationship reflected in the club's recruitment of several
Mendes-represented Portuguese players and managers, including Nuno
Espírito Santo. Fosun remains the club's sole owner; an approach from
American businessman John Textor in late 2025, reportedly valuing the
club at more than £400m, was not accepted.

## Infrastructure & Environment

Molineux has been the club's home since 1889, with a current capacity of
around 31,750.
