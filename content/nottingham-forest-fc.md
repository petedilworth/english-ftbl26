## Origins

Nottingham Forest was founded in 1865, when a group of shinty players met
at the Clinton Arms and agreed to a proposal to switch to association
football instead. At that same meeting the club agreed to buy twelve
tasselled caps coloured "Garibaldi Red," after Giuseppe Garibaldi's Italian
redshirts, then popular in England — giving the club both its colours and
its original nickname, "the Garibaldi Reds." The modern nickname, "the
Tricky Trees," came much later.

## Trajectory

Under Fawaz Al-Hasawi's ownership from 2012, Forest settled into a long
Championship holding pattern, never seriously threatening promotion while
losses mounted; Al-Hasawi wrote off around £60m of the club's debts before
selling to Evangelos Marinakis in 2017. The breakthrough came in 2021-22:
manager Steve Cooper took over with the club bottom of the table, turned
the season around to a fourth-placed finish, beat Sheffield United on
penalties in the play-off semi-final, then won the play-off final 1-0
against Huddersfield Town at Wembley on 29 May 2022 — a first Premier
League return after 23 years away. The promotion was followed by a
record-breaking summer: more than twenty signings, and thirty across the
year, breaking the English record for a single transfer window, headlined
by a club-record fee for Morgan Gibbs-White. That spending, amortised
against Championship-level revenue from the two seasons before promotion,
became the direct cause of a financial-rules breach: in March 2024 an
independent commission docked Forest four points for exceeding the Premier
League's Profitability and Sustainability threshold by £34.5m over the
three years to 2022-23. Forest argued the £47.5m sale of Brennan Johnson
to Tottenham, completed just after the accounting deadline, should have
counted; the commission disagreed, and an appeal was rejected in full that
May. Steve Cooper was sacked in December 2023 after one win in thirteen
matches, and Nuno Espírito Santo was appointed in his place — first
steering the club away from relegation that season, then to seventh place
in 2024-25, Forest's first European qualification since 1995-96.

## Ownership & Finance

Evangelos Marinakis, a Greek shipping magnate who also owns Olympiacos and
Rio Ave, bought Nottingham Forest in May 2017 for a reported £50m. The
club's rapid rise created an unusual governance consequence: with Forest in
contention for a Champions League place in the 2024-25 season and
Olympiacos already qualified for 2025-26, UEFA's rule against one person
controlling two clubs in the same European competition forced Marinakis to
place his Forest shareholding into a blind trust. Companies House filings
confirm he ceased to be a "person of significant control" on 29 April
2025, with trustees taking a majority of the board in his place — a
temporary mechanism also used elsewhere in English football when
multi-club ownership collides with European qualification.

## Infrastructure & Environment

The City Ground opened on 3 September 1898, built on land belonging to
Nottingham City Council on the banks of the River Trent, with £2,000 of
its £3,000 construction cost raised from supporters, members and local
businessmen through bearer bonds. Now a Grade II listed building with a
capacity of around 30,445, the ground has become the focus of a
redevelopment push funded by the club's new Premier League and European
income, with plans for a rebuilt Peter Taylor Stand reported to cost as
much as £1bn.
