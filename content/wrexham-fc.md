## Origins

Wrexham was formed on 4 October 1864 at the Turf Hotel, next to the
Racecourse Ground, by members of Denbighshire County Cricket Club looking
for a way to keep the town's young men active through the winter — making
it one of the oldest football clubs in the world. For most of its first
seventy-five years the club played in blue and white and was informally
known as "the Sugar Bags," after the striped sugar packaging its kit
resembled; the modern nickname "the Red Dragons" was chosen by a fan
ballot organised by the club's commercial department in 2001.

## Trajectory

Wrexham spent 2008 to 2023 outside the Football League — fifteen seasons
in the National League that included repeated play-off near-misses,
among them a lost 2013 final to fellow Welsh club Newport County — before
Ryan Reynolds and Rob McElhenney's ownership finally ended the wait:
National League champions in 2022-23 with a record 111 points, promoted
from League Two in 2023-24, and promoted from League One in 2024-25 — the
first club ever to complete three straight promotions through the top
five tiers of English football, taking them from the fifth tier to the
Championship in three years.

## Ownership & Finance

Hollywood actor Ryan Reynolds and writer-producer Rob McElhenney completed
their takeover of Wrexham in February 2021, paying a reported £2m, and
the publicity around their ownership — including the Emmy-winning
documentary series "Welcome to Wrexham" — has been widely credited with
transforming the club's commercial position alongside its results on the
pitch. The Allyn Family Office bought a 15% stake in October 2024, and
Apollo Sports Capital, a sports-focused fund managed by Apollo Global
Management, took a minority stake in December 2025 to help fund the
club's stadium redevelopment and its push toward the Premier League.

## Infrastructure & Environment

The Racecourse Ground — commercially known as STōK Cae Ras since 2023 —
is the world's oldest international football stadium still in use. The
old Kop end was demolished in 2022-23 and replaced with a temporary
stand; construction of its permanent replacement began in December 2025
and is due for completion in early 2027, part of a redevelopment expected
to take the ground's capacity to around 18,000.
