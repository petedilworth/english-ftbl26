## Origins

Boston United was formed in 1933 as the direct successor to Boston F.C.
(itself a continuation of the earlier Boston Town club, founded 1894),
after Boston F.C.'s directors put the company into liquidation at the end
of the 1932-33 season when it could no longer generate enough revenue to
sustain Midland League football. A supporters' meeting that July resolved
to launch a new club to take up the same league place, continuing an
unbroken tradition of football on the York Street site. The nickname "the
Pilgrims," reportedly adopted around 1969 to replace the plain "United"
tag, draws on Boston, Lincolnshire's historic association with the Pilgrim
Fathers.

## Trajectory

The club's off-pitch history includes two distinct episodes. In 2002, FA
charges over irregular player contracts tied to the 2001-02 Conference
title win brought personal bans and fines for manager Steve Evans and
chairman Pat Malkinson, plus a four-point deduction applied to the club's
first Football League season rather than to the Conference campaign
itself — meaning Boston kept the promotion. Evans and Malkinson were later
also given suspended prison sentences over related tax offences. A more
unusual episode followed in 2007: relegated from League Two on the pitch
amid around £3.5 million of debt, the club entered a Company Voluntary
Arrangement that, at HM Revenue and Customs' insistence as a creditor, left
football creditors unpaid in full. Because that breached the Football
Conference's football creditors rule, the League's AGM voted to demote the
club a further division, to Conference North rather than Conference
National — a regulatory demotion layered on top of, and distinct from, the
sporting relegation and points penalty already applied that season.

## Ownership & Finance

The 2007 financial collapse brought the club close to liquidation before a
rescue takeover led by David Newton and Neil Kempster that summer. Newton
has remained chairman since, and has described the intervening years as
spent working through the debt inherited from the 2007 crisis.

## Infrastructure & Environment

Boston United played at York Street from the club's 1933 founding until the
end of the 2019-20 season, when the ground was vacated and later
demolished. The club relocated to a new-build stadium in Wyberton, part of
a mixed-use development led by chairman David Newton's own property
company, subsequently renamed the Jakemans Community Stadium under a
sponsorship deal with a local confectionery firm.
