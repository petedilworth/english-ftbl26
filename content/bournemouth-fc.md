## Origins

AFC Bournemouth traces its roots to 1899, when the club was formed from the
remains of Boscombe St John's Lads' Institute FC, an institute-linked club
active since around 1890. The reconstituted side, initially known as
Boscombe FC, is said to have been formally organised at a meeting held
under streetlights on Gladstone Road, Boscombe. The club took the name
Bournemouth & Boscombe Athletic FC in 1923 on joining the Football League,
before adopting its current name, AFC Bournemouth, in 1972. The "Cherries"
nickname dates to around 1910 and is generally attributed to the cherry
orchards that stood near the club's new Dean Court ground, reinforced by
the team's traditional cherry-red kit.

## Trajectory

The defining off-pitch crisis in the club's history was the February 2008
administration, triggered by an HMRC winding-up petition over roughly £4m
of accumulated debt. Entry into administration carried an automatic
10-point deduction that helped send the club down from League One to
League Two that season. The following season, because the exiting
administration process did not produce a creditor-approved Company
Voluntary Arrangement in time, the League imposed a further 17-point
deduction at the very start of 2008-09 — repeatedly cited as one of the
largest penalties ever handed to an English league club — and the club had
to accept it without appeal simply to keep playing. Starting the season 17
points in arrears, the club spent the campaign fighting relegation out of
the Football League altogether, with bailiffs reportedly visiting the club
every few days to seize property against unpaid debts and players unsure
when, or if, they would next be paid. Manager Jimmy Quinn was sacked in
January 2009 and replaced, initially on a caretaker basis, by 31-year-old
Eddie Howe — his first managerial job. Howe has since credited the
squad's togetherness for closing the gap match by match; Bournemouth
secured survival with a final-day win over Grimsby Town on 25 April 2009,
finishing 18th. The turnaround that followed was driven by a change in
ownership rather than any single transfer: Maxim Demin's investment from
2011 provided the sustained backing, under Howe, that underpinned the
club's climb through four divisions to the Premier League by 2015.

## Ownership & Finance

Russian businessman Maxim Demin became co-owner of AFC Bournemouth in 2011,
buying out the remaining stake of former chairman Eddie Mitchell in 2013 to
become sole owner; his backing underpinned the rise to the Premier League
in 2015. In December 2022, Demin sold the club to a consortium led by
American businessman Bill Foley, operating as Black Knight Football Club;
Foley's Cannae Holdings paid a reported $126m for a 50.1% stake in the
ownership partnership. Black Knight Football Club has since built a wider
multi-club portfolio including stakes in FC Lorient (France) and Hibernian
(Scotland), alongside Foley's separate, non-football sports holdings.

## Infrastructure & Environment

The club has played at the King's Park site since 1910, when landowner J.E.
Cooper-Dean leased the ground that became known as Dean Court; it was
completely rebuilt in 2001 and now operates commercially as the Vitality
Stadium, with a capacity of around 11,300 — among the smallest in Premier
League history. The club sold Dean Court in a sale-and-leaseback deal to
Structadene in December 2005 to address debt, then leased it back for
nearly two decades; in April 2025, under Black Knight ownership, the club
reacquired the stadium to clear the way for a major expansion project
targeting completion before the 2027-28 season.
