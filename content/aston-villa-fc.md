## Origins

Aston Villa was founded in 1874 by four members of the Villa Cross Wesleyan
Chapel's cricket team in Handsworth, Birmingham, who wanted a way to keep
playing through the winter; the club took its name from the chapel's
locality. Its most consequential early act came in 1888, when Villa
director William McGregor, frustrated by fixtures being cancelled at short
notice, convened representatives of England's leading clubs to guarantee a
regular schedule — directly founding the Football League, with Villa among
its twelve original members.

## Trajectory

The clearest modern off-pitch story is the financial decline under Randy
Lerner's later ownership: investment tailed off sharply in his final
seasons, revenue fell, net transfer spend across 2014–16 was under £15
million, and the club was relegated from the Premier League in 2015–16
after just three league wins, posting an £81 million loss that year. Lerner
sold at a reported loss of more than $400 million to Tony Xia in 2016.
Xia's ownership deteriorated faster still: by mid-2018 the club was
reportedly losing close to £5 million a month, and in June that year it
missed a deadline on a roughly £4 million HMRC tax bill, facing a
winding-up petition before negotiating a partial payment — media reporting
consistently describes this as narrowly avoiding administration, though the
exact closeness to formal insolvency isn't documented in public records.
The following month, Nassef Sawiris and Wes Edens' NSWE consortium bought a
controlling stake from Xia, completing a full buyout the following year.

## Ownership & Finance

Doug Ellis chaired the club across two spells totalling nearly three
decades before selling to Randy Lerner in 2006; Lerner's decade in charge
ended in 2016 amid the losses and relegation described above, and his sale
to Tony Xia's Recon Group was followed within two years by the HMRC crisis
that ended Xia's ownership. Nassef Sawiris and Wes Edens, trading as V
Sports, have owned the club since 2018–19; V Sports also holds stakes in
Vitória SC (Portugal), Real Unión (Spain) and a partnership with Vissel
Kobe (Japan), making Villa genuinely part of a multi-club group rather than
a single-club investment.

## Infrastructure & Environment

Villa played first at Aston Park, then at Wellington Road in Perry Barr —
the source of an old "Perry Barr Pets" nickname — before moving to Villa
Park in 1897, built on the site of a former pleasure park next to Aston
Hall. The club has owned and operated the ground ever since. In 2025 plans
were approved to expand the North Stand from around 5,000 to 12,000 seats,
lifting capacity to roughly 50,000 by the second half of 2027 ahead of
UEFA Euro 2028.
