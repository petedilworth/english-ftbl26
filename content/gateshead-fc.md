## Origins

**Three clubs have carried a Gateshead name, and the one playing now is not
continuous with the one that was in the Football League.** Every record-keeper
files them separately; so does this page.

**South Shields FC** were founded in 1899, elected to the Football League in
1919, and in **March 1930**, in financial trouble, **moved eight miles up the
Tyne from Horsley Hill to Redheugh Park and renamed themselves Gateshead AFC**.
They were in the League until 1960 and were wound up in **1973**.

**A second South Shields club, formed in 1936** expressly to replace the one
that had left, then did exactly the same thing. It sold its ground, was blocked
from returning to Horsley Hill, was left homeless, **moved to Gateshead in 1974
and became Gateshead United**. It lasted three seasons and dissolved at the end
of 1976/77.

**The present Gateshead FC was formed in 1977** to take Gateshead United's
Northern Premier League place, and won that league in 1983 with a record 100
points and 114 goals, and again in 1986.

The pattern is the whole of it: **twice, a South Shields club with no viable
ground of its own was moved up the river and renamed after the town it landed
in.** Both times the cause was property, not football. And present-day South
Shields FC was refounded in 1974 in the vacuum the second departure left, and
now plays one division below Gateshead.

## Trajectory

This site holds **two Football League seasons for Gateshead — 1958/59 and
1959/60 — and they are the last two.** The Third Division North era, where the
club spent essentially its whole League life averaging around ninth, is outside
the data entirely.

That matters, because the restructure is the cause nobody names. **The Third
Divisions North and South were abolished in 1958** and replaced by national
Third and Fourth Divisions, split on where clubs had finished. **A regional
mid-table club was deposited by an administrative reorganisation into the only
division with a re-election ballot at the bottom of it.** Two seasons later they
were out.

**The vote was at the League's AGM on 28 May 1960, and it was not close.**
Gateshead finished twenty-second of twenty-four on 33 points — **two places and
six points above Hartlepools United, who finished bottom.** The recorded ballot:

| | Votes |
|---|---|
| Oldham Athletic (23rd) | 39 |
| **Peterborough United** (non-league applicant) | **35** |
| Hartlepools United (24th, bottom) | 34 |
| Southport (21st) | 29 |
| **Gateshead (22nd)** | **18** |

**Gateshead polled eleven fewer votes than the club that finished bottom and
seventeen fewer than the non-league club that took their place.** The next
lowest survivor had 29. They were not narrowly unlucky; they were adrift by a
division's worth of votes. Peterborough had won the Midland League six times
including five in a row, so this was not a weak club sneaking in. And Gateshead
had not faced re-election since 1937 — this was their second-ever application,
and it killed them.

The travel-cost explanation is universal in the retrospective accounts and it is
commentary rather than a recorded reason: no minute or statement of reasons has
surfaced, and the phrasing is always hedged. What can be said is that this
site's own figures support the premise. **Gateshead's career mean distance to
divisional opponents is 184.2 miles, the third highest of any club with
twenty-five seasons or more** — behind only Plymouth and Torquay, and ahead of
Carlisle. In twenty-four of their twenty-six fifth-tier seasons their nearest
divisional opponent has been more than sixty-five miles away.

One correction, because it is the obvious claim to reach for. **Gateshead were
not the only club voted out of the Football League without finishing bottom.**
Barrow went in 1972 from twenty-second, eight points clear of the bottom club —
a bigger cushion — and Southport went in 1978 from twenty-third. Gateshead's
real distinction is being **the earliest of the three, and the furthest adrift
in the ballot itself.**

Then twenty-three years absent from this data, and since 1983 a fifth-tier club:
**twenty-six seasons at that level, no promotions at all, three relegations out
of it, and a best finish of third in 2013/14.** Zero promotions across
twenty-eight recorded seasons is the sharpest number on the page.

## Ownership & Finance

**In May 2019 the National League did not merely dock Gateshead nine points. It
threw them out.**

The disciplinary findings ran to four: breaching rules on **financial
reporting** and on **permitted loans**, **defaulting on football creditors**,
and **failing to obtain security of tenure over their ground.** The sanctions
were **nine points from the 2018/19 season, a £3,500 fine, suspension from the
competition and refusal of a licence for 2019/20.** Gateshead appealed to the
FA; the appeal failed, and the demotion to National League North was confirmed
at the League's AGM on **8 June 2019**. **Aldershot Town, who had finished
twenty-first, were reprieved** from what would have been a fourth relegation
place.

The nine points cost eight places — this site's match data puts their on-pitch
total at 66, which would have been ninth; they finished seventeenth. But the
deduction did not relegate them. The expulsion did, and **this site's data
records their 2018/19 status as "Stayed"**, because the demotion was
administrative and there is no field for it. The table is not wrong; it simply
cannot see what happened.

Note the fourth finding, because it is the whole of the next section: **failure
to obtain security of tenure over the ground was already a formal regulatory
breach in 2019, five years before it cost them a play-off place.** The 2019 and
2024 stories are the same story.

The one-point deduction of **March 2023** is the small sibling: an independent
panel found a breach of rule 6.9 for failing to ensure a player was free of
suspension, with a £750 fine. The club called it an administrative error and did
not appeal. It cost them one place at season's end, thirteenth to fourteenth.

**On the ownership itself this page has to stop.** There was an ownership
dimension to the 2019 crisis, it was contested in public, a takeover completed
around the middle of that year, and this site's finance table holds no rows for
Gateshead at all. None of the names, dates or share transactions could be
established to the standard this site applies to disputes involving named
owners, and none are asserted here. That the accounts are absent is itself
typical — of the clubs currently in the fifth tier, only those with Football
League histories have any figures on this site.

## Infrastructure & Environment

Gateshead play in an **athletics stadium owned by Gateshead Council**, with a
running track between the stands, and have done since 1971/72. **Redheugh Park —
which the club had been in dispute with the same council over, amid accusations
of unpaid rent and poor maintenance that the club denied — closed in 1971.** A
club in dispute with its council landlord over its ground, going to a ballot of
ninety-two chairmen, is the shape of 1960; it is also the shape of 2024.

**In 2024 Gateshead finished sixth and were barred from the play-offs.** Three
distinctions matter and all three are usually got wrong. **It was the EFL's
decision, not the National League's** — the National League said it was
extremely disappointed and had written to the council in support. **It was
exclusion from the play-offs, not refusal of promotion.** And **the criterion
was security of tenure, not ground grading**: to join the EFL a club must show
it holds its ground for at least ten full seasons after promotion. Nothing
physical about the stadium was in question.

The council's position was not obstruction so much as timing. It could not grant
a ten-year interest in an asset whose operation it was about to hand to someone
else — it was midway through appointing a partner operator for its leisure
facilities. It refused before the **1 March** deadline, offered alternatives on
**28 March** that the EFL judged insufficient, and the exclusion was confirmed on
**21 April**. The club appealed on the ground that the decision was irrational
or unreasonable; **an independent arbitrator rejected it**, and supporters
protested the following day.

It was then fixed. A letter of guarantee was agreed in late 2024 and **a
ten-year security of tenure in the event of promotion was approved**; operation
of the stadium transferred to a leisure trust in April 2025 with the club
remaining as tenants. So Gateshead belong on this site's ground-grading theme
page, and belong there with an explanation, because they are a different
instrument from the pure cases: a membership criterion about property law rather
than a grading of the ground.

And then the thing next door. **Newcastle United's ground is 2.33 miles from
Gateshead's, and the two clubs have never played a league match** — not once in
160,988 matches in this data. Gateshead have never played Sunderland either
(7.85 miles) or Middlesbrough (30.0). **Their only North East league opponents
in the entire record are Hartlepool, fourteen times, and Darlington, eight.**

The mechanism is clean: Newcastle's range in this data is tiers one and two,
Gateshead's is four and five, and the windows never overlapped historically
either — South Shields were in Division Two while Newcastle were in Division
One, and by the time Newcastle came down in 1934 the club had moved to Gateshead
and dropped to the Third Division North.

It is **not**, quite, the closest such pair. **Droylsden and Manchester City are
2.03 miles apart and have also never met** — though Droylsden appear in a single
season and City's stored ground only opened in 2003. Gateshead and Newcastle are
a twenty-eight-season-against-sixty-eight-season non-meeting, two and a third
miles apart, which is the more remarkable object even if it is second on the
list.
