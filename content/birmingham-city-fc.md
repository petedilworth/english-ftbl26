## Origins

Birmingham City began in September 1875 as Small Heath Alliance, formed by
members of a cricket team based at Holy Trinity Church in Bordesley who
wanted a winter pastime; the club played on waste ground off Arthur
Street before moving to its first enclosed home, Muntz Street, in 1877. It
turned professional and became Small Heath in 1888 — the first football
club to incorporate as a limited company — then Birmingham in 1905 and
Birmingham City in 1943. The "Blues" nickname comes simply from the royal
blue shirts worn since the earliest years; no better-sourced origin story
exists.

## Trajectory

The club's defining off-pitch story is the ownership of Hong Kong
businessman Carson Yeung, who took control in 2009. Arrested in 2011,
Yeung was convicted in a Hong Kong court in March 2014 on five counts of
laundering roughly HK$720m (about £55m) through his accounts between 2001
and 2007, sentenced to six years, and lost a final appeal in 2016; the
club spent his imprisonment run by a succession of caretaker executives
before ownership passed on later that year. More recently, the EFL
deducted nine points in March 2019 under its Profitability and
Sustainability rules, after the club recorded losses of about £48.8m over
the previous three years against a permitted threshold of £39m — enough to
drop Birmingham from 13th to 18th in the Championship overnight. A
smaller, suspended two-point deduction followed in 2023 for a different
kind of breach: allowing individuals to acquire control of the club
without the EFL's prior approval, a governance failure rather than a
financial one. An older footnote: Small Heath won the Second Division
outright in 1892–93 but were denied automatic promotion under the "Test
Match" play-off system then in use, winning promotion the following year
by beating Darwen in a deciding match — a different mechanism from the
modern ground-grading rules, but the same basic idea that a title alone
didn't guarantee promotion.

## Ownership & Finance

The Yeung era left the club in prolonged disorder — an imprisoned,
absentee owner, a parent company suspended from the Hong Kong stock
exchange, and boardroom turmoil that continued through a further ownership
change in 2016. The stadium itself was separated from the football club
during this period: St Andrew's was sold off in stakes between December
2020 and April 2021, leaving the club paying roughly £1.25m a year in rent
on a 12-year lease to the new owners. Resolution came from Knighthead
Capital Management, a US investment firm led by Tom Wagner, which took a
controlling stake and reunited club and stadium in July 2023, then
completed a full buyout of the remaining shareholding in November 2025.

## Infrastructure & Environment

St Andrew's opened in December 1906 to replace the outgrown Muntz Street
ground, and the club bought its freehold outright in 1921, before the
2020–2023 separation described above. Knighthead has since set out plans
for a considerably larger move: a 48-acre former industrial site nearby,
bought for around £100m in 2024, earmarked for a 62,000-capacity stadium
at the centre of a "Birmingham Sports Quarter" development. As of the most
recent reporting this remains at the design and consultation stage, with
no planning application yet submitted, and an ambition rather than a
guarantee to open by the 2030–31 season.
