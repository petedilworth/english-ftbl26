## Origins

Luton Town's roots trace to Luton Wanderers, which renamed itself Luton
Town at a secret committee meeting in January 1885; a public meeting at
the Town Hall that April, called by John Charles Lomax and George Deacon,
ratified the new club by an overwhelming vote. In 1891 Luton became the
first fully professional club in the south of England and a founding
member of the Southern Football League. The nickname "the Hatters"
reflects the town's centuries-old straw-plait hat-making industry.

## Trajectory

Luton's modern history is defined by a collapse and an unusually long
recovery. Chairman David Pinkney put the club into administration in
November 2007; the club was docked 10 points that season and, in June
2008, a further 30 — 10 from the FA over irregular payments to a former
manager and agents, and 20 from the Football League after the club failed
to agree a Company Voluntary Arrangement with creditors, a penalty applied
without any right of appeal and still the largest ever imposed on an EFL
club. Relegated to the Conference in 2009 — the first time in their
history outside the Football League — Luton spent five seasons there,
reaching the play-offs twice before John Still's side won the title
outright in 2013-14 with a record 101 points. Back-to-back promotions in
2018 and 2019 then took the club from League Two to the Championship, and
in 2023 they reached the Premier League for the first time via the
play-offs. That stay lasted one season: relegated in 2024 and again in
2025, Luton were back in League One within two years of playing top-flight
football, finishing 2025-26 in mid-table there.

## Ownership & Finance

Consortium Luton 2020, fronted by broadcaster Nick Owen, bought the club
in 2008 in the midst of the administration crisis and has owned it ever
since — closing in on two decades of stability that spans the club's full
arc from non-league exile to the Premier League and back. David Wilkinson
serves as chairman.

## Infrastructure & Environment

Luton have played at Kenilworth Road since 1905, one of the smallest and
most idiosyncratic grounds in English football: away supporters reach the
Oak Road Stand through turnstiles built directly into a row of terraced
houses, then climb a narrow staircase that runs through what feels like
the homes' own back gardens. A new 25,000-capacity stadium at Power Court,
in Luton town centre, began main construction in January 2026 and is
scheduled to open for the 2028-29 season; Kenilworth Road is expected to
be demolished once the move is complete.
