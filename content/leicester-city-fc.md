## Origins

Leicester City was founded in 1884 as Leicester Fosse F.C., taking its
name from a field the club rented off Fosse Road South, itself named for
the Roman road nearby. Early fans knew the side as "the Fossils"; when
the club renamed itself Leicester City in 1919, coinciding with the city
being granted official city status, the nickname changed too, settling
on "the Foxes" in reference to Leicestershire's long association with
fox hunting.

## Trajectory

Leicester's off-pitch history has twice followed the same shape: a
relegation, an immediate promotion back, and a second relegation before
the underlying finances caught up. The first came in 2002: relegated
from the Premier League the same year the club moved into its new,
£37m Walkers Stadium and the ITV Digital broadcasting deal collapsed,
Leicester entered administration in October 2002 with debts of around
£30m. A consortium fronted by former player Gary Lineker helped bring
the club out of administration via a company voluntary arrangement in
February 2003; promotion followed that same season, but the club went
back down again in 2004.

The second instance came over two decades later, either side of the
club's financial position being transformed by the 2015-16 Premier
League title and the Champions League football that followed it.
Relegated in 2023, Leicester won the Championship outright in 2023-24
and returned to the Premier League — only to be relegated again in
2024-25. The following season, back in the Championship, the club was
docked six points after an EFL commission found it had breached
Profitability and Sustainability rules during that promotion-winning
campaign, exceeding the permitted three-year loss threshold by £20.8m,
a sanction upheld on appeal in February 2026. A separate Premier League
case covering an earlier assessment period was dismissed on jurisdiction
grounds, an appeal board ruling that the Premier League no longer had
standing to pursue it once the club had left the division.

## Ownership & Finance

Thai duty-free retailer King Power, led by Vichai Srivaddhanaprabha,
bought Leicester City from Milan Mandarić in August 2010, immediately
writing off around £103m of the club's existing loans and buying the
stadium outright on the club's behalf. Vichai died in a helicopter crash
just outside King Power Stadium in October 2018, minutes after a home
match; his son, Aiyawatt "Top" Srivaddhanaprabha, has chaired the club
since.

## Infrastructure & Environment

Leicester played at Filbert Street from 1891 until 2002, by the end
holding around 22,000 having once held a record attendance of 47,298.
The club moved, less than 300 yards away, into the newly built Walkers
Stadium in July 2002, opened by club icon Gary Lineker; renamed the King
Power Stadium in 2011, it holds 32,259.
