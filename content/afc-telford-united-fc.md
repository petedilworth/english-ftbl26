## Origins

Telford United F.C. traced back to Wellington Town F.C., founded in 1872
and renamed Telford United in 1969 as the new town grew up around it. In
2003–04, playing in the Football Conference under sole owner Andy Shaw, the
club was destabilised by the collapse of Shaw's own business; it entered
administration in April 2004 and was liquidated the following month,
ending 132 years of history, with the club's own manager saying at the
time he couldn't see a benefactor coming forward to save it. None did.
Supporters raised roughly £50,000 in two months through pub collections
instead, forming Telford United Supporters Limited, which on the day of
liquidation announced a new, wholly fan-owned club — AFC Telford United —
run on £1 shares and built explicitly to avoid a repeat of one person's
finances being able to sink it again. It took up the newly built stadium
on the same site and adopted the motto *numquam obliviscere* — "never
forget".

## Trajectory

The reformed club's off-pitch history has been one of recurring cash-flow
strain rather than a repeat collapse. A 2015 members' consultation had
already floated letting outside investors buy up to 20% of the club each;
a fresh financial crisis in 2016, needing £50,000 raised in two monthly
instalments, forced the Supporters Trust to act on it that October, ending
twelve years of pure fan ownership. A 2017 approach from an Irish-led
consortium seeking a 51% stake was turned down by the board over concerns
it would raise running costs with no clear funding plan behind it. The
club's only points deduction, three points in April 2015, was an
administrative rather than financial matter — fielding a player whose loan
paperwork had not been submitted in time — and contributed to relegation
from the Conference that season.

## Ownership & Finance

AFC Telford United began in 2004 as a trust-owned club in direct reaction
to the club it replaced: rather than risk another single benefactor's
fortunes, ownership sat with Telford United Supporters Limited on £1 fan
shares, with no outside investors. That model held until October 2016,
when financial strain forced the trust to open the club to outside buyers,
capped at 20% of the shares each. The two largest stakes since have both
traced back to people already embedded in the club rather than newcomers:
AFC Telford Holdings Ltd, now around 46%, is controlled by the Pryce
family, whose involvement dates to 2004 when Win Pryce helped found the
club and served as vice-chairman until his death in 2017 — his son Andy
chaired the club from 2016 to January 2024. Local businessman Walter
Gleeson bought a 14% stake in 2022, and a US consortium fronted by film
financier Chris Ball bought 21% in February 2024 — a stake that, for
reasons not publicly explained, exceeded the original 20% single-buyer
cap. Roughly a fifth of the shares remain unaccounted for among smaller
holders. The original Supporters Trust no longer appears among the club's
shareholders; a separate fan body, the Telford United Supporters
Association, exists today as a fundraising and social group rather than an
ownership vehicle.

## Infrastructure & Environment

The club plays at New Bucks Head in Wellington, built in 2000–2003 on the
site used by Wellington Town and Telford United for over a century, and
has played nowhere else since its 2004 formation. Capacity is around
6,300, with cover on three sides. Since 2024 the ground has carried
sponsor branding tied to a local mental-health charity, though New Bucks
Head remains its established name.
