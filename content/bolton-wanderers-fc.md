## Origins

Bolton Wanderers began in June 1874 as Christ Church F.C., founded by Rev.
Thomas Ogden, a schoolmaster at Christ Church, Deane Road, and run out of
the church. In August 1877 the club broke away from the church — commonly
attributed to a dispute with the vicar, Rev. John Farrall Wright — and
renamed itself Bolton Wanderers, "Wanderers" reportedly reflecting its
early difficulty securing a settled ground; it used three different venues
in its first four years. The nickname "the Trotters" has no single
confirmed origin: competing local folklore variously attributes it to an
old nickname for a Bolton practical joker, a culinary story about pig or
sheep trotters sold near an early ground, or a piggery adjoining a ground
that players had to cross to retrieve the ball.

## Trajectory

By spring 2019, under then-owner Ken Anderson, the club had stopped paying
players and staff, prompting a players' food bank and a threatened match
boycott, while HM Revenue and Customs pursued a High Court winding-up
petition over an unpaid tax bill of around £1.2 million. A proposed sale to
Laurence Bassini repeatedly stalled on proof of funding, which Anderson
cited as leaving the club "no choice" but to enter administration; joint
administrators were formally appointed on 13 May 2019. The automatic EFL
insolvency penalty of 12 points was applied at the start of the following
season, in League One. Separately, the EFL also handed the club a
suspended five-point penalty over postponed and abandoned fixtures against
Brentford and Doncaster during the crisis, though that sanction was never
actually enforced.

## Ownership & Finance

Football Ventures (Whites) Limited, fronted by Sharon Brittan alongside
business partner Michael James, was announced as preferred bidder for the
stricken club on 1 July 2019. The takeover was disrupted by a rival court
challenge from Laurence Bassini that August before completing on 28 August
2019. No evidence has emerged of Football Ventures owning stakes in other
football clubs; its related entities are Bolton-specific football and
hotel operating subsidiaries rather than part of a multi-club structure.

## Infrastructure & Environment

The club has played at its current ground since 1997, when it opened as
the Reebok Stadium; it has carried several sponsor names since — Macron
Stadium from 2014, University of Bolton Stadium from 2018, and Toughsheet
Community Stadium from 2023 — while capacity has held at around 28,723
throughout.
