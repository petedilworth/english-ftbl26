## Origins

The original Aldershot F.C. was founded in 1926 and elected to the Football
League in 1932, but decades of financial strain culminated in a High Court
winding-up in March 1992 — its second, after a 1990 rescue by a 19-year-old
property developer had failed — forcing the club to resign from the League
mid-season with its results for the year expunged. Within weeks, a
committee of local fans and businessmen formed Aldershot Town F.C., managed
by a former Aldershot F.C. player, and took up the vacant Recreation
Ground.

## Trajectory

Aldershot Town's rise back into the Football League was built on non-league
promotion rather than any inherited safety net: the club won its way up
through the Isthmian League and the Conference, taking the Conference title
outright in 2007–08 to return to League football for the first time since
the original club's 1992 collapse. That return proved short-lived —
relegated from League Two at the end of 2012–13, the club entered
administration just five days later with debts of around £1.4 million, and
began the following season with a ten-point deduction, the standard
sanction for an insolvency event, in the Conference Premier.

## Ownership & Finance

Aldershot Town has been run under benefactor rather than fan-trust
ownership, despite a supporters' trust having pushed for a community
takeover during the 2013 administration — a push that did not succeed, with
a consortium led by Shahid Azeem taking control instead when the club
exited administration that August. Azeem remained the club's
longest-serving chairman until January 2026, when he wrote off his
outstanding loans and transferred his shares at no cost to fellow director
Deane Wood, who became the new owner.

## Infrastructure & Environment

The club has played at the Recreation Ground — the same ground the original
Aldershot F.C. used from 1927 — since its first match in 1992, giving it
continuity of home venue despite the discontinuity of the club itself. The
ground remains council-owned rather than club-owned, but a 2023 deal
replaced the short leases the club had held throughout its history with a
118-year lease at a nominal £1 a year, also bringing adjoining land under
the club's control for future redevelopment.
