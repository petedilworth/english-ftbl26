## Origins

Sunderland was founded in October 1879 as Sunderland and District Teachers'
Association Football Club, formed by schoolteacher James Allan; the club
dropped "Teachers" from its name within a year once it opened up to
non-academics. The "Black Cats" nickname is older than its official
adoption: the club's early home stood near the Black Cat Battery on the
Wear, giving Sunderland an unofficial "Black Cats" tag almost from
inception, reinforced in 1937 when a 12-year-old fan, Billy Morris,
smuggled a black cat into Wembley as a good-luck charm for that year's FA
Cup final win. The club only formally embraced it as its official
nickname in February 2000, following a fan vote that retired the older
"Rokerites" and "Team of All Talents" tags.

## Trajectory

Sunderland's defining modern story is a collapse and recovery both
unusually fast and unusually well documented. Relegated from the Premier
League in 2016-17 under David Moyes with just 24 points from 38 games, the
club kept sliding rather than rebuilding: three permanent managers across
2017-18 — Simon Grayson, sacked in November with the club 22nd, then Chris
Coleman — presided over a second straight relegation, dropping Sunderland
into the third tier for the first time since 1987-88. Owner Ellis Short,
who had stopped funding the club at the level managers needed to compete,
agreed that summer to let Netflix film behind the scenes in the hope it
would attract investors; the result, "Sunderland 'Til I Die," became a
three-season documentary charting the immediate aftermath of relegation
(season one), the chaos of a new ownership group under Stewart Donald and
Charlie Methven (season two), and the eventual return to promotion form
under Kyril Louis-Dreyfus (season three). Louis-Dreyfus, then 23, completed
his takeover in February 2021 with Sunderland seventh in League One; the
club won promotion via the play-offs the following season — the fourth
attempt in as many years — then took a further three seasons to close the
gap to the Premier League, finally sealing it with a 2-1 Championship
play-off final win over Sheffield United on 24 May 2025, decided by a
95th-minute winner from teenager Tommy Watson.

## Ownership & Finance

Ellis Short's ownership left Sunderland close to £200m in debt and losing
around £35m a year by the time he stepped back, having personally
capitalised more than £100m of that debt and taken on a further loan from
Security Benefit Corporation. As part of selling the club to Stewart
Donald's consortium in 2018, Short wrote off what he was owed and cleared
the remaining creditors, leaving Sunderland debt-free — a state the club
was still in when Kyril Louis-Dreyfus acquired control in February 2021.
Louis-Dreyfus's ownership has been built on patient rebuilding rather than
a single benefactor's transfer spending, taking the club from League One
to the Premier League within four years.

## Infrastructure & Environment

The Stadium of Light opened in 1997 on the site of the former Wearmouth
Colliery, replacing Roker Park, the club's home since 1898. At just under
49,000, it remains one of the largest grounds in English football, and
Sunderland's crowds have repeatedly outsized whatever division they were
actually playing in: the club set the all-time League One attendance
record with 46,039 for a Boxing Day fixture against Bradford City in 2018,
and averaged 32,157 across the whole of that 2018-19 League One season —
figures that would have ranked among the better-supported clubs in the
Premier League itself.
