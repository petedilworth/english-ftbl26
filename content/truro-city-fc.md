## Origins

Truro City were **a founding member of the Cornwall County FA on 18 September
1889**, and played their first recorded match at Truro College on 4 October,
beating Penzance 7–1. The first Cornwall Senior Cup came six years later.

**Truro is the only city in Cornwall**, and the club says outright that this is
why it is called City — a point it made again in June 2024 when it rebranded
around the cathedral and St Piran's flag.

That rebrand also killed a nickname. **The White Tigers were introduced in a
crest redesign around 2004 and abolished in June 2024**, the club stating that
the name had a "lack of historical connections with the club and the Duchy as a
whole". It readopted **the Tinners**, its original nickname, from Cornish tin
mining. A club that spent years exiled from its town came home and, in the same
month, discarded the invented identity it had acquired while away.

## Trajectory

**Truro City's entire presence in the top five tiers of English football is one
season.** In 2025/26 they finished **twenty-fourth of twenty-four in the
National League on 34 points**, and were relegated.

That makes this the shortest record of any club with a page here — though not
uniquely so. Twelve clubs in this data have exactly one completed season, and
one of the others, Brackley Town, has the same season. Truro's is the worst of
them.

**Was it the travel?** The temptation is enormous and the match data does not
support it on its own. Their splits were:

- **Home: 5 wins, 6 draws, 12 defeats — 21 points from 23 games.**
- **Away: 3 wins, 4 draws, 16 defeats — 13 points from 23.**

The away record is dreadful. But **21 points at home is also a relegation
record**, and would not have kept them up by itself. They finished eight points
from safety in a division that relegated four. Travel is a plausible
contributory cause and it is not a demonstrated one, and the off-pitch evidence
that would settle it — budget, squad, ground grading — is not on the public
record. This page leaves it open rather than asserting the obvious answer.

What *can* be measured is how far they had to go, and the answers are records.

## Ownership & Finance

The insolvency and the ground are one event.

In **August 2011 HMRC issued a winding-up petition** over more than £100,000 of
unpaid tax. In **June 2012** the club paid the remaining squad wages; on **17
August 2012 the chairman was declared personally bankrupt** by Truro County
Court and resigned a week later. In **September 2012 the club filed for
administration and was deducted ten points.**

**Creditors came to £3,901,352** — including £1.5m to an Irish firm, just over
£1m to a director, £645,000 to the company set up to run the club's commercial
affairs, and £355,000 to the former chairman's own property company. It was
rescued by local businessmen, with the debt agreement still being negotiated
into 2013.

**Treyew Road was sold in 2012**, and the stadium assets were tied to the
collapsed property developments. What the club got back was **Section 106 money
from the supermarket development on the site**, earmarked toward a new stadium —
which is why the ground took twelve years to arrive and why the exile happened
in between.

The current ownership could not be established to the standard this site applies
and is not stated here; nor could the new stadium's ownership, capacity or
financing. This site's finance table holds no rows for Truro.

## Infrastructure & Environment

**Truro City is the most isolated football club in this data, and the
superlatives need separating because they do not all say the same thing.**

**Their nearest club of any kind, in any tier, in any era, is Plymouth Argyle at
43.6 miles.** Measured across all 165 clubs in this site with coordinates, that
is **the greatest nearest-neighbour distance of any of them** — ahead of Norwich
at 39.0, Dover at 34.5, Swansea at 33.6 and Carlisle at 30.5. It is also further
than any actual member of the 2025/26 Football League was from its nearest
fellow member: Norwich, the most isolated of the ninety-two, had Ipswich 39.8
miles away. **From outside the League, Truro were further from the League than
anyone inside it was from each other.**

Their **nearest divisional opponent in 2025/26 was Yeovil Town, 117.3 miles
away.** That is the fifth loneliest club-season of the 7,280 in this data — and
it is not the record. Plymouth twice had a nearest divisional opponent 138.3
miles away, in 1986/87 and 2021/22, and Exeter twice had one 125.5 miles away in
the early sixties. Within the fifth tier alone, Truro's 117.3 is the highest
there has been.

Where Truro *are* first, of all 7,280 club-seasons, is the measure that
describes a whole season rather than one fixture. **Their mean distance to
divisional opponents in 2025/26 was 248.9 miles** — twenty-seven clear of
Plymouth's worst-ever season, at 221.9 in 1986/87. Total away travel came to
**11,448 straight-line round-trip miles against a divisional median of 5,655**,
more than twice the median and 31 per cent more than second-placed Carlisle. And
**Truro to Gateshead, 357.1 miles, is the longest same-division pairing anywhere
in this data** — longer than Carlisle to Plymouth, which is the example this
site has used for years.

The fixture list did some of the damage and not all of it. **Seven midweek or
bank-holiday away trips averaging 217 miles each way**, including two brutal
sequences: Scunthorpe on a Saturday and Brackley the following Tuesday, 1,016
round-trip miles in four days; and Southend on a Tuesday and Altrincham on the
Saturday, 1,030 miles in five. But the compiler was careful where it counted —
**Truro never had more than two consecutive away fixtures all season.** The
damage is in the midweek scheduling and the turnarounds, not in clustering.

Before any of that they had nowhere to play at all. **Truro left Treyew Road for
Plainmoor, Torquay, at the start of 2018/19** because a supermarket development
was due to begin — a 200-mile round trip for a home fixture — and **came back
in December 2018 because the development had not started.** The ground did go in
the end: a final match in October 2020, and then **four years without a home**,
at Bolitho Park in Plymouth from January 2021, at Taunton from February 2024,
and at Meadow Park in Gloucester in the spring of 2024. Gloucester is **166
miles from Truro in a straight line** and about 190 by road — and it is the same
ground **Newport County were exiled to in the 1990s**, which this site already
records. **Yeovil Town clinched the National League South title against Truro
there**, in a Truro home fixture, in a stadium belonging to a club from the
division below.

They came home for 2024/25, went up, and went straight back down.

On rivalries, this page claims none, and the reason is the same as everything
else on it. **Truro and Plymouth have never shared a division** — Plymouth's
lowest tier here is the fourth, Truro's only season was the fifth — and the
Plymouth page, which grounds the Devon derby explicitly in geography, does not
mention Cornwall at all. The most substantial relationship Truro have with
another club is **landlord and tenant with Torquay**, and the Cornish clubs that
might have supplied a derby are three tiers below the bottom of this data. A
club whose nearest neighbour is forty-three miles away and whose nearest
opponent was a hundred and seventeen does not get to have one.
