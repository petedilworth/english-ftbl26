## Origins

Blackpool F.C. was founded on 26 July 1887 by a group of members who split
from the town's existing St John's F.C. after a disagreement, reconvening
at the Stanley Arms Hotel to found a new club under the town's own name.
The club is known as "the Seasiders," reflecting the town's identity as a
holiday resort; "the Tangerines," from the orange home shirt, is also
widely used.

## Trajectory

For over three decades Blackpool was controlled by the Oyston family —
Owen Oyston, later joined by son Karl Oyston and daughter Natalie
Christopher — whose ownership became the subject of a long-running legal
dispute with minority shareholder Valeri Belokon. When the Oystons failed
to pay the resulting judgment debt, a court-appointed receiver was
installed on 13 February 2019 over Owen Oyston's and Segesta Ltd's shares
in the club and its stadium company, to enforce payment. The EFL board
considered whether this triggered its standard insolvency provisions and
explicitly ruled that it did not: a receivership over an owner's shares was
found not to be material to the club's ability to fulfil its league
obligations, and no automatic points deduction was applied — a notable
contrast with a genuine club administration. The receivers were discharged
in December 2019 following Simon Sadler's takeover and an Oyston–Belokon
settlement.

## Ownership & Finance

Blackpool-born businessman Simon Sadler completed his takeover of the club
on 13 June 2019, following High Court and EFL approval, ending the
Oyston family's roughly 32-year period of control. Under the Oystons, the
stadium had been held in a separate company — Blackpool Football Club
(Properties) Ltd, formerly Segesta Ltd — a structure fans and Belokon's
camp argued had facilitated the extraction of funds from the club. Since
the 2019 takeover, the stadium, hotel and training ground have been held
together with the club under Sadler's ownership.

## Infrastructure & Environment

Blackpool have played at Bloomfield Road since 1901, when the club's first
Football League match was staged there. Capacity has varied considerably
over the club's history — a peak of around 38,000 in the 1950s reduced
sharply by Taylor Report-driven all-seater conversion in the 1990s to a low
of roughly 9,000, before a temporary East Stand expansion around 2010
brought it back up to its current capacity of approximately 16,500.
