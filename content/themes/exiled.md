A club playing "home" games in another town is displaced, and the cost is
measurable. Attendance falls because the journey is too far for the ordinary
supporter; matchday income falls with it; the atmospheric advantage of a home
ground disappears entirely. Exile has followed a lost lease, a stadium
dispute, a ground sold before its replacement was ready, and safety
certificates that could not be renewed.

The interesting question is what an exile costs beyond the seasons it lasts.
A generation that stops attending during the exile does not automatically
return afterwards, and clubs that came back to their own ground have often
taken years to rebuild the crowds they had before they left.
