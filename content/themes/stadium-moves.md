The Taylor Report's all-seater requirement, published after Hillsborough,
reshaped English grounds through the 1990s, and many clubs concluded it was
cheaper to build new than to convert a Victorian ground hemmed in by terraced
streets. The moves that followed traded capacity, corporate facilities and
parking for a location usually further from the town centre the club was
named after.

Grouping them shows how differently it has gone. Some moves unlocked the
revenue that funded a rise through the divisions; others left clubs with debt
against a stadium too big for their crowds, and a ground their supporters
never warmed to. The difference usually lies in who paid for it and who ended
up owning it.
