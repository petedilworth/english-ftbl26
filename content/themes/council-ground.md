A significant number of English clubs do not own the ground they play in.
Many stadiums are owned by the local authority and leased back, an
arrangement that often dates to a post-war rebuild or to a council stepping
in when the club could not fund the ground itself.

The arrangement cuts both ways. A council landlord is usually a stable one,
and unlike a commercial owner it has reasons beyond rent to want the club to
survive. But the club cannot borrow against a ground it doesn't own, cannot
develop it freely, and depends on lease terms set by a body answerable to
voters rather than supporters. Ownership of the ground shapes what a club can
do at least as much as ownership of the club.
