Insolvency is the sharpest off-pitch force in English football, and the
league's response to it has hardened over time. Administration once carried
no sporting penalty at all; the Football League introduced a ten-point
deduction in 2004, the Premier League a nine-point deduction in 2009, and the
rules now reach further — a club that exits administration without a Company
Voluntary Arrangement can be penalised again the following season.

What makes these clubs worth grouping is that the damage is rarely confined to
the year it happens. A deduction turns a mid-table season into a relegation,
relegation cuts central distributions, and the smaller budget that follows
makes the next promotion harder. The table records a demotion; the cause was
usually a balance sheet.
