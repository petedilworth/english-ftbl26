Supporter ownership in England is mostly a response to something going wrong.
Trusts have taken control after insolvency, after a benefactor withdrew, or
after a sale that supporters organised to prevent — rarely as a first choice,
and rarely from a position of strength.

The model's constraint is also its argument. A trust-owned club cannot be
funded by an owner absorbing losses, so it has to live within its income,
which caps how fast it can rise and makes sustained success genuinely
difficult. What it buys in exchange is that the club cannot be sold, moved or
borrowed against without the members agreeing — the failure modes that put
most of the other clubs on this site's theme pages.
