Every division has a trapdoor and a ladder, and most clubs use each a
few times a century. A few use them every other year. This page is
about those: the clubs for whom promotion is the start of a relegation
and relegation the start of a promotion, and the elastic between the
divisions that keeps snapping them back.

The purest form is the bounce run – up, down, up, down, in consecutive
seasons, with never a year of staying put. Rotherham United hold the
record: six straight, from 2016/17 to 2021/22, relegated from the
Championship three times and promoted back three times without
pausing. Burnley are on five and still going, which would mean a
seventh season of it if they come straight back this year. Fulham and
the old Wimbledon FC managed five.

The elastic is strongest at the top. A club promoted to the top flight
is relegated straight back a third of the time; one promoted to the
Championship, one time in eight. And the top-flight elastic has
tightened: in the 1960s one relegated club in twenty came straight
back; in the 2020s so far it is nearly one in two. The other half of the
elastic has tightened more: of clubs promoted to the top flight this
decade, three in five were relegated straight back, against one in
seven in the 1960s. Parachute payments are the usual explanation, and
the decade chart below is at least consistent with it.

The old version of this page scored clubs on promotions plus
relegations per season, which put a club with one relegation in one
season at the top. That measure is still here, over a fifteen-season
minimum, beside the bounce run and a third one, restlessness, which
rewards distance as well as frequency. They disagree, and the
disagreement is the point: Rotherham bounce between two divisions;
Luton Town have been in all five of the Football League's and the
National League's tiers since 1992 and are back where they started.
