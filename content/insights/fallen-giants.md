Nine clubs have won the English title since 1958/59 and later fallen to
the third tier or below. Leicester City did it faster than anyone:
champions in 2015/16, a third-tier club eleven years later. The record
had been Derby County's thirteen. Ipswich Town took fifty-eight.

Falling is quick; climbing back is slow. Bristol City and Wolves went
from the top flight to the fourth tier in three seasons each in the
1980s. Of the 190 relegations from the top flight since 1958/59, a
quarter ended after a single season and two-thirds within ten. The rest
wait – and here the numbers turn hopeful in an odd way. A club still out
after ten seasons has about an even chance of ever returning, and a club
still out after twenty has the same. Cardiff came back after fifty-one
years, Huddersfield after forty-five, Blackpool after thirty-nine. Time
away does not close the door; it stops mattering.

The climb the other way has been done fastest by Swansea and Wimbledon,
fourth tier to top flight in four seasons each. From the fifth tier,
only Luton have made it: National League in 2013/14, Premier League in
2023/24.

Which of the fallen could do it again? Size is the best guide the site
has. Leicester draw on nearly 800,000 people – a catchment that predicts
a top-flight club – and play in the third tier, the biggest gap between
size and standing in the Football League. Below them the sleeping
giants are non-league: Torquay, Southend, Truro, and a run of seventh-
tier clubs in Gloucester, Taunton, Bath and Worcester whose towns would
carry a club nearly two divisions higher.
