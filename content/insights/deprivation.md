Big clubs live among deprivation. The clubs in the top two divisions draw
a median of nearly nine per cent of their people from England's most
deprived tenth of neighbourhoods; in the sixth and seventh tiers it is
under two. Across this season's clubs, the more deprived a catchment,
the higher the club tends to play.

It is not cause, and the page has to say so before anything else. The
same deprivation tracks size: England's big towns are its old industrial
towns, and big towns hold big clubs. Hold the number of people a club
draws fixed and the link between deprivation and tier falls to −0.03,
which is nothing. The mills and the docks built both the clubs and the
hardship; neither built the other.

What the page is for is the kinds of hardship, because "deprived" hides
seven different things and they fall on different parts of the country.
Hartlepool United draw from the worst health in England – the 83rd
percentile – but from cheap, reachable housing. West Ham draw from the
opposite: housing pressure in the 69th percentile and schools better
than the national average. Ramsgate's catchment is among the worst for
crime in the country. FC Halifax Town, Accrington and Bradford City sit
on the worst living environments: old terraces, no central heating,
traffic and dirty air. And the worst education is not in the cities at
all, but on the coast and in the Fens – King's Lynn, Whitby, Boston,
Scarborough.

Use the sliders. Solo one measure and watch the map redraw: crime lights
up the Kent coast and the inner cities, health lights up the north-east,
and housing lights up two places that have nothing else in common:
London, where a home costs too much, and the countryside, where the
nearest GP, school or shop is too far away.

Then look where deprivation and income disagree. West Ham, Millwall and
Dulwich Hamlet draw from places with decent incomes that are deprived
anyway – overcrowded, high in crime, with old people in poverty. York,
Carlisle and AFC Fylde draw from the reverse: tight money, easy lives.

The most deprived catchment of any club is Avro's, in Oldham: more than
half the people it draws live in England's most deprived tenth. The
least deprived belong to Oxford City, Brackley Town and St Albans City.
Every one of them plays outside the Football League.
