A football club is a poor investment with one redeeming feature: the
price is set by the division it plays in, and divisions change. Buy a
club one level below where it belongs, get it back there, and the
broadcast money alone rewrites what it is worth. Everything else on this
page is about how likely that is and what could go wrong on the way.

The levers come in six families. **Football**: how far below its
natural level the club sits, which way it has been moving, and how high
it has ever been. **Place**: how many people it would draw at its best,
how well off they are, and how many of them it keeps from the club down
the road. **Ground**: how big it is and who owns it. **Risk**: how much
it yo-yos and whether it has been in administration or docked points.
**Price**: how low in the pyramid it is today, which is the nearest
thing here to what it costs. **Money**: turnover, wages and profit from
the latest accounts, where they exist.

With every lever equal, the top of the list is the Premier League,
because most levers are measures of size and big clubs are big. Turn
up *room to climb* and *cheap to buy* and the list becomes a list of
fallen clubs in large towns. That second list is the one a buyer wants,
and the gap between the two is the point of the sliders.

Missing data is the catch. The accounts cover mostly the clubs in the
top four tiers, and ground ownership is on file for fewer than a
hundred. A lever with no data counts at the middle, which is generous
to a club nobody has written anything down about. The hatching shows
how much of each bar is that generosity.
