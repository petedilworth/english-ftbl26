The "magic number" for avoiding relegation is famously cited as 40 points in the Premier League, and roughly 50 points in the 46-game seasons of the EFL and National League. But what happens when you actually look at the historical data?

The truth is that safety is relative. It depends entirely on how poorly the bottom teams are performing. In seasons where the relegated clubs are historically weak and drop a lot of points, the threshold for survival plummets. Conversely, when the bottom of the table is competitive, the safety mark is pushed higher.

This page explores the extremes of the relegation battle: the clubs that went down despite achieving points totals that would have kept them safe in almost any other year, and the clubs that survived with record-low tallies because the teams below them were even worse.

By looking at the "highest points relegated" and "lowest points survived" for each division, we can see how wide the margin of safety truly is, and how much luck plays a part in which side of the line a club lands on.
