Relegation is rarely a single event. A squad assembled for one division
is expensive in the next one down, and the contracts holding it together
do not expire the day the income does — so the season after a relegation
is often played by a weakened, unsettled team on wages the club can no
longer comfortably carry. That is how a club goes down twice.

The pages that track this usually stop at the bottom. The more useful
question is what happened afterwards, and the answer is not uniform: of
the falls recorded here, a meaningful minority were completely reversed,
some clubs are still climbing years later, and a good number never got
back at all. The strips below show that whole arc — every season from
1993/94 to today, coloured by division — so the fall and whatever
followed it can be read together.
