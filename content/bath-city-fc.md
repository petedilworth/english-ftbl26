## Origins

Bath City was founded on 19 July 1889 as Bath AFC, formed when a group of
local men met at the Christopher Hotel to establish an association
football club. The fledgling side amalgamated with the town's rugby club
in 1891 amid financial strain, and organised football effectively lapsed
for close to a decade before the club was re-formed around 1900, later
settling on the name Bath City. The nickname "the Romans" evidently nods
to the city's Roman heritage, though no source documents exactly when or
why it was formally adopted.

## Trajectory

Off-pitch, the club's story has been one of recurring financial fragility
rather than formal insolvency. It came close to administration in
September 2009, avoided only by an emergency fundraising appeal that hit
its £50,000 target within about a week. A deeper crisis in 2014–15 —
debts approaching £1 million, including a large sum owed to the outgoing
chairman — triggered the "Big Bath City Bid," a crowdfunding campaign
publicly backed by Ken Loach and Eric Cantona, which culminated in the
Supporters' Society becoming majority owner in 2017. A suspended
four-point deduction and £4,000 fine followed in 2021, tied to fixture
obligations disrupted by the pandemic. The longest-running off-pitch saga
has been Twerton Park's redevelopment: a joint-venture plan with a private
developer, including a new grandstand, 3G pitch and housing, was refused
by the local council over the accommodation block's design and lack of
parking, and a smaller 2024 bid for public funding toward a 3G pitch was
also unsuccessful.

## Ownership & Finance

Bath City has been majority-owned by the Bath City Supporters' Society, a
community benefit society, since 2017, when the "Big Bath City Bid"
crowdfund raised around £300,000 in community shares to complete a fan
takeover. That arrangement may be transitional: in August 2026 the board
and the Society's committee agreed in principle to sell the club to an
investor group backed by roughly £6 million and led by Andrew Stalbow,
subject to a shareholder vote.

## Infrastructure & Environment

Twerton Park has been Bath City's home since 1932, when the club moved in
after earlier spells at North Parade Ground and other grounds around the
city. The club owns the ground outright, though its future has been
entangled for years in a redevelopment partnership with a private
developer that also owns the adjoining shops on Twerton High Street. Its
historic capacity of 8,840 has been reduced by modern safety certification
to around 4,070. The ground has also served as a home away from home for
others: Bristol Rovers played there as tenants for a decade after leaving
Eastville in 1986, and university side Team Bath from 1999 to 2009.
