## Origins

Stockport County was formed in 1883 as Heaton Norris Rovers and renamed
Stockport County in 1890. The nickname "the Hatters" refers to the town's
historic hat-making industry — a heritage Stockport shares, coincidentally,
with Luton Town, nicknamed for exactly the same reason 30 miles from any
hat trade the two towns had in common with each other.

## Trajectory

Stockport entered administration in April 2009 amid mounting debts,
surviving that season's ten-point penalty but not the two relegations
that followed: down from League One in 2010, then out of the Football
League entirely in 2011 after 106 consecutive seasons in it. A third
relegation in 2013 took the club out of the National League and into the
deep non-league pyramid, where it spent seven seasons — several of them
part-time — before local businessman Mark Stott's January 2020 takeover
began a rapid climb back: National League champions in 2022-23, League Two
champions in 2023-24, and into League One for the first time in fourteen
years. Stott set out a seven-year plan to reach the Championship; by 2025
the club was competing for promotion well ahead of that schedule, reaching
the 2025-26 League One play-off final before losing to Bolton Wanderers.

## Ownership & Finance

Mark Stott completed his takeover of Stockport County in January 2020,
shortly after the club had won promotion from the National League North,
and has funded the climb through four divisions since — explicitly
targeting a return to the Championship within seven years of taking
charge.

## Infrastructure & Environment

The club has played at Edgeley Park since 1902. A redevelopment intended
to raise capacity from around 10,850 towards 18,000 began in 2025, tied to
the club's push back up the divisions.
