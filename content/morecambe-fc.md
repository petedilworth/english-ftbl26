## Origins

Morecambe Football Club was formed on 7 May 1920 at a public meeting at the
West View Hotel, after several earlier attempts to establish a senior club
in the town had failed. It was admitted directly to the Lancashire
Combination, where it played for its first 48 years, winning the league
title five times. The nickname "the Shrimps" is a nod to the town's
shrimp-fishing industry and appears on the club crest.

## Trajectory

Morecambe reached League One for the first time in 2021, via the League
Two play-offs, but were relegated back after two seasons. The relegation
coincided with the start of a much longer financial crisis under owner
Bond Group Investments: through 2023 and 2024 the club repeatedly paid
players and staff late and fell behind on payments to HMRC, drawing a
suspended three-point penalty that was activated in April 2024 after the
club failed to keep the required funds in its account, and a further
suspended two-point penalty in December 2024 for failing to report HMRC
defaults on time. Relegated again in 2025, this time to the National
League, the club was suspended from the league altogether on 28 July
2025 — withdrawn from membership, placed under a transfer embargo and
excluded from the National League Cup — for failing to meet financial
compliance requirements, days after a takeover intended to resolve the
crisis was itself delayed by the outgoing owner. The suspension was
lifted three weeks later once the takeover completed. The club was
relegated out of the National League at the end of the following season,
2025-26 — a second relegation in as many years, this one taking Morecambe
out of the top five tiers of English football entirely for the first time
since securing promotion into them in 2007.

## Ownership & Finance

Bond Group Investments, fronted by Jason Whittingham and Colin Goldring,
acquired an 82% stake in the club in May 2018. The club was put up for
sale in September 2022, but the process dragged on for close to three
years while the financial crisis above unfolded. A takeover by the Panjab
Warriors consortium was approved by the EFL in June 2025, only for
Whittingham to delay completing it; HM Revenue and Customs filed a
winding-up petition against the club on the same day the rescue deal was
announced, and staff went unpaid through June and July 2025. Panjab
Warriors completed their takeover in August 2025, settling the outstanding
wages and lifting the National League's suspension of the club. A separate
winding-up petition, brought by a company set up by two former club
directors over a £56,000 land debt, followed later in 2025 and was
withdrawn after the two sides reached a settlement.

## Infrastructure & Environment

The club played at Christie Park (originally known as Roseberry Park) from
its 1920 formation until 2010, when it moved to a newly built stadium
funded at a cost of around £12 million. Opened as the Globe Arena, it was
renamed the Mazuma Stadium in 2020 under a sponsorship deal, and holds
6,476.
